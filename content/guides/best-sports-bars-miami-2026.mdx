Miami is a city of two sporting cultures that don't always overlap. There's the Latin American football world — passionate, loud, deeply knowledgeable, extraordinary during Copa America and World Cup cycles — and there's the expat scene clustered around Brickell, watching Premier League on Saturday mornings and trying to convince the bar to turn the sound up. Both are worth knowing about. The best evenings in Miami's sports bars happen when the two collide around a fixture that everybody cares about.

The timezone is the honest difficulty. Eastern Time puts Miami in the same position as New York — those 7:30am EPL kickoffs are nobody's idea of fun. But the compensations are real: Miami is one of the best cities in the world to watch Latin American football, and when Inter Miami are in form, local football fandom has a new and growing energy.

## Fado Irish Pub — Brickell

The anchor of Miami's EPL-watching scene. Fado opens early on weekend mornings for Premier League, has reliable screens throughout, and has built a consistent expat crowd over the years. It's Irish rather than British in character, but the two communities overlap comfortably on football mornings. Good Guinness, staff who understand the fixture list, and a Brickell location that makes it convenient for a lot of the international crowd who live in that area.

## American Social Bar & Kitchen — Brickell

A more polished, restaurant-forward venue than Fado, but American Social does sport seriously. The screen setup is excellent, the food is genuinely good (it's worth ordering), and it covers a broad range of sport without losing focus. For Champions League afternoons — a relatively civilised 3pm ET — this is a very pleasant option.

## Duffy's Sports Grill — Multiple Locations

Duffy's is a Miami institution and earns its reputation as the city's most reliably sports-focused chain. Every wall is screens, the sound is always on, the beer is cold. It's not atmospheric in a traditional pub sense, but when you want guaranteed coverage of multiple games simultaneously — NFL Sunday, or a day with EPL, Serie A, and LaLiga all running — Duffy's delivers.

## The Boteco — Wynwood

Not a sports bar in the traditional sense, but worth knowing about. Boteco is a Brazilian restaurant that becomes one of the best places in Miami to watch football when Brazil, or more broadly South American football, is on. The energy during Copa América and World Cup Brasil matches is unlike almost anything else the city offers. Check what's on before you go — this is appointment sport rather than casual viewing.

## Swine Southern Table — Various

Another venue that crosses the restaurant/sports bar line but does it well. Swine pulls a crowd that cares about sport and has the screens and audio to back it up. Worth considering for a venue that doesn't feel like it was built around sport but handles it better than most places that were.

## Cock & Bull — Coral Gables

Coral Gables' British pub option and a dependable one. It's a neighbourhood local more than a destination venue, but Coral Gables has a significant expat community and Cock & Bull serves them well. For EPL mornings when you're staying south of the city, this is the answer.

## Ball & Chain — Little Havana

Ball & Chain is primarily a music and cocktail venue — not a sports bar — but its Little Havana location and Cuban cultural identity make it a fascinating place to watch Latin American football when they're hosting viewing events. Worth checking their events calendar during Copa América cycles specifically.

## The Brickell Cluster

One thing Miami gets right that cities twice its size don't always manage: the expat sports bars in Brickell are genuinely clustered together. If one venue is full, or showing the wrong match, you can walk to another in five minutes. For major EPL mornings or Champions League afternoons, it's worth knowing that you have options within easy walking distance rather than committing to one venue hours in advance.

## The Latin American Football Difference

Nothing in this guide is as important as understanding what Miami becomes during Copa América, the World Cup, or even major CONMEBOL qualifiers. The Latin American football-watching culture here is genuine, knowledgeable, and passionate in a way that transforms the city. The crowds in Little Havana, Wynwood, and Hialeah are not passive — these are people for whom the Clásico del Río de la Plata or Colombia vs Brazil means something real.

If you're visiting Miami during a major South American tournament, going to a venue in these communities will be one of the better sporting experiences of your life. Seek it out.

## Practical Tips

**Brickell for EPL, Wynwood and Little Havana for Latin football.** The city is geographically spread — knowing which part serves which sporting culture will save you significant time and disappointment.

**The 7:30am kickoff is brutal.** Miami's nightlife runs late, and so does its hospitality industry. Don't assume a bar that's open at 10pm on Friday will be open at 7am on Saturday. Confirm everything in advance.

**Inter Miami CF has changed things.** Messi's tenure shifted the city's football culture noticeably — there's now a layer of local engagement with football that didn't exist five years ago. It means more screens showing football in more venues, even ones that previously wouldn't have bothered.

**Uber beats driving.** Miami traffic and parking are not your friends. For any early morning or big game night, get a car rather than drive. Brickell especially has negligible parking options that won't cost a fortune.
