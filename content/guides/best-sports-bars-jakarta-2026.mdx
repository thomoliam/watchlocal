Jakarta is a city that rewards people who understand how it works and punishes those who don't. The traffic is the first lesson: it's not bad in the way that London or Bangkok traffic is bad; it's bad in a category of its own. Getting from SCBD to Kemang on a Friday evening can take two hours. Getting from Kemang to Blok M is rarely less than 45 minutes. The rule for sport-watching in Jakarta is the same rule that applies to everything in this city: choose your neighbourhood before you need to be there, and don't plan on changing your mind.

The other thing to understand is the timezone. GMT+7 is a genuine gift for EPL fans — Saturday's standard 3pm UK kickoff lands at 10pm, which is a perfectly good evening out. The 7:30pm early kickoff means you can have dinner first. But Champions League midweek is brutal: 2am or 3am local, which only the most committed insomniacs are watching live.

## The Patio Sports Bar — Kemang

Kemang's answer to the expat sports bar question, and a reliable one. The Patio has the screens, the sound, and the crowd — British, Australian, and European expats who've made Kemang their neighbourhood and made The Patio their local. EPL Saturday nights draw a consistent crowd; for major fixtures, it's worth arriving early.

The area around The Patio has enough surrounding bars and restaurants that a sports night in Kemang can be made into a proper evening. It's the most established expat district in Jakarta, which means it also has the infrastructure — taxis know it, Gojek drivers know it, and you're unlikely to end up somewhere deeply confusing.

## O'Reilly's Irish Pub — SCBD

The upscale alternative, and the obvious choice if you're staying or working in the Sudirman Central Business District. O'Reilly's is a genuine Irish pub — decent Guinness, sport on the screens, staff who know the fixture list — embedded in one of Jakarta's most international business districts. The crowd skews toward finance and professional expats; the atmosphere is more office crowd than football obsessive, but it works well enough.

For Champions League — if you're genuinely committing to a 2am kickoff — O'Reilly's has the right combination of comfortable seating, reliable screens, and late-night credibility.

## Paulaner Brauhaus — Central Jakarta

The German brewhouse alternative. Paulaner is primarily a restaurant with excellent beer rather than a sports bar, but it has screens and a reliably international crowd. Worth knowing about for a Bundesliga fixture or a Champions League night when you want proper food alongside the match. The beer quality is notably better than most venues on this list.

## The Goat — Kemang

A younger, more craft-beer-oriented alternative to The Patio in Kemang. The Goat has developed a loyal following among the newer generation of Jakarta expats — the kind of crowd who have opinions about IBUs and also watch the Premier League. The screen setup is solid; the selection of draught and bottled beer is the best in Kemang. On quieter EPL nights when The Patio might feel loud and crowded, The Goat is a genuinely pleasant alternative.

## Blok M — The Budget Scene

Blok M deserves its own section because it's a different world from Kemang and SCBD. Historically the heart of Jakarta's Japanese expat community, the area around Blok M Plaza has a cluster of sports bars and entertainment venues that are less polished but often more interesting than the expat-bubble alternatives further south.

The sports bars here are less likely to be focused on EPL and more likely to show whatever's on — which can mean you stumble into a venue watching something you wouldn't have sought out but end up enjoying. The prices are lower, the crowd is more mixed, and the atmosphere tends toward the local rather than the international. Worth exploring if you've already done Kemang and want to see a different side of the city.

## Hotel Bars — The Emergency Option

Jakarta's five-star hotel cluster around SCBD and the Golden Triangle has a number of sports bars attached to international hotels — the kind that are reliably open, reliably air-conditioned, reliably showing major sport, and reliably expensive. If you're visiting Jakarta briefly and staying in a business hotel, these are worth knowing about. They're not the most atmospheric venues in the world, but they will absolutely be showing the match and they will absolutely have cold beer. Sometimes that's enough.

## The Timezone Reality

GMT+7 is genuinely excellent for EPL and genuinely challenging for everything else:

- **Early EPL Saturday (12:30pm UK):** 7:30pm local — dinner and a match.
- **Standard EPL Saturday (3pm UK):** 10pm local — prime evening sport.
- **Late EPL Saturday (5:30pm UK):** 12:30am Sunday — only for the committed.
- **Champions League (8pm CET / 7pm UK):** 2am local — highlights territory for most people.
- **Australian sport:** varies wildly, but AEDT puts Australian summer sport at roughly workable hours.

The Champions League situation is worth addressing honestly. Most Jakarta expats watch Champions League on delay, or get up for the highlights. The bars that do stay open for 2am kickoffs are showing it mainly for the die-hards, and the atmosphere reflects that. If there's a specific fixture you genuinely can't watch on delay, O'Reilly's and The Patio both manage it. For anything else, the timezone is working against you.

## Practical Tips

**Jakarta traffic is not an exaggeration.** A 10km journey can take 90 minutes. Use Gojek (motorbike) rather than GrabCar when you need to be somewhere on time. If you're going to Kemang for a 10pm kickoff, leave at 8:30pm from central Jakarta.

**Kemang is the neighbourhood.** If you have flexibility over where you stay, Kemang is the right answer for an expat sports bar experience. SCBD is convenient for business visitors; Blok M is interesting for the curious; but Kemang has the density and character.

**Air conditioning is non-negotiable.** Jakarta is hot and humid. Every decent venue has it; if one doesn't, leave.

**Indonesian football culture is real and growing.** Liga 1, the Indonesian top flight, has a passionate local following. Persija Jakarta vs Persib Bandung is a genuine derby with the kind of fervour that surprises visitors. If you're in Jakarta for an extended period, it's worth seeking out the local football culture beyond the expat bars.
