Prague has always punched above its weight as a sports-watching city. Its central European location, enormous expat population, and long-standing tradition of cheap, excellent beer have made it a natural gathering point for British, Irish, American, and Australian fans living in or passing through Central Europe. The sports bar scene has grown to match — and in some respects exceeds — cities twice its size.

The timezone is close to perfect for EPL: Prague sits at UTC+2 (CEST in summer, CET in winter), meaning a 3pm Saturday Premier League kick-off in England is 4pm in Prague. You watch the game, have a few more beers, and you're done by 7pm. For Champions League, the prime-time 9pm CET slot is exactly 9pm in Prague. There are worse cities in the world to be an English football fan.

## Kick-off Times in Prague

**EPL Saturday 3pm UK → 4pm Prague.** The ideal slot. Afternoon sport watching with Czech beer at Czech prices.

**EPL early kick-off 12:30 UK → 1:30pm Prague.** Fine. Easy.

**Champions League 9pm CET → 9pm Prague.** On the dot. Prague is in the same timezone as the primary broadcast timezone for UCL.

**Six Nations (UK afternoons) → Prague early evening.** Straightforward viewing. The expat crowd turns out well for this.

**NFL Super Bowl → approximately 00:30 CET.** Late, but some venues open for it.

## Old Town and the City Centre

Old Town (Staré Město) is where most visiting fans end up, and for good reason — the density of sports bars within a ten-minute walk of each other is exceptional. The narrow streets around Old Town Square, Wenceslas Square, and the surrounding area have enough options that you can scout a few before committing.

**Legends Sports Bar** near the Old Town is the anchor venue for Prague's international expat sports scene. It's been showing sport for years, draws a consistent crowd, and has the screen setup and broadcast rights to match. For Champions League and big EPL weekends, it's the obvious choice — arrive thirty minutes ahead of kick-off for anything significant. The crowd here is noticeably international: you'll hear English, Irish accents, occasional Australian inflections.

**O'Che's Irish Bar** is frequently cited as one of the best Irish pubs in Central Europe — and Irish pubs, done properly, are still some of the most reliable places on earth to watch sport. Six Nations weekends here have a genuine atmosphere. The mix of expat regulars, travelling fans, and Czech locals who've adopted the place gives it something more than a typical tourist bar. For Ireland games especially, it gets properly packed.

**Hemingway Bar** sits in a slightly different category — it's known primarily as a cocktail bar, and it shows sport on the side rather than anchoring its identity around it. But for quieter games or if you want to watch something specific in a more relaxed setting, it's worth knowing about.

The practical advantage of Old Town: you can walk between options. If one bar is heaving, you're two minutes from an alternative. Prague's Old Town is compact and very walkable — most of the key sports venues sit within a ten-minute walking radius of each other.

## Žižkov — The Local Alternative

Žižkov is Prague's answer to the question of what the city looks like when tourists aren't present. The neighbourhood east of the centre has a rougher, more authentic feel — less Baroque architecture, more working-class bar culture, and considerably cheaper prices.

**Jósef Bar** in Žižkov has built a following among expats who've been in Prague long enough to move beyond Old Town. The sport coverage is reliable — EPL, Champions League, Czech football — and the prices reflect the neighbourhood rather than the tourist footfall. If you're in Prague for longer than a weekend and you want somewhere that feels less like it was designed for stag parties, Žižkov is worth exploring.

Žižkov is also where Czech football culture has a stronger pulse. Bars here will show AC Sparta Prague and SK Slavia Prague matches with genuine investment — it's not background noise.

## Czech Football — The Derby

The Sparta Prague vs Slavia Prague derby is the fixture that defines Czech football. Both clubs are based in Prague, both have historic trophy cabinets, and the rivalry is fierce — tactically, culturally, and occasionally physically. When the derby is on, bars across the city fill with partisan crowds.

**Sparta** (red and white, based at Letná) traditionally represents a more populist identity. **Slavia** (red and white — yes, both teams wear broadly similar colours, which adds to the confusion for newcomers) has a reputation as the more technically focused club. Their European campaigns have improved considerably in recent years, which means the occasional Champions League qualifier adds international stakes to the local rivalry.

If you're in Prague when the derby is scheduled, find a Czech bar for it rather than a tourist sports bar. The authentic experience is worth seeking out.

## Beer and Value

Czech beer culture is one of the genuine pleasures of sport watching in Prague. Pilsner Urquell — brewed in Plzeň, 90km from Prague — is on tap everywhere, and a half-litre costs a fraction of what it does in London, Paris, or Amsterdam. Kozel dark (tmavé) is another option worth knowing: a dark lager that's richer than the standard pale, and goes well with a cold evening game.

The combination of cheap, excellent beer and comfortable EPL timing makes Prague exceptionally good value for a sporting weekend. A Saturday afternoon in an Old Town sports bar — arrive at 1pm, watch two EPL games, eat — costs less than a single match pint at many London venues.

## Six Nations in Prague

Six Nations is the surprise hit of Prague's expat calendar. The British and Irish populations in the city are substantial — English teachers, finance workers, long-term residents — and they converge on O'Che's and Legends for the February–March window. For England-Ireland or any of the title-decider weekends, these venues fill up properly.

The timing works well: matches kick off in the afternoon Prague time, there's no early-morning alarm involved, and the beer and atmosphere are reliably good. If you're in Prague during Six Nations and you have even a passing interest in rugby, it's worth finding your way to one of the main expat bars.

## Practical Notes

- **Walk between venues.** Prague Old Town is genuinely compact — you can scout three or four sports bars in fifteen minutes on foot. If a venue is full, the next option is close.
- **Cash is still common.** Many Prague bars, including sports bars, prefer or require cash. Have crowns on you.
- **Book for major events.** Champions League Final, Six Nations title weekends, and big Premier League days draw large crowds in the expat-focused bars. Reserve ahead or arrive early.
- **Czech is the language.** Most staff in tourist-facing sports bars speak good English, but a brief attempt at Czech (dobry den, prosím, díky) goes a long way.
- **The Czech football season** runs August–May. Sparta and Slavia both have central training grounds and stadium areas — bars near Letná (Sparta's ground) are worth knowing for Prague Derby weekends.
