Taipei does not always appear on lists of great sports bar cities. It probably should. The city sits at GMT+8 — the same timezone as Singapore and Hong Kong — which means Premier League Saturday fixtures land at 8:30pm and 11pm. You are not dragging yourself out of bed at 4am. You are going to the pub after dinner.

Add a genuine local passion for basketball and baseball, a safe and extremely navigable city, and an established expat community that has built a decent bar scene, and you have a much better sporting proposition than Taipei is usually given credit for.

## The Taipei Sporting Context

### The Timezone Advantage

UTC+8 is one of the best timezones on earth for watching European football. The 12:30pm BST Premier League kickoff is 8:30pm Saturday in Taipei. The 3pm BST slot is 11pm — late, but within normal nightlife hours. Champions League midweek games at 8pm BST land at 4am, which is genuinely brutal — but the weekend schedule is exceptional.

For reference, Bangkok and Ho Chi Minh City (UTC+7) have it one hour earlier and lose the late game to midnight rather than 1am. Taipei's slightly later offset is not a handicap for the main Saturday windows.

### Local Sport: Baseball First, Basketball Close Behind

Taiwan has its own professional baseball league — the CPBL (Chinese Professional Baseball League) — which runs from March to October and draws genuine local passion. The national team is competitive at the Asian level, and games attract real crowds. If you are in Taipei during the season, catching a local game at Tianmu Stadium is worth doing alongside your bar sessions.

Basketball runs a close second. The NBA has had a significant following in Taiwan for over two decades — Taiwanese fans were tuning in long before the recent global expansion of the league. Most sports bars treat NBA as a priority broadcast, and the audience is genuinely knowledgeable.

### Where the Expats Are

The two main districts for expat nightlife are **Zhongshan** (central-north, around the MRT Zhongshan and Shuanglian stations) and **Da'an** (central, around Daan Park and Xinyi Road). Both have clusters of international bars and restaurants. Zhongshan is slightly more bar-heavy; Da'an is more residential and popular with long-term expats. The Xinyi entertainment district has newer venues and a younger crowd.

Getting around is easy — Taipei's MRT is clean, frequent, cheap, and comprehensive. A Youbike (the city's excellent bike-share scheme) handles anything the metro does not.

## The Best Sports Bars

### Carnegie's — The Expat Institution

Carnegie's on Zhongshan North Road is the longest-running expat bar in Taipei and the de facto home of Premier League viewing in the city. It has been operating for over 25 years and has built a loyal crowd of regulars who return specifically for the sport.

The venue is spread across two floors with a good-sized screen setup and a terrace. On Saturday evenings — which are, conveniently, peak EPL time — it fills up quickly. Book a table for big fixtures.

**Best for:** Premier League, Champions League, Six Nations, any event that draws the British expat community

**Rating:** 4.5 / 5

### On Tap — American-Style with Multiple Locations

On Tap operates several venues across Taipei and is the go-to for American sports in the city. The formula is familiar — wide range of craft beers, pub food, large screens, and a crowd that mixes expats, Taiwanese sports fans, and travellers.

NFL, NBA, and MLB are reliably on. For EPL, On Tap is solid rather than specialist — they will show it, but it is not as central to their identity as at Carnegie's.

**Best for:** NFL, NBA, MLB, EPL, mixed crowds

**Rating:** 4.3 / 5

### The Brass Monkey — The Neighbourhood Option

The Brass Monkey in Da'an is the kind of bar that becomes your local. It is not the biggest venue in Taipei — the screen setup is good rather than spectacular — but the atmosphere is warm and the crowd is genuinely mixed between expats and Taiwanese regulars who are there for the sport.

It is particularly good for quieter sporting evenings where you want to watch without the noise of a larger venue. Live music runs on non-match nights, which gives it a dual personality that works well for the neighbourhood.

**Best for:** EPL, NBA, quieter viewing sessions

**Rating:** 4.2 / 5

### Main Pub — The Late-Night Option

Main Pub is a Zhongshan stalwart that keeps later hours than most of the competition. That makes it the bar of choice for anyone chasing the 11pm EPL kickoff or the occasional mid-week fixture that runs past midnight. The crowd skews younger and more energetic than Carnegie's, which suits some matches better than others.

**Best for:** Late EPL kicks, Champions League on late nights, younger crowd

**Rating:** 4.0 / 5

### Revolver — Live Music Bar That Shows Sport

Revolver is primarily a live music venue in Zhongshan, but it doubles as a sports bar on match nights. The screen setup is modest by dedicated sports bar standards, but the atmosphere — particularly for big events — is excellent. Worth knowing about if the regular sports bars are full.

**Best for:** Overflow option for big events, combined music and sport evenings

**Rating:** 3.9 / 5

## Practical Tips

### Getting There and Around

Taipei's MRT is genuinely one of the world's best urban rail systems. It runs cleanly, on time, and until midnight (with reduced services after that). Most sports bars are a short walk from MRT stations. A 7-day tourist pass is good value if you are moving around the city regularly.

### Safety and Logistics

Taipei is one of the safest cities in Asia — genuinely so, not just compared to other major urban centres. Street crime is rare, the city is well lit, and public transport runs late enough to get you home after most sporting events. A late EPL finish at 1am is entirely manageable by MRT if you are quick, or by Uber/taxi otherwise.

The food scene around every bar district is excellent and available late. Post-match beef noodle soup or scallion pancakes from a nearby stall has become a Taipei sports bar ritual for many expats.

### Language

English is widely spoken in expat bars and by younger Taiwanese locals. Bar menus are almost always bilingual. You will not struggle.
