Paris isn't the first city that comes to mind when you think of sports bars, and that's exactly why the ones that exist here are so good. The expat community — heavily British, Irish, Australian, and American — has carved out a genuinely excellent circuit of venues that know what they're doing. And unlike watching EPL in New York, where you're dragging yourself out of bed at 7:30am, weekend Premier League in Paris kicks off at 1:30pm and 4pm local time. Civilised.

The main clusters are in Le Marais, Bastille, and around Opéra/Grands Boulevards. Here's where to go.

## The Bombardier (Latin Quarter)

If you only go to one sports bar in Paris, make it The Bombardier near the Panthéon. It's the best rugby bar in the city — full stop. On Six Nations weekends it becomes a small slice of England or Ireland transported to the 5th arrondissement, and the atmosphere for France vs. England fixtures is something you'll remember. It's a proper British pub with a genuinely impressive screen setup.

- The undisputed home of Six Nations rugby in Paris — book well in advance
- Also excellent for EPL and Champions League — they show everything
- Pints of proper British ales, not just Kronenbourg
- RER B to Luxembourg or Metro Line 10 to Cluny–La Sorbonne
- **Price range:** €€ | **Rating:** 4.8/5

## O'Sullivan's by the Mill (Grands Boulevards)

O'Sullivan's is the biggest Irish bar chain in Paris and the Grands Boulevards location is the flagship. It's large, loud, and reliably packed for anything major. This is the place to go when you want a crowd — it fills up quickly for Champions League nights and stays packed through Ligue 1 fixtures when PSG are in Europe.

- Multiple floors and dozens of screens — you will always have a view
- Opens for NFL afternoon games (around 6pm local) and stays for night games
- Consistently the best atmosphere for Champions League knockout nights
- Metro Line 8/9 to Grands Boulevards
- **Price range:** €€ | **Rating:** 4.4/5

## Frog & Rosbif (Les Halles)

The Frog & Rosbif is a Paris institution — a British-style pub with its own in-house microbrewery on Rue Saint-Denis, right in the centre of the city. It's popular with Anglophone expats and does a good job of balancing EPL screenings with the broader French sports calendar. The beer is genuinely excellent.

- One of the few Paris bars where you'll get real craft ales alongside the sport
- Reliable for EPL on Saturday and Sunday afternoons — very relaxed atmosphere
- Also shows NFL and Six Nations without much fuss
- Metro Line 4 to Les Halles/Étienne Marcel
- **Price range:** €€ | **Rating:** 4.4/5

## The Highlander (Saint-Michel)

The Highlander is a Scottish pub on the Left Bank that punches well above its size for sport. It's small, which means it gets very loud very quickly, and that actually works in its favour for big match nights. The crowd is a genuine mix of expats and locals who are into sport, not just tourists who wandered in.

- Best for Six Nations — Scotland fixtures especially, as you'd expect
- Good for EPL and Champions League midweek games
- Gets claustrophobically packed for semi-finals and finals — arrive 45 minutes early
- Metro Line 4 to Saint-Michel or RER B/C to Saint-Michel–Notre-Dame
- **Price range:** €€ | **Rating:** 4.3/5

## The Long Hop (Bastille)

The Long Hop in Bastille is one of the better-kept secrets on the Paris sports bar circuit. It's a cricket bar first — run by and for the British and Australian communities — but it shows a full range of sport and the cricketing focus means they're set up for awkward timezone viewing in a way most Paris bars aren't.

- Essential for cricket: The Ashes, IPL, and international test matches
- Also shows AFL and NRL for the Australian contingent
- Quieter and more focused than the Irish chains — great for actually watching the match
- Metro Line 1/5/8 to Bastille
- **Price range:** €€ | **Rating:** 4.5/5

## Carr's (Opéra)

Carr's is an Irish pub near the Opéra that has quietly become one of the most reliable sports bars in the city. It's not flashy but the screens are good, the sound is up, and the staff don't mess around when it comes to getting matches on. Strong choice for Six Nations weekends when The Bombardier is fully booked.

- Solid fallback for any major rugby fixture when more famous spots are sold out
- Dependable for EPL and Champions League — shows every game
- Good food menu for a sports bar
- Metro Line 3/7/8 to Opéra
- **Price range:** €€ | **Rating:** 4.2/5

## The Real McCoy (Le Marais)

A newer entry to the Marais sports bar scene, The Real McCoy has quickly become popular with the American expat crowd and anyone who wants to watch NFL or NBA without travelling across the city. They've invested properly in screens and sound, and the programming is genuinely sport-first rather than background TV.

- The best place in Paris to watch NFL — they show Sunday night and Monday night games
- Strong NBA coverage, popular with the American and Canadian expat communities
- Lively crowd for big NBA playoff games
- Metro Line 1 to Saint-Paul or Hôtel de Ville
- **Price range:** €€ | **Rating:** 4.3/5

## Practical Tips for Watching Sport in Paris

**The timezone is your friend for European sport.** Premier League kicks off at 1:30pm and 4pm on weekends — you can have a proper lunch and still make kickoff. Champions League midweek games start at 9pm, which fits perfectly into a Paris evening. No early mornings required.

**Book ahead for Six Nations.** The Bombardier takes reservations and they go fast for England, Ireland, or Scotland fixtures. If you're hoping to walk into any decent bar on match day without a reservation, you'll be disappointed.

**PSG in the Champions League changes everything.** When PSG are in the knockout rounds, even non-rugby bars fill up with locals. The atmosphere in any bar showing the match is something else entirely.

**Speak a little French.** Making even a basic effort at the non-Irish-chain bars will be well received and occasionally gets you better service.
