Copenhagen operates on Central European Time, which means Premier League football lands at 1:30pm and 4pm on Saturdays. If you've spent any time watching EPL in America or Asia, you'll immediately understand why this matters: these are proper afternoon kick-offs, the kind you can build a Saturday around with no alarm clock involved. For British expats living in Copenhagen, the timezone is one of the genuine compensations of being abroad.

The city itself is compact, walkable, well-organised, and has a strong English-language expat community built largely around the international business and diplomatic presence. The result is a small but high-quality circuit of British and Irish pubs that take football seriously.

## The Dubliner — Nørreport

The Dubliner is the first answer to almost every "where do I watch football in Copenhagen" question, and it's earned that position. Located near Nørreport station — one of the most central spots in the city — it's an Irish pub with a loyal British expat following and consistent coverage of Premier League and Champions League.

The screens are well-positioned, the crowd knows the football, and the central location means you can combine it with the rest of your Copenhagen day without too much planning. On a Saturday afternoon for a big EPL fixture, expect a proper crowd. On a quiet midweek Champions League evening, it's the reliable fallback.

## The Old English Pub

The Old English Pub delivers exactly what its name promises: a British pub template in central Copenhagen, with the EPL fixtures that go with it. It's been a fixture on the expat circuit for years and has a consistent following among British and Irish residents. Not reinventing anything, but doing the fundamentals reliably — good screens, familiar format, dependable crowd on match days.

Worth knowing as a central backup if The Dubliner is at capacity for a major fixture.

## Braveheart — Scottish Pub

Braveheart is Copenhagen's Scottish pub, and while the branding is proudly north-of-the-border, the football coverage is comprehensive for all British football. It's a smaller, more characterful venue than some of the larger Irish pubs — the kind of place where you end up in a conversation about Scottish Premiership results even if you weren't expecting to. Six Nations rugby coverage is strong here.

## The Scottish Pub Copenhagen

As the name suggests, there are enough Scottish and British expats in Copenhagen to sustain more than one Scottish-themed venue. The Scottish Pub is the other contender in that space — worth knowing if Braveheart is full, and slightly different in atmosphere and regular crowd, which gives you options without having to commit to one loyalty.

## The Australian Bar

Copenhagen's Australian Bar reflects the broader Commonwealth expat community that the city has attracted. It covers a wider range of international sport than the British pubs — cricket, rugby union, AFL — and its timezone advantage is obvious: Australian sport that's normally a timezone nightmare becomes slightly less nightmarish when you're already eight or nine hours ahead of Sydney. For British visitors, it's worth knowing for the Six Nations and for the different sports culture it represents.

## Ørsted Ølbar

Not a sports bar in the traditional sense, but Ørsted Ølbar is worth knowing for its beer selection and the fact that it occupies a useful neighbourhood position for those exploring beyond the immediate Nørreport circuit. Copenhagen's craft beer scene is genuinely good, and Ørsted Ølbar is part of that. It shows sport and it serves interesting beer, which is not a combination you can take for granted.

## Practical Tips

**The CET timezone gift.** 1:30pm and 4pm on Saturdays for EPL is as good as it gets outside the UK and Ireland. The early kick-off lands after a reasonable morning; the late kick-off finishes at a perfectly civilised hour. Weeknight Champions League at 9pm CET is also normal evening timing. Copenhagen is genuinely one of the best cities in the world to be an expat football supporter.

**Booking matters.** The expat pub circuit in Copenhagen is small relative to the demand on big match days. For a major EPL fixture — Manchester City vs Liverpool, Arsenal vs Tottenham — arriving at The Dubliner early or making a reservation is sensible.

**The Danish football context.** Danes follow football seriously. The Superliga doesn't have the global reach of the Premier League, but FC Copenhagen in the Champions League have shown there's a base of real knowledge and passion. If you're in Copenhagen during a Danish club's European campaign, the atmosphere in any sports bar becomes something different.

**Six Nations is underrated here.** Denmark has a small but committed rugby union following, and the Six Nations draws a serious crowd at the British-facing pubs. If you follow rugby, Braveheart and The Old English Pub are both worth targeting.

**Vesterbro and Nørreport.** The expat bar scene clusters around Nørreport and extends into Vesterbro and the city centre. All are walkable from each other, which matters when venues fill up and you need a backup.
