# The Best Sports Bars in the World (2026 Edition)

Not all sports bars are created equal. You've been in the bad ones — four screens showing the same domestic league, sound turned down, a bartender who has to "check if we have Sky" when you ask about the Premier League. Then you've been in a great one, and you know the difference immediately: the noise, the crowd, the moment a goal goes in and the whole room moves together.

This is WatchLocal's global shortlist for 2026. We ranked these venues on five factors: **screen coverage** (size, number, sightlines), **sound** (audio, not just picture), **programming breadth** (how many leagues and sports they carry), **atmosphere** (are there real fans in the seats?), and the factor that trumps everything else — **whether they reliably show your sport**.

---

## The Shortlist: 11 Venues Worth Travelling For

### 1. The Sportsman — Bangkok, Thailand

Sukhumvit Soi 11 is ground zero for sports bars in Southeast Asia, and The Sportsman is the standard-setter. Multiple floors, dozens of screens at every angle, and a crowd that shows up at 6am on Saturday for early Premier League kick-offs without complaint. The programming list is extraordinary — Premier League, La Liga, NFL, NFL Sunday Ticket, NBA, UFC, and more, including niche broadcasts most venues wouldn't bother with. The beer is cold, the prices are among the best of any world-class sports bar, and the expat regulars treat it like their living room.

### 2. Football Factory at Legends — New York City, USA

On 33rd Street near Penn Station, Football Factory is the best purpose-built Premier League venue in North America. American fans here are not casual — they've set their alarms for 7:30am, they're wearing shirts, and they care. The venue has two floors of screens, dedicated supporters areas for major clubs, and the kind of electric atmosphere on a big match morning that rivals many London pubs. For NFL and college football, it transforms into something equally intense.

<WatchPageLink league="Premier League" city="New York City" href="/watch/premier-league/new-york-city" />

### 3. The Woolshed — Dublin, Ireland

Dublin knows sport. The Woolshed on Parnell Street is a barn-sized venue with a capacity that fills fast for anything meaningful — Six Nations, Champions League, Premier League, GAA. The screens are enormous, the audio is immersive, and Irish crowds bring a warmth and intensity to watching that makes even a midweek Europa League game feel like an occasion. The late bar licence means you're not watching a second-half at closing time.

<WatchPageLink league="Premier League" city="Dublin" href="/watch/premier-league/dublin" />

### 4. Hobgoblin Roppongi — Tokyo, Japan

Tokyo's Roppongi district is the international nightlife hub, and Hobgoblin is where expats congregate for sport. It's a proper English pub with the full Sky Sports and BT Sport stack, Guinness on tap, and a crowd that takes football seriously. The time difference works surprisingly well for Premier League — Saturday 3pm UK is 11pm in Tokyo, which means an excellent pub atmosphere for games rather than an early-morning bleary-eyed start. For rugby, it's the best venue in the city.

<WatchPageLink league="Premier League" city="Tokyo" href="/watch/premier-league/tokyo" />

### 5. Muddy Murphy's Irish Pub — Singapore

Clarke Quay and Orchard Road have no shortage of sports bars, but Muddy Murphy's has the atmosphere and the programming to back it up. Football, rugby, GAA — they cover it. The Irish expat crowd ensures genuine noise on big match nights, and the venue has the screens and sound system to justify the reputation. Singapore's timezone is neutral to kind for most European sport: Premier League at 3pm UK is 11pm local, which is a very acceptable Saturday night.

<WatchPageLink league="Premier League" city="Singapore" href="/watch/premier-league/singapore" />

### 6. Boxpark Shoreditch — London, England

London has more sports bars per square mile than almost anywhere on earth, but Boxpark Shoreditch earns its place on a global list because of its scale and energy on big match days. The outdoor screens, the covered areas, the food vendors, the crowd mixing football tourists with genuine East London supporters — it's a genuine spectacle for a Champions League final or an international tournament. London also has the depth of offering that nowhere else quite matches: you can find a dedicated supporters pub for almost any club in almost any league within the M25.

<WatchPageLink league="Premier League" city="London" href="/watch/premier-league/london" />

### 7. The Wanch — Hong Kong

Wan Chai's The Wanch is a Hong Kong institution — a live music venue that doubles as one of the best sports bars in the city, with a loyal crowd and a genuine neighbourhood feel rather than a tourist-trap finish. For football specifically, it punches well above its size, and the late-night Hong Kong lifestyle means post-midnight Premier League viewing (the time difference puts most matches deep into the night) is treated as standard. The regulars are passionate and the bar staff actually know what they're doing.

<WatchPageLink league="Premier League" city="Hong Kong" href="/watch/premier-league/hong-kong" />

### 8. Old Man's — Canggu, Bali

A beach club and bar hybrid that's evolved into the de facto sports bar for Canggu's enormous expat and digital nomad community. The screens are better than you'd expect for a venue that opens onto a garden facing the ocean, the programming covers all the major leagues, and the crowd skews towards younger travellers who care about sport. For Sunday NFL or Saturday Premier League, it's the obvious answer in an area not particularly well-served by dedicated sports venues. Order food, grab a Bintang, and settle in.

<WatchPageLink league="Premier League" city="Bali" href="/watch/premier-league/bali" />

### 9. Ferryman's Tavern — Cape Town, South Africa

At the V&A Waterfront with Table Mountain as a backdrop, Ferryman's Tavern is the most visually spectacular setting on this list. But it earns its spot on merit: the programming covers Super Rugby, Premier League, and international cricket at a high level, the crowd is genuinely passionate (Cape Town takes sport seriously), and the venue has the capacity and screens to handle a full house on a big night. For rugby specifically, there are few better atmospheres in the southern hemisphere.

<WatchPageLink league="Premier League" city="Cape Town" href="/watch/premier-league/cape-town" />

### 10. The Sporting Globe — Melbourne, Australia

Australia's sports bar scene has matured, and The Sporting Globe chain — with its Melbourne venues in particular — sets the benchmark. Large screens, a comprehensive AFL and NRL schedule alongside international sport, and a crowd that understands the assignment. For Premier League fans dealing with the time difference (kick-offs often land at 1am–4am local), The Sporting Globe is one of the venues that actually opens and broadcasts.

<WatchPageLink league="AFL" city="Melbourne" href="/watch/afl/melbourne" />

### 11. Brotzeit German Bier Bar — Singapore

A second Singapore entry because it earns it. Brotzeit at VivoCity is the definitive Bundesliga and Champions League venue in the city, with a German expat crowd that creates real atmosphere for German football and a menu and beer list that backs up the theme. For European football nights, it's a different energy to the Irish pub experience — and for the right fixture, the better one.

## The Honest Verdict

The best sports bar for you is the one that shows your sport. A venue with 30 screens is useless if none of them has the channel you need. Before you commit to a seat, verify the broadcast — use WatchLocal to search by league and city, and call ahead for major fixtures.

The venues above are exceptional by any measure. But the world is full of brilliant local spots that didn't make a global list: the bar in Lisbon where they show Champions League on a rooftop, the Irish pub in Medellín that opens at 4am for El Clasico, the beach bar in Koh Samui with a single screen and a crowd that somehow cares more than anyone at home.

Find your venue. Then find your people.
