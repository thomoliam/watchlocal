You're in an unfamiliar city. Your team is playing in four hours. You need to find a sports bar.

This scenario is familiar to anyone who travels frequently or lives abroad. The process of finding a reliable venue — one that actually shows your sport, has decent screens, and won't disappoint — can feel like guesswork. This guide gives you a repeatable process for finding the right place, every time.

## Start with the Right Search

Most travellers default to Google Maps or TripAdvisor when searching for sports bars. Both have significant limitations:

- **Google Maps** shows every bar in the area without filtering for sports coverage. "Sports bar" is often a self-applied label with no verification.
- **TripAdvisor** reviews focus on food and service rather than screen quality and sports coverage. A bar rated 4.5 stars for its cocktails might mute the game.

**WatchLocal** is built specifically for this problem — every listing is categorised by which sports and leagues the venue shows, with community-verified coverage details. Start here, then cross-reference.

## Know Your League's Broadcasting Situation

Before you start searching, understand how your league is broadcast in the country you're visiting. Broadcasting rights are territorial, which means:

- A bar in Thailand needs a Thai broadcaster with the right rights to show the Premier League
- A bar in Australia showing NFL needs a specific Australian sports channel or streaming subscription
- A bar in Germany showing the NBA needs a German or European package

If a sports bar in a particular country doesn't have access to the right broadcaster, they simply can't show your match — regardless of how many screens they have. WatchLocal's verified listings flag which leagues each venue is confirmed to show.

## Ask the Right Questions

Once you have a shortlist of venues, a quick call or message can save you a wasted trip:

- **"Do you show [league/competition] games?"** — Be specific. "Football" means different things in different countries.
- **"Will you be showing [specific fixture] on [date]?"** — Useful for high-demand games where venues might prioritise other content.
- **"How many screens do you have?"** — A bar with two screens showing two different games can't accommodate your match if both are already allocated.
- **"Do you have sound on for sport?"** — Some bars run sport muted. For a match that matters, you want commentary.
- **"Can I reserve a table?"** — Essential for big fixtures. Many sports bars hold tables on a first-come basis, but some take reservations for groups of 4+.

## Timing Matters More Than You Think

Finding a venue is only half the challenge — understanding local kickoff times is the other half. A 12:30pm Premier League kickoff in the UK is a midnight watch in Bangkok and a 6am start in Sydney. Before you search, convert your match time to local time.

Key time zone references for popular leagues:

**Premier League kickoffs (UK time) in major travel cities:**
- Bangkok: +7 hours (ICT)
- Singapore: +8 hours (SGT)
- Dubai: +4 hours (GST)
- Sydney: +10–11 hours (AEST/AEDT)
- New York: -5 hours (EST)

**Champions League kickoffs (CET) in major travel cities:**
- London: -1 hour
- Bangkok: +6 hours
- Singapore: +7 hours
- Sydney: +9–10 hours

## Make Friends with the Staff

In a foreign city, your best source of information is the venue itself. If you find a sports bar showing your match, ask the staff:

- Where else in the city shows this league reliably
- Whether there's a regular supporter group that meets locally
- What nights they reliably get a good crowd for your sport

Sports bar staff in expat-heavy cities are often expats themselves with a genuine knowledge of the local sporting scene. Tap into that knowledge.

## Join Local Expat Communities

In virtually every major city, there are Facebook groups, Reddit communities, and WhatsApp groups for expats from specific countries. These communities maintain running lists of venues that show specific sports — often more accurate and more current than any travel website.

Search for:
- "[City] + [your nationality] expats" on Facebook
- "[Sport] + [City]" on Reddit (r/soccer, r/nfl, r/AFL communities are particularly active)
- "[Club name] supporters [city]" — most major clubs have official international supporters' chapters with designated venues

## What to Do When There's No Good Option

Sometimes you're in a city with no decent sports bar option — either because the sport isn't popular there, or because the broadcasting rights don't exist locally.

Your options:

1. **Find an expat bar.** Even if a bar doesn't have your league's specific rights, British/Irish/Australian pubs often use streaming workarounds that a local sports bar wouldn't have access to.

2. **Hotel bars.** Larger international hotels often have sports bars or dedicated screen rooms that can be arranged with advance notice — sometimes for a fee.

3. **Ask at the venue.** Some bar owners will go out of their way to find a stream if you ask nicely and in advance. Not a guaranteed solution, but worth trying.

4. **Accept it.** Sometimes the match can't be watched live. Follow on your phone, catch the highlights later, and use the experience as motivation to find a better city next time.

## Build a Personal Database

As a frequent traveller and sports fan, the most valuable asset you can build is a personal record of reliable venues in the cities you visit regularly. Note down:

- Venue name and area
- Which leagues they reliably show
- Screen quality (1-5, your own assessment)
- Best time to arrive for your usual match
- Whether reservations are needed

After a few visits to the same cities, you'll have a mental map of exactly where to go for every competition — no searching required.
