Vancouver is a city that British expats should know about. The EPL times are, to be blunt, ridiculous — 4:30am for a Saturday early kickoff is not a lifestyle anyone would design. But here's the thing: Vancouver has one of the largest British and Australian expat communities in North America, it has bars that genuinely commit to opening at 4:30am for the faithful, and the midweek Champions League fixtures land at 12:45pm and 3pm local time — perfectly sensible lunchtime viewing.

Work out which games actually matter to you, then plan accordingly. Nobody is suggesting you drag yourself out of bed for a Southampton vs Brentford clash. But for the games that count, Vancouver has the infrastructure and the crowd.

And then there's 2026. BC Place is hosting World Cup matches — group games and knockout rounds — and this city is going to be transformed. The intersection of a huge British and Australian expat community, a growing Canadian football fanbase, and the spectacle of a World Cup is going to be extraordinary. If you're planning to be in Vancouver during the tournament, start thinking about it now.

## The Blarney Stone — Gastown

The Blarney Stone is the anchor of Vancouver's expat sports bar scene and has been for years. It sits in Gastown, the historic district that forms the heart of Vancouver's bar scene, and it has earned its reputation through consistency. This is where the British and Irish expat community gravitates for early EPL mornings — the ones who are still wearing their replica shirts at 5am on a Saturday, which is either dedicated or deranged depending on your perspective. Probably both. Multiple screens, a crowd that actually cares, and the sort of atmosphere that makes 4:30am feel less insane than it should.

## Johnnie Fox's Irish Pub — Vancouver

Johnnie Fox's is the Blarney Stone's main competition for the early-morning EPL crowd. It draws a similar demographic — British, Irish, Australian expats who have fully committed to the Pacific timezone punishment — and provides a reliable venue for big fixture weekends. Worth having on your list as an alternative when the Blarney Stone is particularly rammed.

## Irish Heather — Gastown

Irish Heather is a smarter venue than its Irish-pub neighbours — better food, a more considered whisky and beer selection, and a crowd that slightly overlaps with Gastown's foodie scene. It shows sport and opens for the morning EPL games, making it a good option when you want a slightly more civilised experience. Not the place for full-kit-wearing chaos at 5am, but excellent for a Saturday brunch session around the 10am fixtures.

## The Shark Club Sports Bar & Grill — City Centre

The Shark Club is Vancouver's big sports bar — large format screens, multiple sports shown simultaneously, and a mainstream sports crowd that skews heavily Canucks and NHL. For British sport it's less specialist than the Gastown Irish pubs, but for sheer scale and production values it's hard to beat. This is where you come for a Canucks playoff game, for NFL, and for the kind of World Cup match that half the city wants to watch simultaneously.

## Browns Socialhouse — Multiple Locations

Browns is a BC-based chain with several Vancouver locations and it punches above its weight for sport. It's a comfortable, well-run venue that shows all major sport without being exclusively a sports bar. The food is genuinely good, which matters when you're committing to a full Saturday of EPL fixtures. Good for groups where not everyone is purely there for the football.

## The Roxy — Granville Street

The Roxy is primarily known as a live music and nightlife venue, but it has the screen infrastructure for big sports events and turns itself over to major matches when it counts. World Cup 2026 is the obvious occasion — expect the Roxy to be one of the main city-centre venues for the big games. Not your first choice for a routine EPL Saturday, but absolutely on the list for tournament football.

## World Cup 2026 — BC Place

BC Place is hosting World Cup 2026 group games and knockout rounds and Vancouver is going to be one of the best cities on the continent to experience the tournament. The combination of a genuinely diverse and internationally minded city, a massive expat community from footballing nations, and the proximity to Seattle (another host city) makes this a serious football destination in summer 2026. Get tickets early if you can; the bar scene around BC Place and in Gastown will be electric on match days regardless.

## Practical Tips

**The timing problem:** Pacific Time is brutal for EPL. Accept it. The early-riser regulars at the Blarney Stone have made their peace with it and there's something genuinely communal about watching football at an ungodly hour with people who are just as committed as you are.

**Midweek CL:** Champions League at 12:45pm and 3pm local time is the silver lining of the Pacific timezone. Lunchtime football on a weekday is very manageable.

**Gastown vs elsewhere:** Gastown is the nucleus. Yaletown has bars but fewer dedicated sports venues. For pure EPL reliability, Gastown is the neighbourhood.

**Local sport:** Don't ignore the Canucks. Vancouver during an NHL playoff run has an atmosphere unlike anything else in the city. If the Canucks are in the playoffs while you're here, embrace it.
