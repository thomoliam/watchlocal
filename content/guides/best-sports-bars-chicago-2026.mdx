Chicago is one of America's great sports cities. Six major league teams, a fanatical local fanbase, and a bar culture built around supporting them. For British expats and international travellers, the challenge is the Central timezone: EPL matches kick off at 6:30am and 9am on Saturdays, which sounds grim, but the city's established British and Irish community has long since worked out the solutions.

Here's where to find them.

## Fado Irish Pub — River North

Fado is the cornerstone of Chicago's EPL scene. It's a proper Irish pub, not a theme bar, and the weekend morning crowd reflects that — a mix of Irish Americans, British expats, and anyone else who knows this is where the serious football supporters end up. They open early for matches, the screens are well-positioned throughout, and the staff are used to pulling pints before most of the city is conscious.

River North is an easy neighbourhood to get to, and Fado's size means you're not fighting for a stool even on a big fixture weekend.

## The Globe Pub — Lakeview

If you want the closest thing to an actual British pub in Chicago, The Globe is it. Lakeview is a neighbourhood with a strong expat presence, and The Globe has leaned into that — it feels like a place you'd find somewhere between Clapham and Kentish Town. Football on the screens, British beers on tap, and a crowd that actually knows what's happening on the pitch.

It's slightly further from the centre, but regulars will tell you it's worth the extra journey. Good for Six Nations rugby too.

## George Street Pub — River North

Another River North option that punches above its weight for international sport. George Street has a loyal morning crowd for EPL, and the pub's layout means you can usually find a decent angle on the screen regardless of where you sit. Reliable for Champions League fixtures midweek as well.

## Vaughan's Pub — Wicker Park

Wicker Park is one of Chicago's main expat clusters, and Vaughan's is the local that anchors it. It's a neighbourhood pub rather than a sports bar — smaller, more relaxed, and the better for it. Good for watching a match without the full tourist-bar experience. Know it exists before you find yourself in Wicker Park on a Saturday morning with nowhere obvious to go.

## Benchmark Bar & Grill

A solid all-rounder that covers the full range of American sport as well as international fixtures. Benchmark has multiple screens, a straightforward menu, and a no-fuss approach that works well if you want reliability over atmosphere. Worth knowing for weekday Champions League when some of the more expat-specific venues are quieter.

## Tavern on Rush — Streeterville

More of a Chicago institution than a dedicated football venue, but Tavern on Rush has the screens and the setup to cover whatever's on. Particularly good during American football season, but it handles international fixtures competently. The location near the Magnificent Mile makes it convenient if you're staying in that part of the city.

## Wrigleyville

Not a single bar, but an area worth mentioning in its own right. The cluster of Cubs bars around Wrigley Field — Sheffield's, Sluggers, Murphy's Bleachers — is a very American experience, but the atmosphere during a big game is genuinely something. It's not where you'll watch EPL at 6:30am, but if you want to experience Chicago sports culture at full volume, Wrigleyville on a Cubs game day is hard to beat.

## Practical Tips

**The timezone reality.** Central Time means EPL at 6:30am and 9am on Saturdays. The 6:30am fixtures are genuinely early, but Fado and George Street both open for them. For the 9am slots you have more options. If you're flying in from the East Coast, your body clock will be adjusted — lean into it.

**Midweek Champions League.** The 3pm UK kick-off becomes 9am CT for the early matches, 2pm CT for the main slate. These are civilised hours, and you'll find coverage at most venues without needing to plan too carefully.

**American sport is unavoidable (in a good way).** Chicago is one of the few cities where the local teams genuinely compete across every major league. The Bears, Blackhawks, Bulls, Cubs, White Sox, and Fire all command real attention. Screens in most bars will be shared, so arrive early for big EPL fixtures.

**The River North cluster.** If you don't know the city, start in River North — Fado and George Street are both there, and you can always walk between them if one is too busy.
