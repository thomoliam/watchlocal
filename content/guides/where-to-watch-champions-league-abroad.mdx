The UEFA Champions League is the most prestigious club football competition in the world — and one of the most watched sporting events globally. From the group stage in September through to the final in May, Tuesday and Wednesday evenings belong to European football.

If you're travelling or living abroad during the season, finding a good venue to watch Champions League is usually straightforward in major cities — but it pays to know what to look for and where to go.

## Champions League Fixture Schedule

The Champions League runs from September to May:

- **September–December**: League phase (previously group stage), matches on Tuesdays and Wednesdays
- **February–March**: Knockout round play-offs and Round of 16 first legs
- **March–April**: Round of 16 second legs and Quarter-finals
- **April–May**: Semi-finals and Final

Matches kick off at **18:45 CET** (first batch) and **21:00 CET** (main batch). Convert these to your local time:

| City | 18:45 CET kickoff | 21:00 CET kickoff |
|------|------------------|------------------|
| London (GMT) | 17:45 | 20:00 |
| Bangkok (ICT) | 23:45 | 02:00 |
| Singapore (SGT) | 00:45 | 03:00 |
| Sydney (AEDT) | 03:45 | 06:00 |
| New York (EST) | 12:45 | 15:00 |
| Los Angeles (PST) | 09:45 | 12:00 |

The late kickoff is the more important one — the biggest matches nearly always fall in the 21:00 CET slot.

## Best Cities for Watching the Champions League

### London

London is arguably the best city outside of the home stadiums to watch Champions League. With Arsenal, Chelsea, and often Spurs in the competition, the city takes Tuesday and Wednesday nights seriously. Sports bars around Soho, the City, and Shoreditch fill from 7pm onwards.

### Barcelona

Barcelona is one of the great Champions League cities — particularly in spring when FC Barcelona are deep in the competition. The city's bars are vibrant on match nights, with a passionate local following that makes even a group stage game feel important.

### Amsterdam

Ajax have a long Champions League tradition and the Dutch fan culture makes Amsterdam an excellent city for UCL nights. Leidseplein and Rembrandtplein both have clusters of sports bars that show every game.

## What to Look for in a Champions League Venue

**Multiple screens.** On nights when two matches run simultaneously (most of the league phase), a good venue will show both. Look for bars that explicitly offer "split-screen" or advertise multiple games.

**Sound.** Champions League deserves to be heard. The anthem, the commentary, the crowd noise — make sure the venue doesn't mute the match.

**Booking.** For semi-finals, finals, and any game involving a major English, Spanish, or German club, book ahead. Spontaneous Champions League viewing only works for less-popular fixtures.

**Proximity to transport.** For 21:00 CET kickoffs, you're finishing around midnight local time. Make sure you have a plan home — especially important in cities where public transport winds down early.

## Tips for the Best Champions League Experience Abroad

1. **Arrive 30 minutes before kickoff.** The anthem is part of the experience. Don't miss it.
2. **Find out which clubs the bar supports.** In some cities, a venue will be explicitly aligned with one club — which can affect the atmosphere dramatically.
3. **Check for a minimum spend on big nights.** Some sports bars require a food or drink minimum on Champions League nights, particularly for final rounds.
4. **Ask about the second game.** If you arrive for the 18:45 kickoff and want to stay for the 21:00 game, check whether the venue plans to show it.
