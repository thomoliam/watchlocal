New York City has one of the best sports bar scenes in the world, and if you're an expat or traveller who takes watching live sport seriously, you've landed in the right place. The city runs on sport — and thanks to the huge British, Irish, and Australian expat communities, you can find a packed room for a Premier League match at 7:30am on a Saturday just as easily as you can for a Giants game on Sunday afternoon.

The highest density of serious sports bars sits in Hell's Kitchen (roughly 9th–10th Ave between 42nd and 57th St) and Midtown, but there are solid options across every borough. Here's what you need to know.

## The Football Factory at Legends

The Football Factory is the most famous EPL bar in New York — possibly in the entire United States. Located inside the Legends venue on 33rd Street, it fills up fast on Premier League mornings and goes absolutely nuts for Champions League nights. They show every game, the staff know their football, and the atmosphere on a big match day is genuinely hard to beat outside of a UK pub.

- Books out weeks in advance for CL knockout stages — reserve your spot online
- Opens as early as 7am on Premier League Saturdays
- Multiple screens at every angle, no bad seats
- **Price range:** $$ | **Rating:** 4.8/5

## Stout NYC (Hell's Kitchen)

Stout NYC on 9th Avenue is the go-to for the Irish and British expat crowd and one of the most reliable early-morning sports bars in the city. It's a proper pub — Guinness on tap, big screens everywhere, and a crowd that actually cares about what's on. They're set up for EPL from 7:30am and keep it going through NFL afternoons without missing a beat.

- One of the few spots where you can watch EPL at 7:30am with a full Irish breakfast
- Great for NHL (Rangers) and NBA (Knicks) evenings
- Central Hell's Kitchen location, easy walk from the A/C/E at 42nd St
- **Price range:** $$ | **Rating:** 4.6/5

## Foley's NY

Foley's on 33rd Street is a New York institution, known primarily as a baseball bar (the walls are covered in memorabilia) but it punches well above its weight for any big event. The basement level is small and atmospheric — a great shout for quieter midweek fixtures when you don't want a massive crowd.

- Legendary for Yankees and Mets fans but shows everything
- Quieter and more laid-back than the Football Factory crowd
- Excellent beer selection and proper pub food
- **Price range:** $$ | **Rating:** 4.4/5

## Nevada Smiths (East Village)

If you're staying on the East Side or Lower Manhattan, Nevada Smiths in the East Village is the oldest soccer bar in New York and still holds its own. It's a bit rough around the edges but that's part of the charm — this is where serious football fans go, not tourists looking for a theme bar.

- Opens at 7am on weekend match days, no questions asked
- Known for passionate supporters' group screenings
- 14th St / Union Square subway stop, very accessible
- **Price range:** $ | **Rating:** 4.3/5

## The Australian on Broad Street (Financial District)

A rare find in the Financial District — The Australian is exactly what it sounds like, and if you want to watch NRL, AFL, or cricket at genuinely ungodly hours without feeling like you're the only one who showed up, this is your bar. They work hard to cater to the Antipodean community and the timezone challenges that come with it.

- Will open for 4am and 5am kickoffs for Australian sport — call ahead to confirm
- Surprisingly good food for the hours they're operating
- Dead quiet on weekends outside of match times — great for focused viewing
- **Price range:** $$ | **Rating:** 4.2/5

## Smithfield Hall (Midtown)

Smithfield Hall is a large, polished sports bar in Midtown that does a brilliant job of covering multiple sports simultaneously without feeling chaotic. On NFL Sundays it becomes one of the best places in the city — they have enough screens to show every game at once and the sound system is properly loud.

- Essential for NFL Sundays — arrive early, fills completely by noon
- Also shows NBA, NHL, and big Champions League nights
- Good food menu, not just bar snacks
- **Price range:** $$$ | **Rating:** 4.5/5

## Professor Thom's (East Village)

Professor Thom's is the unofficial home of Boston Red Sox fans in New York, which tells you something about its spirit — these people came to watch sport in hostile territory and they committed. It's welcoming to all, genuinely sports-focused, and has a neighbourhood pub feel that the bigger Midtown bars can't replicate.

- Excellent for NBA playoff nights and NHL evenings
- Smaller and more intimate than Midtown options
- L train to 1st Ave, or a short walk from Union Square
- **Price range:** $$ | **Rating:** 4.3/5

## Practical Tips for Watching Sport in NYC

**Timezones work against you for European sport.** EPL kicks off at 7:30am and 10am ET on weekends — the good bars open for both, but arrive by 7am for the early games if you want a seat. Champions League midweek fixtures kick off at 3pm ET, which is far more civilised.

**Book ahead for big matches.** The Football Factory and Smithfield Hall both take reservations, and you will need one for Champions League knockout rounds and NFL playoff games. Don't assume you can walk in.

**Hell's Kitchen is your base.** If you're staying anywhere in Midtown or the Upper West Side and want to watch European sport, 9th and 10th Avenue between 42nd and 57th is where you should head first. More options per block than anywhere else in the city.
