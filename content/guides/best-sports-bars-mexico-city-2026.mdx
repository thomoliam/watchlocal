There is one overriding reason to be in Mexico City as a sports fan in 2026: Estadio Azteca is hosting World Cup matches. One of the most iconic sporting venues on earth — the stadium of Pelé's greatest game and Maradona's hand of God — is staging group stage fixtures and knockout rounds in June. Watching those matches unfold in a Mexico City bar, with a city of 22 million Mexicans at full emotional pitch, is a genuine once-in-a-generation experience.

But even without the World Cup, Mexico City has a serious case for sports fans. Liga MX is deeply passionate domestic football. The NFL has built a genuine following. And the GMT-6 timezone — early for European football, admittedly — means the Premier League lands at 7:30am and 10am on Saturdays, which is manageable for the dedicated.

The neighbourhoods to know: Condesa and Roma Norte for the international expat scene, Polanco for the upscale crowd, and — for domestic football — honestly everywhere. Liga MX doesn't need a dedicated sports bar. It's on everywhere.

## The Pub, Condesa/Roma

The Pub is the anchor of Mexico City's expat sports scene. Multiple screens, reliable sport programming, and a crowd that mixes English-speaking expats, internationally-minded Mexicans, and the rotating cast of business travellers who always end up at places like this. EPL coverage is consistent — 7:30am kickoffs get a committed audience. Liga MX nights are excellent. For Champions League at 2pm on a weekday, this is a very pleasant place to be.

## The Irish Pub, Polanco

The Polanco Irish Pub serves the more upscale, hotel-adjacent crowd. Reliable sport coverage, good food, and a comfortable setting. Particularly good for NFL — Mexico City has a genuine NFL following, partly because the league actually plays regular season games at the Azteca. Sunday NFL here draws a crowd that genuinely cares about the results.

## Big Red Bar & Grill

Big Red leans American in its sporting diet — NFL, NBA, and MLB all get proper screen time here. If you're from North America and missing stateside sport, this is your first stop. It also covers European football, but it's the American sports programming that sets it apart from the Irish pub contingent.

## Patrick's Irish Pub

Patrick's operates on the standard Irish pub template but executes it well. Premier League weekend mornings are busy — 7:30am kickoffs attract the most committed fans, who tend to be the best company. Six Nations gets a proper treatment here, which is worth noting in a city where rugby union is not naturally part of the sporting diet.

## Rokai

An unusual entry — Rokai is a Japanese-influenced bar that has developed an unexpected sports following. The Formula 1 crowd in particular congregates here for race weekends, and the Japanese GP and other Asian-timezone races land at reasonable Mexico City hours. Interesting alternative to the Irish pub circuit.

## Cardinal Bar

Cardinal is more neighbourhood bar than dedicated sports venue, but it screens Liga MX with seriousness and is embedded in the Roma Norte fabric in a way that The Pub — slightly more self-consciously international — isn't. Good for watching Club América with locals rather than expats.

## Liga MX Cantinas

Don't overlook Mexico City's traditional cantinas for Liga MX. Club América and Cruz Azul fixtures turn the city's cantinas into passionate, loud, sometimes chaotic watching parties. The football is excellent (Liga MX is seriously underrated in European football conversation), the atmosphere is authentic, and the beer is cold. You don't need a guide to find them — just follow the noise on a match day.

## Practical Tips for Watching Sport in Mexico City

**EPL is early but doable.** GMT-6 means 7:30am and 10am kickoffs on Saturdays. This sounds brutal, but Mexico City's sports bar culture handles early starts better than you might expect — particularly when there's a full programme of matches to run through. Arrive at 7am, order breakfast, settle in for the morning. It works.

**Champions League timing is excellent.** The 8pm UK kickoff translates to 2pm in Mexico City — perfect for a mid-afternoon session. This is a genuine advantage over, say, Bangkok or Mumbai. Midweek Champions League afternoons in Condesa are a very pleasant way to spend a Tuesday.

**World Cup 2026 — this is the one.** June 2026, the Azteca is hosting. The matches themselves will sell out immediately and require tickets purchased long in advance. But the bar atmosphere in Mexico City during a World Cup group stage will be extraordinary without any ticket at all. Condesa, Roma Norte, and Polanco bars will be absolutely electric. Book ahead, manage your expectations on space, and enjoy one of the great live sport experiences available anywhere.

**Altitude matters.** Mexico City sits at 2,240 metres above sea level. This is relevant for sport-watching in one specific way: alcohol hits harder at altitude. Drink water, pace yourself, and be slightly less ambitious about your session length than you would be at sea level.
