Buenos Aires might be the best city in the world to watch football. Not because of the screen technology or the bar infrastructure — though both are fine — but because the football here is felt in a way that transcends sport. Boca Juniors vs River Plate is not a football match; it is a city-wide emotional event. The Argentine Primera División runs with intensity that the English Premier League, for all its quality, rarely matches in terms of raw feeling. If you're a football fan visiting Buenos Aires, you have won the lottery. For everyone else — there are very good Irish pubs showing EPL at noon, which is a civilised time to watch football.

## The Kilkenny, Palermo

The benchmark expat sports bar in Buenos Aires and the natural first port of call for British and Irish visitors chasing EPL. The Kilkenny does everything an Irish pub abroad should do: reliable screens, a crowd that knows the football, and a kitchen that can sustain a full afternoon session. The noon Saturday kick-off for EPL makes this one of the more enjoyable football-watching setups anywhere in the world.

- The go-to EPL venue for Buenos Aires expats and visitors
- Noon Saturday kick-off is genuinely one of the best sport-watching timings globally
- Champions League at 5pm local on Tuesdays and Wednesdays — comfortable and well-attended
- Irish and British expat crowd creates authentic atmosphere for European football
- Price: $$
- Rating: 4.4/5

## The Temple Bar, Palermo

The second major Irish pub in Palermo's expat corridor, and a serious alternative to The Kilkenny for EPL and Champions League. The Temple Bar has its own loyal regulars and a slightly different crowd — some prefer it for the atmosphere, others for the location. Worth knowing as an alternative on days when The Kilkenny is at capacity.

- Reliable EPL and Champions League coverage with a loyal regular crowd
- Palermo location puts it in the middle of the city's best bar neighbourhood
- Good food menu that sustains a long afternoon or evening session
- Rugby union well-covered during the Rugby Championship (Los Pumas matches)
- Price: $$
- Rating: 4.3/5

## Seddon Craft Beer & Burgers, Palermo

Not a traditional sports bar, but one of the better venues for combining quality food and drink with sport. Seddon is a Palermo institution — excellent burgers, a serious craft beer list — and it screens major sport reliably. Better for Champions League and big international fixtures than for routine league rounds.

- Excellent food and craft beer make it a venue worth visiting regardless of sport
- Good for big-occasion sport — Champions League knockouts, World Cup, Copa Libertadores finals
- Younger, mixed crowd with a strong local Argentine following
- Less oriented around EPL than the Irish pubs but reliable for major events
- Price: $$
- Rating: 4.4/5

## Bar El Federal, San Telmo

San Telmo is Buenos Aires's historic barrio — cobblestones, tango, colonial buildings — and Bar El Federal is one of its great traditional bars. It has been operating since 1864. Sport is watched here the Argentine way: football on the TV behind the bar, conversation as important as the scoreline, nobody leaving their table just because the game started. If you want to experience Argentine football culture rather than expat football culture, San Telmo is your neighbourhood.

- A genuine Buenos Aires institution with real local atmosphere
- Argentine football watched the proper way — communal, emotional, serious
- Historic setting that can't be manufactured
- Better for local Argentine football than for EPL or European competition
- Price: $
- Rating: 4.5/5

## Antares Palermo, Palermo

One of Argentina's best craft beer chains, with the Palermo location being the busiest and best-equipped for sport. Antares brews its own beer at its Mar del Plata facility — the Scotch Ale and Imperial Stout are reliably excellent — and the Palermo venue screens major sport throughout the season.

- Best craft beer in the Palermo sports bar circuit
- Major sport covered including EPL, Champions League, and local Argentine football
- Good food menu, more serious kitchen than a typical sports bar
- Popular with a young Argentine crowd as well as expats
- Price: $$
- Rating: 4.3/5

## Gran Bar Danzón, Recoleta

An upscale option in the Recoleta neighbourhood, Gran Bar Danzón is more cocktail bar than sports bar — but it screens major sport on its well-positioned screens, and the setting (a beautifully designed narrow bar) makes it a destination in its own right. Better for Champions League and Copa Libertadores than for routine rounds, and better for the cocktail-drinking crowd than for dedicated rugby fans.

- Genuinely beautiful bar design — one of the most elegant sport-watching settings in the city
- Champions League and Copa Libertadores well-covered
- Better for an upscale evening than for a rowdy afternoon session
- Recoleta location puts it in the city's most affluent neighbourhood
- Price: $$$
- Rating: 4.4/5

## Practical Tips

**The time zone is exceptional for European sport.** EPL at noon on Saturday. Champions League at 5pm on Tuesday and Wednesday. These are civilised, excellent sport-watching times. Buenos Aires may be the best city outside Europe for watching European club football purely on timing.

**For local football, do it the Argentine way.** Don't watch Boca vs River at a tourist bar. Ask a porteño friend to take you to their local. The passion in a neighbourhood bar during a Superclásico is one of the most intense sports experiences available anywhere on earth. It will recalibrate your understanding of what football means.

**Copa Libertadores is essential viewing.** South America's premier club competition produces extraordinary football and extraordinary atmospheres. When Boca or River are in a knockout fixture, the entire city is engaged. Any Buenos Aires bar — tourist or local — will have it on.

**Palermo for expats, San Telmo for atmosphere.** Palermo has the infrastructure for comfortable expat sport-watching. San Telmo has the soul. Both are accessible from each other by taxi or subway — there's no reason to restrict yourself to one.

**Language:** Spanish. A basic grasp helps in non-tourist venues, but football is a universal language in Buenos Aires and nobody will ignore a fellow fan.
