Cape Town is one of the most beautiful cities on earth, and somehow it also has a genuinely excellent sports bar scene. The city's expat community — a mix of British, Australian, and European — means consistent demand for EPL, cricket, and rugby union. But the local rugby culture is what really drives the atmosphere. When the Springboks play, Cape Town doesn't merely watch — everything stops. The V&A Waterfront and Green Point are the natural centres for sport-watching. Long Street adds the nightlife dimension. Here's where to go.

## Ferryman's Tavern, V&A Waterfront

The benchmark Cape Town sports venue. Ferryman's sits right on the Waterfront, has a large covered terrace, and has been doing this long enough to get it right. The screen setup is solid across multiple areas — you can find a good viewing angle whether you're at the bar, at a table inside, or on the terrace. On Springboks weekends, this place is essential.

- Best overall sports bar setup at the Waterfront
- Excellent for rugby — Stormers and Springboks matches fill it reliably
- EPL screens consistently, good Saturday afternoon atmosphere (2:30pm kick-off local time is ideal)
- Waterfront location means parking is straightforward, unlike central Cape Town
- Price: $$
- Rating: 4.5/5

## Slug & Lettuce, V&A Waterfront

The familiar British pub brand translates well to Cape Town. The Slug & Lettuce is the natural home for British expats who want EPL in a setting that feels recognisable — and the Saturday afternoon timing (2:30pm local, UTC+2) makes it genuinely excellent football-watching territory. Rugby is covered equally well.

- British expat crowd makes EPL viewing feel authentic
- Saturday football at 2:30pm local is one of the best timings anywhere in the world
- Champions League nights draw a mixed crowd with strong atmosphere
- Consistent food and drink quality
- Price: $$
- Rating: 4.3/5

## The Fireman's Arms, Green Point

Near the Cape Town Stadium and in the middle of the Green Point strip, The Fireman's Arms is a Cape Town institution. It's been around long enough to have real character — old signage, dark wood, proper pub lighting — and the sport coverage is extensive. The proximity to the stadium means match-day energy spills in from the street.

- Best atmosphere on the Green Point strip for major rugby
- Cricket coverage is reliable — South Africa Test matches shown throughout
- Older, more character-driven than the Waterfront venues
- Gets genuinely packed for Springboks and big EPL fixtures
- Price: $
- Rating: 4.4/5

## Grand Café & Beach, Granger Bay

This one has a view. Grand Café sits right on the water at Granger Bay with Table Mountain visible behind the city — it is, frankly, one of the most spectacular settings you will ever watch sport in. They screen matches on the terrace and indoors. The vibe is more restaurant than sports bar, but on a Sunday afternoon with the mountain lit up and a game on the screens, it's hard to fault.

- Arguably the most beautiful sports viewing setting in the southern hemisphere
- Table Mountain backdrop from the terrace is not exaggerated — it's extraordinary
- Better for afternoon cricket and rugby than late-night EPL
- Book ahead for weekends — fills up for reasons beyond sport
- Price: $$$
- Rating: 4.6/5

## The Sports Pub, Long Street

Long Street is Cape Town's nightlife corridor, and The Sports Pub is exactly what the name suggests — unpretentious, screen-heavy, and focused on getting the sport right rather than the aesthetics. It draws a younger, mixed crowd and runs late. If you're already on Long Street for the evening and want to catch a late game or extend a post-match session, this is the natural stop.

- Multiple screens, straightforward setup, no frills
- Good for late-night EPL — crowd stays energised
- Younger crowd, livelier atmosphere than the Waterfront spots
- Reliable for Six Nations and Rugby Championship
- Price: $
- Rating: 4.1/5

## Mitchell's Scottish Ale House, V&A Waterfront

Mitchell's is a Cape Town craft beer pioneer and one of the older brewing operations in South Africa. The Waterfront location does solid sport coverage, and the beer — brewed on-site — is genuinely worth the visit. EPL and rugby are the primary focus, and the crowd has enough regulars that the atmosphere builds properly on big days.

- Own-brewed craft beer is the main draw and it's excellent
- EPL crowd of regulars makes for a proper atmosphere
- Rugby union comprehensively covered
- Good food to accompany sport — the kitchen takes it seriously
- Price: $$
- Rating: 4.4/5

## The Waiting Room, Long Street

Rooftop bar with screens and a view. The Waiting Room is primarily a music and cocktail venue but it screens major sport and has become a reliable spot for Champions League and Springboks games. The rooftop setting with Long Street below gives it an energy that the standard sports bar setup can't match. Better for big occasion games than routine league rounds.

- Rooftop terrace gives it an atmosphere that's genuinely different
- Best for Champions League finals, Springboks Tests, and major cricket
- Cocktail and drinks program stronger than the typical sports bar
- Less reliable for lower-profile fixtures — check ahead
- Price: $$$
- Rating: 4.2/5

## Practical Tips

**Timing is exceptional here.** UTC+2 means EPL Saturday kick-offs land at 2:30pm and 5pm — perfect afternoon viewing. Champions League midweek games kick off at 9pm, which is late but entirely reasonable. For Australian sport, expect early mornings, which Cape Town generally doesn't do well. Cricket between South Africa and England or Australia is primetime local.

**Rugby is non-negotiable.** If you're in Cape Town during a Springboks Test or a Stormers URC fixture, get to a pub. The atmosphere — especially for a Bok Test against the All Blacks or British & Irish Lions — is one of the great sport-watching experiences anywhere. Venues fill up fast, so arrive 45 minutes before kick-off.

**The Waterfront vs Long Street.** The V&A Waterfront venues (Ferryman's, Slug & Lettuce, Mitchell's) are better for afternoon and early evening sport — easier to get to, safer late at night, more consistent service. Long Street is the better choice if you want to watch sport and stay out after.

**Seasons matter.** Cape Town winter (June–August) is cold and rainy, but this coincides with peak Super Rugby and Six Nations. Most venues have good indoor options. Summer (November–March) is dry and warm, making terrace venues like Grand Café exceptional.
