Pacific Time is the great obstacle of the San Francisco EPL experience. The first Saturday kick-offs land at 4:30am. By the time the main slate starts at 7am, you're looking at what most of the country would consider a lie-in. It's a genuine commitment, and the city's British pub scene has responded by building a culture specifically designed around it — early openings, dedicated crowds, and the knowledge that anyone else in the bar at 6am on a Saturday is there for the same reason you are.

The tech industry has helped. San Francisco's British and international community is large, well-resourced, and accustomed to operating on non-standard schedules. The British pub scene here is more robust than cities twice the size.

## The Chieftain — SoMa

The Chieftain is the most consistent EPL venue in San Francisco. It's a proper Irish pub rather than a sports bar — solid screens positioned throughout, a good bar, and staff who understand what a midweek Champions League fixture means at 9am Pacific. SoMa is an easy neighbourhood to reach from most parts of the city, which matters when you're navigating public transport at 6:30am.

The Chieftain opens early on match days and gets the big fixtures right. If you don't know San Francisco at all and need one reliable answer, this is it.

## Mad Dog in the Fog — Haight

The Mad Dog is a San Francisco institution. It's been running as a British pub in the Haight for decades — genuinely one of the longer-running British pubs in America — and it has the atmosphere of a place that knows exactly what it is. The Haight is an interesting neighbourhood anyway, and having a proper British pub embedded in it creates a slightly surreal but entirely enjoyable experience.

Critically for the EPL question: Mad Dog opens at 6am on Saturday mornings for football. That's not a marketing claim — it's the actual policy, backed by actual regulars who show up for it. The 7am wave gets a proper crowd; the 4:30am fixtures are for the committed. Six Nations rugby coverage here is among the best in the city.

## Kezar Pub — Haight

Kezar Pub sits right on the edge of Golden Gate Park, near the old Kezar Stadium. It's a neighbourhood local with strong sports credentials — the name alone does half the work — and a loyal regular crowd that turns out for EPL and the big American sport fixtures. It's a more relaxed alternative to Mad Dog if the Haight is your part of the city, and the setting near the park is pleasant in a way that's hard to replicate.

Good for afternoon fixtures when you might combine a walk with a match.

## The Dubliner — Castro

The Castro has a strong expat community and The Dubliner serves it well. It's an Irish pub without pretension — straightforward, reliable, good screens — and the neighbourhood means it draws a diverse, friendly crowd on weekend mornings. Not the biggest venue on this list, so worth arriving early for the major fixtures.

## Maggie McGarry's — North Beach

North Beach is San Francisco's historically Italian neighbourhood, and Maggie McGarry's provides the Irish counterpoint. It's a well-regarded pub with consistent football coverage and a loyal crowd. If you're staying in or near North Beach — or heading there anyway for the neighbourhood — it's the obvious choice. Gets notably busier during football season.

## The Liberties — SoMa

The Liberties occupies the same SoMa territory as The Chieftain and offers a slightly different take — more of an American craft beer bar with solid international sport coverage than a traditional Irish pub. Good screens, good beer selection, and a relaxed atmosphere that works well for longer sessions. Worth knowing as a SoMa alternative if The Chieftain is full.

## Practical Tips

**The timezone maths.** The EPL first wave at 12:30pm UK becomes 4:30am PT. The main 3pm UK kick-off is 7am PT. The 5:30pm UK fixture is 9:30am PT. Once you accept the 7am slot as your primary EPL time, the timezone stops feeling like a punishment.

**The 4:30am regulars.** A small but genuine community of San Francisco football supporters treats the 4:30am fixtures as their normal Saturday morning. If you find yourself at Mad Dog in the Fog at that hour, you're in good company.

**No NFL team in the city.** The 49ers play in Santa Clara, which means San Francisco doesn't have that American football anchor that dominates screen space in cities like Chicago or Boston. That's actually good news — bar screens are more available for international sport on NFL Sundays.

**Warriors dominance.** The Golden State Warriors have one of the most passionate fanbases in the NBA, and the team's recent success means basketball is a major competing interest on screens. If you're watching EPL during Warriors playoff season, check what else is on before you sit down.

**The British tech community.** San Francisco's tech industry has brought a large professional British and international community to the city over the past decade. That demographic has been good for the pub scene — venues have invested in screens, early opening, and a broader range of international fixtures precisely because the demand exists.
