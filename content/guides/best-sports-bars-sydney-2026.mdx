Sydney is Australia's biggest city and one of the best places in the world to watch live sport. Rugby league runs through the city's DNA — the NRL season is followed passionately from March to October — but AFL, cricket, football (soccer), and international sport all have strong followings. Sydney's sports bar scene is diverse, from dedicated sports venues to neighbourhood pubs and rooftop bars with big screens.

## The Best Areas for Sports Bars in Sydney

### The CBD and Circular Quay

The central business district has plenty of large sports bars open for lunchtime and after-work crowds. Venues here tend to be well-equipped — multiple screens, solid sound systems — and cater to the mix of office workers and tourists who make up the weekday crowd. For evening matches, the CBD empties slightly, giving you more room than you'd expect.

### Newtown and Glebe

Inner-west Sydney is where the AFL following is strongest. A significant Melbourne expat community means these suburbs reliably screen every AFL game, often with dedicated Melbourne club supporters in the crowd. Newtown pubs are generally no-frills, focused on the sport, and unpretentious about it. Excellent for NRL and Premier League too.

### Surry Hills

Surry Hills is one of Sydney's most vibrant neighbourhoods, and its pubs and bars reflect that. Plenty of venues here with solid TV setups and menus that go beyond bar snacks. Good coverage of Premier League and Champions League, and a go-to for international matches.

### Manly and the Northern Beaches

The beaches are a destination for sport watching too, particularly for rugby league. The NRL's Manly Sea Eagles have a loyal local fanbase, and local pubs show every game. The northern beaches area also has good coverage of international cricket.

### Coogee and Bondi

The eastern suburbs have a large British and Irish expat presence, which means strong Premier League coverage — sometimes at early morning hours (8am kick-off for a 10pm UK game). Look for dedicated English/Irish pubs in these areas if you're chasing EPL at unsociable hours.

## NRL Season in Sydney

The NRL season runs from March to early October, with the Grand Final typically held on the first Sunday of October. Sydney has nine NRL clubs across the city's suburbs — following any of them, you'll find local pubs that function as unofficial fan zones on game days. Look for:

- **Roosters**: Eastern Suburbs pubs
- **Rabbitohs**: Redfern and Surry Hills
- **Bulldogs**: Western Suburbs and Canterbury
- **Manly Sea Eagles**: Northern Beaches

## AFL in Sydney

AFL has grown significantly in Sydney, with the Sydney Swans as the city's main team and Greater Western Sydney (GWS Giants) based in Parramatta. Inner-west suburbs like Newtown and Glebe have the strongest AFL culture, with Melbourne expats filling the pubs for every game. The AFL finals (September) and Grand Final create an electric atmosphere in Sydney's Aussie-rules pubs.

## Premier League in Sydney

The Premier League kicks off at around 6am–9am on Saturday mornings in Sydney (depending on daylight saving). A dedicated group of fans assembles at specific venues from early morning — coffee in hand, scarves on. It sounds extreme but the atmosphere at a Sydney sports bar during a big EPL game at 6am is genuinely special.

Sunday morning EPL matches start around 8am–11am in Sydney, which is more civilised. Tuesday and Wednesday Champions League games kick off around midnight–2am local time — some dedicated sports bars open late or through the night for big knockout fixtures.

## Cricket in Sydney

The Sydney Cricket Ground (SCG) hosts Test matches and Big Bash League (BBL) games. During Test series, sports bars across the city screen the action — particularly the Ashes, which draws enormous pub crowds. The BBL's Sydney Sixers and Sydney Thunder have growing fanbases.

## Tips for Watching Sport in Sydney

- **Call ahead for early morning matches.** Not every bar opens at 6am for EPL. Confirm before you make the trip.
- **Grand Final day is a big deal.** NRL Grand Final (October) and AFL Grand Final (September/October) are major events — book early.
- **Screen quality varies.** Newer sports bars invest in large LED screens; older pubs may still have smaller TVs. Check reviews or photos if screen size matters to you.
- **Outside areas get full fast.** Sydney's climate means beer gardens and rooftop bars are popular. Arrive early if you want an outside seat.
