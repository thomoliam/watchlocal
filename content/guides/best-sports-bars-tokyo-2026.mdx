Tokyo is a genuinely great city to watch sport in, once you know where to look. Japan's sports culture is deep — J.League football, baseball, sumo, and rugby all have passionate followings — but the expat sports bar scene in Roppongi is something else entirely. Bars that open until 5am to catch early-morning Premier League, dedicated NFL followings, a rugby nation that takes Six Nations seriously. This is not a city where you'll be watching a pixelated stream on a laptop in the corner of a restaurant.

The key thing to get your head around with Tokyo is the timezone. UTC+9 means EPL Saturday 3pm kickoffs land at midnight in Tokyo, and Sunday early kicks are 9:30pm — actually a perfectly reasonable Saturday night out. Champions League nights start at 3:45am, which is where Tokyo's late-night culture genuinely helps. Many venues in Roppongi are licensed until 5 or 6am, and bars that specifically cater to sport fans will open early or stay open late for big European fixtures.

## Hobgoblin Tokyo (Roppongi)

The Hobgoblin is the closest thing Tokyo has to an anchor sports bar — it's been here long enough that most expats have a story involving it. Part of the British-run Hobgoblin chain, the Roppongi location is a proper pub: dark wood, multiple screens at sensible angles, and a crowd that knows what it's watching. Premier League and Champions League are the headline acts, but Six Nations gets serious treatment here given Japan's own rugby credentials. The food is pub-standard British comfort — pies, burgers, Sunday roasts — and the beer selection is better than most comparable venues.

- Multiple screens across two floors, good sightlines from most seats
- EPL, Champions League, Six Nations, and J.League all shown regularly
- Open late for overnight fixtures — check their schedule for specific matches
- Price range: ¥700–¥1,200 per drink, mid-range for Roppongi
- Rating: 4.5/5

## Legends Sports Bar (Roppongi)

Legends is purpose-built for sport in a way that few bars in Tokyo are. The screen setup is serious — large format displays covering football, American sports, and F1 simultaneously, often with separate sections running different fixtures. If you need to watch NFL while someone else in your group wants J.League, Legends is the place that can make that work. It draws a mixed crowd of expats and Japanese sports fans, which keeps the atmosphere genuine rather than tourist-facing.

- One of the largest screen installations in Roppongi
- Strong NFL and NBA coverage alongside Premier League
- F1 races shown live, with a dedicated fanbase for the sport
- Gets genuinely lively for big Premier League and Champions League nights
- Price range: ¥800–¥1,400 per drink
- Rating: 4.3/5

## The Pink Cow (Roppongi)

The Pink Cow is an institution — an American-run bar and gallery that has been drawing creative types and expats to Roppongi for years. It's not a traditional sports bar in the way Hobgoblin or Legends is, but it screens NFL and NBA with genuine enthusiasm, and its American expat crowd means Super Bowl night here is a proper event. The vibe is more eclectic than a standard sports pub — art on the walls, a varied food menu, and a crowd that spans nationalities — which makes it a good option if you want sport without the full-on bar atmosphere.

- NFL and NBA focused, particularly strong during American sports seasons
- Super Bowl events are a highlight — book well in advance
- More relaxed atmosphere than the bigger sports-focused venues
- Vegetarian-friendly food menu, unusual for sports bars in Tokyo
- Price range: ¥700–¥1,200 per drink
- Rating: 4.1/5

## Fiddler's Elbow (Roppongi)

A British-style pub that has been doing solid work for expat sport watchers for years. Fiddler's Elbow is slightly smaller than Hobgoblin but has a loyal regular crowd and screens Premier League and Champions League without fail. Six Nations gets big treatment here — the British and Irish expat crowd makes the atmosphere for England vs anyone reliably loud. A good call if Hobgoblin is packed for a big fixture.

- Reliable Premier League and Champions League coverage
- Six Nations atmosphere is excellent — one of the better rugby venues in Roppongi
- Smaller and more intimate than the larger sports bars
- Regular quiz nights and events between sport seasons
- Price range: ¥700–¥1,100 per drink
- Rating: 4.0/5

## What the Dickens (Ebisu/Shibuya)

Move away from Roppongi and you find What the Dickens in Ebisu — a British pub spread over several floors of a converted building, with a more neighbourhood feel than the tourist-facing Roppongi strip. The expat crowd here skews slightly older and more settled, which tends to mean better conversation and less chaos on big match nights. Premier League and Champions League are covered well, and they show J.League for local interest. Ebisu is a nicer area to drink in generally — less frantic than Roppongi at 2am.

- Multi-floor pub with screens on each level
- Good Premier League and Champions League coverage
- J.League shown for domestic fixtures — a good way to get into Japanese football
- Better food menu than most Roppongi equivalents
- Price range: ¥700–¥1,100 per drink
- Rating: 4.2/5

## Aldgate (Shinjuku)

Shinjuku's sports bar scene is less developed than Roppongi's but improving, and Aldgate is the standout. It's a British-style pub that draws a solid mix of expats and Japanese sports fans, particularly for Premier League. The location in Shinjuku makes it convenient if you're staying in that part of the city — no need to trek to Roppongi for every match. Screens are well-placed and the sound levels are set sensibly (a genuine issue in some Tokyo venues that crank volume to compensate for poor positioning).

- Reliable Premier League and Champions League coverage in Shinjuku
- Good mix of expat and Japanese sports fans — authentic atmosphere
- Convenient for anyone staying in Shinjuku or Shibuya
- Stays open late for overnight fixtures
- Price range: ¥700–¥1,200 per drink
- Rating: 4.0/5

## Two Dogs Taproom (Roppongi)

An American-style craft beer bar with sports screens that punches above its weight for atmosphere. Two Dogs isn't purely a sports bar, but it covers NFL, NBA, and Premier League competently, and the craft beer selection is the best of any venue on this list. If you care about what's in your glass as much as what's on screen, this is your spot. The crowd is younger and more mixed than some of the older Roppongi pubs.

- Excellent craft beer selection — the best in Roppongi for variety
- NFL, NBA, and Premier League coverage
- Younger, mixed crowd with good atmosphere for American sports
- Less focused on sport than Hobgoblin or Legends, but better beer
- Price range: ¥900–¥1,600 per drink (premium craft beer pricing)
- Rating: 4.1/5

## Practical Tips for Watching Sport in Tokyo

- **Roppongi is your base.** Nearly every expat-facing sports bar worth knowing is within a 10-minute walk in Roppongi. Make it your starting point and explore from there.
- **Late-night is normal here.** Tokyo nightlife runs late, and sports bars built for overseas sport are genuinely set up for overnight fixtures. EPL at midnight is not unusual — you'll have company.
- **Saturday is the best night.** The 3pm EPL kick-off landing at midnight Saturday in Tokyo is the sweet spot — you get a night out and live sport in one.
- **Book for Champions League knockouts.** Roppongi bars fill up for quarterfinals and beyond. Message ahead or arrive early.
- **J.League is worth trying.** If you're spending time in Tokyo, going to a Kashiwa Reysol or Yokohama F·Marinos match is a fantastic experience — but local bars also screen J.League and the atmosphere for big domestic fixtures is excellent.
- **Eat before you go out.** Roppongi sports bars serve food but it's expensive. The ramen and izakaya options nearby are better value for a pre-match meal.
