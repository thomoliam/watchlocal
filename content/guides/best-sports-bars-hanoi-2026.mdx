Hanoi is a different beast to Ho Chi Minh City. Where Saigon is sprawling, loud, and relentless, Hanoi has a cooler, more considered energy — and that extends to its sports bar scene. The expat community here is smaller and more settled, the bars tend to close earlier, and you won't find an entire street of open-fronted venues showing the same match simultaneously at 2am. What you will find is a genuinely warm community of regulars who know their sport, some excellent viewing setups around Tay Ho (West Lake), and the same unbeatable timezone that makes Hanoi just as good as Saigon for watching EPL on a Saturday night.

One thing that makes Hanoi unique: V.League (Vietnamese professional football) actually has a following here in a way it doesn't in the south. You'll find it on screens in the Old Quarter, and watching local fans care deeply about Hanoi FC is a great experience if you're spending any time in the city.

## Bier Garden — Xuan Dieu, Tay Ho

Bier Garden on Xuan Dieu is the name you'll hear most often from long-term Hanoi expats. It's the closest thing the city has to a dedicated sports bar institution — big screens, reliable sport coverage, cold Hanoi Beer and decent imported options, and a crowd that shows up for the big matches. The lakeside location in Tay Ho means you can sit outside on a good night, which is a genuinely excellent way to watch a Champions League match.

- Multiple large screens with reliable EPL and Champions League coverage
- Good turnout for NBA and UFC events on big nights
- Outdoor lakeside seating for fair-weather match nights
- Popular with long-term expats — the regulars here know their football
- **Price range:** $$ | **Rating:** 4.6/5

## GC Pub — Tay Ho

GC Pub is a dependable British-style pub that's been part of the Tay Ho expat fabric for years. It's smaller and more intimate than Bier Garden, which works in its favour for big knockout rounds when you want to actually feel something in the room.

- Reliable EPL every matchday, Champions League mid-week
- Intimate atmosphere — genuinely feels like a proper pub
- UFC and boxing shown on event nights
- Grab will get you to Xuan Dieu in minutes from most of Tay Ho
- **Price range:** $$ | **Rating:** 4.3/5

## Highway4 — Hang Tre, Old Quarter

Highway4 is primarily known as a Vietnamese restaurant with excellent food, but it's also one of the better places in the Old Quarter to catch sport in a setting that doesn't feel like a tourist trap. A good option if you want to watch a match and eat properly at the same time.

- Good food alongside sport — one of the best eating experiences on this list
- EPL and Champions League shown on match nights
- More relaxed atmosphere than dedicated sports bars
- **Price range:** $$ | **Rating:** 4.0/5

## Hanoi Social Club — Hoi Vu, Old Quarter

The Social Club is a hipster-friendly café-bar that has quietly become one of the better places to watch sport in the Old Quarter. The screens are good, the craft beer selection is strong, and the atmosphere on Champions League nights is something different to the traditional expat pubs — younger crowd, more mixed, and it all works.

- Craft beer and quality cocktails alongside sport coverage
- Champions League and EPL the primary sport shown
- Book a table for knockout rounds — fills up fast
- **Price range:** $$ | **Rating:** 4.2/5

## Polite Pub — Bao Khanh, Old Quarter

If the Cock & Bull is the institution of Saigon, Polite Pub fills a similar role in Hanoi's Old Quarter. It's been going for years, the staff know what sport needs to be on, and the atmosphere on a Saturday EPL evening is reliably good. It gets packed quickly for the big games — get there early.

- One of Hanoi's longest-running expat sports bars
- Reliable EPL, Champions League, and some boxing coverage
- Gets very busy for big match nights — arrive 45 minutes early minimum
- Central Old Quarter location, easy to reach by Grab or on foot
- **Price range:** $$ | **Rating:** 4.4/5

## The Bia Hoi Corner — Ta Hien, Old Quarter

This is less a single bar and more an institution of Vietnamese street culture, but it deserves a mention because some of the surrounding venues on Ta Hien now have screens up during big matches. On EPL Saturdays you can find yourself on a plastic stool at the corner of the Old Quarter's most famous crossroads, drinking bia hoi at 10,000–15,000 VND, watching football on a screen mounted somewhere above the chaos. It's not reliable, it's not comfortable, and the sound is probably someone's phone speaker. It's also one of the best experiences you'll have in Vietnam.

- Bia Hoi at 10,000–15,000 VND — the cheapest sport viewing on earth
- EPL shown on some surrounding venues during big match nights
- Completely unpredictable — arrive early and check what's on where
- **Price range:** $ | **Rating:** 4.1/5

## Summit Lounge — InterContinental Hanoi Westlake, Tay Ho

For when you want to watch the Champions League Final or the Super Bowl in comfort, Summit Lounge at the InterContinental is Hanoi's premium option. It's expensive by Vietnamese standards, but the view over West Lake is remarkable, the screens are excellent, and on a major event night it's a genuinely memorable place to watch sport.

- Premium setting with outstanding West Lake views
- Best screens in Hanoi for major events
- Champions League Final, Super Bowl, and major UFC PPVs shown
- Book well in advance for finals and major events
- **Price range:** $$$$ | **Rating:** 4.5/5

## Practical Tips for Watching Sport in Hanoi

**Same timezone as HCMC.** UTC+7 means EPL Saturday 3pm kickoffs are 9pm local — perfect. For late Champions League or US sport (2–3am local), double-check the bar is staying open. Worth a message on Facebook before you make the trip to Tay Ho at midnight.

**Tay Ho is further than it looks.** Allow 20–30 minutes from the Old Quarter by GrabBike — it's a proper journey. Plan your evening around being there rather than commuting back and forth.

**Bia hoi comes from the north.** The original bia hoi culture started in Hanoi. Ten thousand dong — about 40 cents. Order another.
