Auckland is New Zealand's largest city and one of the world's better places to watch live sport — provided you accept that geography has put you at the end of the world for European and British kick-off times. What Auckland lacks in convenient timezones it makes up for in genuine sporting passion. Rugby union is not a sport here; it is a civic religion. The All Blacks are followed with an intensity that visitors from rugby-playing nations still find surprising. The NRL's Warriors are the second religion. Add a significant British and Irish expat community, and you have a city that genuinely wants to watch whatever is on.

## The Occidental Belgian Beer Café, Vulcan Lane

One of the most respected sports bars in central Auckland, and one of the city's best pubs full stop. The Occidental sits on Vulcan Lane in the CBD — a charming, narrow heritage laneway — and has been doing serious sport coverage for years. Belgian beers on tap, proper bar food, and screens positioned well across the venue. It draws a mixed crowd of locals, expats, and regulars who know their sport.

- CBD location makes it excellent for after-work weekday fixtures
- One of the better setups in Auckland for EPL — regulars assemble for the early morning kick-offs
- Covered laneway setting means weather is never a problem
- Rugby and NRL comprehensively covered
- Price: $$
- Rating: 4.5/5

## O'Hagan's Irish Pub, Viaduct Harbour

The anchor of the Viaduct Harbour sports scene, O'Hagan's is an Irish pub that has absorbed enough of Auckland's rugby culture to feel completely local. It's large, well-screened, and positioned perfectly for the Viaduct's big-match energy. When the All Blacks play, this place fills fast. The Irish-pub DNA means EPL and Champions League are taken seriously as well.

- Prime Viaduct Harbour location — the Viaduct strip's most reliable sports venue
- Large capacity handles big All Blacks crowds without losing atmosphere
- EPL and Champions League well-covered, strong Irish expat following
- Excellent for NRL season — Warriors games draw dedicated regulars
- Price: $$
- Rating: 4.4/5

## Brew on Quay, CBD

Brew on Quay is Auckland's answer to the CBD sports pub: good craft beer, big screens, and a relaxed atmosphere that works for everything from a Tuesday Champions League game to a Saturday Super Rugby fixture. The waterfront-adjacent location and the quality of the beer make it a step above the standard big-screen pub.

- Quality craft beer selection sets it apart from straight sports bars
- Good screen setup across multiple areas of the bar
- Champions League and EPL well-covered for the CBD lunch and after-work crowd
- Rugby and NRL consistently screened
- Price: $$
- Rating: 4.3/5

## De Fontein Belgian Beer Café, CBD

A sibling venue to the Occidental, De Fontein brings the same Belgian beer focus and sports-coverage seriousness to a slightly different CBD location. If the Occidental is full — and it will be on big rugby days — De Fontein is the natural alternative. The beer list alone justifies the visit.

- Excellent Belgian and European beer selection
- Reliable sports coverage across rugby, NRL, and football
- Good fallback option when the Occidental is at capacity on big match days
- Central CBD location, easy to reach on foot from most city hotels
- Price: $$
- Rating: 4.3/5

## Ponsonby Road Bars

Ponsonby, Auckland's inner-city neighbourhood of restaurants and bars, doesn't have one dominant sports venue — it has a cluster of good neighbourhood options that are worth knowing about. The crowd here is slightly older and more local than the Viaduct, and the rugby passion is no less intense.

- Multiple bars along Ponsonby Road screen major sport — walk the strip and find the atmosphere you want
- Better for All Blacks and Super Rugby than for EPL early mornings
- More local, less touristy feel than the Viaduct
- Excellent dining options before or after the match
- Price: $$
- Rating: 4.2/5

## The Bluestone Room, CBD

A larger CBD venue that handles the big-occasion, big-crowd events well. The Bluestone Room is where Auckland goes when the All Blacks are in a World Cup final or the Warriors are in an NRL Grand Final — it has the screen size and the capacity to do justice to those moments.

- Best capacity in the CBD for major event sport
- Screen setup is genuinely impressive for big fixtures
- Super Rugby Pacific comprehensively covered throughout the season
- Service holds up under the pressure of big crowds better than smaller venues
- Price: $$
- Rating: 4.2/5

## Practical Tips

**The EPL time problem is real.** Saturday 3pm UK = 3–4am Auckland Sunday. This is not a civilised kick-off time. A small number of dedicated Auckland bars open specifically for early-morning EPL — call ahead to confirm before making the trip. Not every venue with screens will open at 3am for a mid-table Premier League fixture.

**For big All Blacks matches, the Viaduct is the play.** Multiple venues clustered together all showing the same game, Harbour views, walk between bars at half time. Arrive at least 45 minutes before kick-off for major Tests — venues fill completely.

**NRL timing is much better.** Warriors games and most NRL fixtures kick off in the evening Auckland time, which is civilised. The NRL season (March–October) is one of the better times to be in Auckland sports bars.

**Super Rugby Pacific is genuinely excellent viewing.** The Blues play their home games at Eden Park, and the city follows all five New Zealand teams. Super Rugby runs February to June — if you're visiting during this window, you'll have no shortage of excellent live rugby to watch.

**Transport:** Uber and rideshare work well across Auckland. The CBD and Viaduct are walkable between themselves. Ponsonby is a short Uber from the Viaduct.
