Nairobi is one of the best cities in the world to watch Saturday Premier League football, and most people outside Africa don't know it. EAT (UTC+3) puts the 12:30pm GMT kickoff at a perfect 3:30pm local time, the 3pm game at 6pm, and the 5:30pm at 8:30pm. You can have lunch, watch three EPL games, and still make dinner plans. Compare that to a San Franciscan dragging themselves out of bed at 4:30am, and Nairobi starts to look very appealing.

The city has a large and established British expat community — NGO workers, finance people, tech sector arrivals from Nairobi's growing startup scene — and they have built a genuinely good sports bar infrastructure. Westlands is the international district; Karen and Muthaiga serve the suburban crowd. Between them, you won't miss a game you actually want to watch.

## The Brew Bistro — Westlands

The Brew Bistro is the headline act for sports watching in Nairobi, and the rooftop setting is what sets it apart. Multiple large screens, a serious craft beer selection (rare in East Africa), and a crowd that's genuinely into the football rather than just there for a drink. The rooftop is spectacular on a clear Nairobi evening — and most evenings here are clear. Saturday EPL here is one of the better viewing experiences on the continent. Arrive early for the first game; it fills up fast.

## Gipsy Bar — Westlands

One of the longest-running expat bars in Westlands, Gipsy has the lived-in quality that new venues struggle to replicate. It shows all major sport, the screens are well-positioned, and the crowd is a solid mix of expats, local professionals, and international visitors. It's less flashy than Brew Bistro but arguably more consistent for atmosphere across the week. Good for midweek Champions League when you want a reliably full room.

## The Local — Westlands

Exactly what the name suggests — an unpretentious venue that serves a regular crowd of people who actually live in Nairobi rather than just passing through. The Local has earned genuine loyalty and that shows in the atmosphere on big match days. Sport is taken seriously here; you won't be competing with a DJ for the audio feed on a Saturday afternoon.

## Kiza Lounge — Westlands

A step up in terms of the venue — Kiza is more lounge than sports bar, but it screens major sport and the quality of the food and cocktails means it works well for Champions League evenings when you want something a bit more relaxed. Good option if you're with someone who isn't purely there for the football. The crowd skews international and well-heeled.

## K1 Klub House — Westlands

K1 is an institution. It's been a fixture of the Nairobi expat circuit for years and has a following that no new venue can simply buy. Multiple sports shown simultaneously, a large outdoor area, and a well-established loyal crowd. Rugby gets serious airtime here — Kenya Sevens is watched with genuine national pride, and the British Lions tours bring the place to a standstill.

## Barnley's — Karen

For those based in Karen, Langata, or anywhere in the southern suburbs, Barnley's is essential. It has the feel of a proper British country pub transplanted to sub-Saharan Africa, which sounds strange and works completely. The garden setting is beautiful, the food is well above average, and the expat community that uses it as a local is warm and knowledgeable about sport. Sunday sessions here after the morning EPL games are one of Nairobi's genuine pleasures.

## Soho Bar — Westlands

Soho completes the Westlands cluster — you could, in theory, walk between Brew Bistro, Gipsy, The Local, and Soho in under ten minutes. Soho skews younger and livelier than some of the others, which makes it excellent for the big weekend games when you want a bit more noise and energy. AFCON and African football generally gets a more prominent showing here than at the more expat-focused venues.

## Practical Tips

**Timing:** EPL Saturday fixtures at 3pm, 6pm, and 8:30pm EAT. CL midweek kicks off at 11pm — late, but Nairobi nightlife runs late anyway. AFCON and Kenya Premier League games are shown everywhere and draw enormous local crowds.

**Rugby:** Don't overlook it. Kenya Sevens is a genuine source of national pride and the World Rugby Sevens Series gets real coverage. Ask locals about upcoming Kenya fixtures — the atmosphere is different to anything EPL-related.

**Westlands vs Karen:** If you're staying centrally or in Westlands, Westlands is easy. Karen requires a drive (Uber is reliable and cheap) but Barnley's is worth it for a full afternoon or evening session. Muthaiga and Gigiri (near the UN complex) have their own embassy-area bar scene that's more subdued.

**Altitude and heat:** Nairobi sits at around 1,700 metres. Nothing dramatic, but worth knowing if you're arriving from sea level and planning a long session.

**AFCON:** When the Africa Cup of Nations is running, every bar in Nairobi has it on. Local knowledge about which Kenyan or East African team is performing well will earn you considerable goodwill with the local crowd.
