Hong Kong might be the best city in Asia to watch live sport. Lan Kwai Fong in Central is one of the most concentrated bar districts in the world, Wan Chai's Lockhart Road runs it close, and the expat community — British, Australian, American, Irish, Indian — is large enough to fill venues for almost any fixture worth caring about.

The city's sporting highlight is the Hong Kong Rugby Sevens. Usually held in March or April at Hong Kong Stadium, Sevens week transforms the city. The stands are a carnival, the bars spill onto the streets, and the whole city — locals and expats alike — buys in for the weekend. If you have never been to HK Sevens, plan a trip. It sits alongside Wimbledon and the Melbourne Cup as a sporting event that is as much about the experience as the sport itself.

Outside Sevens week, EPL dominates. At UTC+8, a 3 p.m. Saturday kickoff hits at 11 p.m. local — Hong Kong nightlife never really stops, so the late slate lands without much difficulty.

## Best Overall — The Globe (Central)

[The Globe](/venues/the-globe-hong-kong) on Hollywood Road in Central is the benchmark sports bar in Hong Kong. It is a proper pub — multiple floors, every conceivable screen angle, and a crowd that turns up for the sport rather than the scene. Expats from every background drink here, and the football knowledge in the room is real.

**What makes it special:**

- Multiple floors with dedicated seating areas for different fixtures showing simultaneously
- Satellite feeds only — no IPTV, no buffering during the critical moment
- Strong food menu with full pub meals available until late
- Craft beer and import selection well above the average HK sports bar
- Reliably packed for EPL and Champions League — arrive at least 45 minutes early for big fixtures

**Best for:** Premier League, Champions League, Six Nations, FA Cup, international rugby

**Price range:** Mid-range ($$). Pints from HK$65.

**Rating:** 4.7 / 5

## Best Wan Chai Institution — The Wanch

[The Wanch](/venues/the-wanch-hong-kong) on Jaffe Road is one of those places that has no right to still be going and yet outlasts everything around it. It is a live music venue that became a sports bar by accident and has never looked back. The screens are well-placed, the crowd is proper, and the atmosphere — especially for rugby — is hard to match anywhere in HK.

**What makes it special:**

- Legendary Wan Chai institution — open since the 1980s
- Warm, local atmosphere that the polished Lan Kwai Fong bars can't replicate
- Live music after sport on weekend nights
- Known for rugby coverage — fills up fast for Six Nations and Rugby Championship tests
- Unpretentious and welcoming — no dress code, no nonsense

**Best for:** Six Nations, Rugby Championship, EPL, Champions League

**Price range:** Budget to mid-range ($/$). Pints from HK$55.

**Rating:** 4.5 / 5

## Best Lockhart Road — Stormies

[Stormies](/venues/stormies-hong-kong) on Lockhart Road is the Wan Chai sports bar that the serious fans know about. It is louder and more raucous than The Globe, which is exactly what you want for a late Saturday EPL night when the full slate is running. The screen count is impressive and coverage is comprehensive.

**What makes it special:**

- One of the best screen-to-floor-space ratios in Wan Chai — no bad views
- Long happy hour that overlaps with early evening EPL kickoffs
- Popular with Australian and British regulars who have claimed their regular stools
- Reliable for less mainstream fixtures — Bundesliga, La Liga, and MLS get airtime here
- Kitchen serves bar food until late

**Best for:** Premier League, Bundesliga, Champions League, UFC

**Price range:** Mid-range ($$). Pints from HK$60.

**Rating:** 4.4 / 5

## Best Irish Pub — Delaney's (Wan Chai)

[Delaney's](/venues/delaneys-wan-chai) has two locations in Hong Kong — Wan Chai and Tsim Sha Tsui — and both deliver a classic Irish pub experience done properly. The Wan Chai branch is the better of the two for sport. Guinness on tap, wooden booths, and a loyal Irish and British expat crowd that takes the football seriously.

**What makes it special:**

- Best Guinness pour in Hong Kong — matters more than you think after a long week
- Strong Six Nations following — Irish expats turn this place into a temporary Dublin on match days
- Multiple screens at the bar and in booths — good for groups who want their own space
- TST branch is convenient if you're staying on the Kowloon side
- Quiz nights on non-sport evenings maintain a community feel

**Best for:** Six Nations, EPL, Champions League, Heineken Champions Cup (rugby)

**Price range:** Mid-range ($$). Pints from HK$70.

**Rating:** 4.5 / 5

## Best Lan Kwai Fong Option — Mad Dogs (LKF)

[Mad Dogs](/venues/mad-dogs-hong-kong) is the Lan Kwai Fong bar you end up in after the Globe is full. That sounds like a back-handed compliment, but Mad Dogs holds its own — large screens, central location, and a crowd that keeps the noise levels where a proper sports bar should be.

**What makes it special:**

- Prime LKF location — easy to find, easy to get a Grab home from
- Larger floor plan than many LKF venues — can absorb a big crowd on a Champions League night
- Multiple screens showing different fixtures simultaneously
- Popular with American expats for NFL — one of the better spots in HK for American football
- Food menu covers the basics well

**Best for:** EPL, Champions League, NFL, NBA

**Price range:** Mid-range ($$). Pints from HK$65.

**Rating:** 4.3 / 5

## Best Kowloon Option — Ned Kelly's Last Stand (TST)

Tsim Sha Tsui across the harbour has fewer dedicated sports bars, but [Ned Kelly's Last Stand](/venues/ned-kellys-hong-kong) on Ashley Road fills the gap for anyone staying on the Kowloon side. It is an Australian-run pub with a live jazz tradition that was somehow also one of the better places to watch sport in TST.

**What makes it special:**

- Australian ownership means AFL, NRL, and cricket get proper treatment
- Accessible from TST MTR — no need to cross the harbour for a match
- Lively even on quieter sporting nights thanks to the live music programme
- Aussie expat regulars who make newcomers feel welcome quickly
- Decent kitchen covering pub staples

**Best for:** AFL, NRL, EPL, cricket

**Price range:** Mid-range ($$). Pints from HK$65.

**Rating:** 4.2 / 5

## Best for the Rugby Sevens — Wan Chai, Generally

During HK Sevens week, the concept of a "best bar" collapses — the entire stretch of Lockhart Road and Jaffe Road becomes a continuous party. [The Wanch](/venues/the-wanch-hong-kong), [Stormies](/venues/stormies-hong-kong), and [Delaney's](/venues/delaneys-wan-chai) are all heaving. Get to Wan Chai by midday if you want a seat. Wear a costume. Know your team's pool fixtures.

**What makes it special:**

- The best sporting atmosphere in Asia, possibly the world for a two-day Sevens tournament
- Every bar in Wan Chai runs Sevens coverage — screens spill onto the street
- Hong Kong locals join the party in full — it is genuinely one of the most multicultural sporting events anywhere
- The post-match bars stay open until the last person standing
- Even if you don't have a stadium ticket, Wan Chai delivers the full experience

**Best for:** Hong Kong Rugby Sevens — nothing else compares this weekend

**Price range:** Mid-range ($$). Expect premium pricing during Sevens week.

**Rating:** 4.9 / 5 (Sevens week only)

## Practical Tips for Watching Sport in Hong Kong

### Kickoff Times in Hong Kong (HKT, UTC+8)

| League | Typical Kickoff (Hong Kong) |
|--------|----------------------------|
| Premier League | 9 p.m. – 1:30 a.m. (Sat/Sun) |
| Champions League | 3 a.m. (Wed/Thu) |

| NFL (Sunday slate) | 2 a.m. – 8 a.m. (Mon) |
| AFL | 8 p.m. – 1 a.m. (Fri/Sat/Sun) |
| Formula 1 | Varies by race location |

### Getting There

Wan Chai (MTR Wan Chai station) and Central / Lan Kwai Fong (MTR Central station) are both straightforward on the MTR. The two districts are one stop apart on the Island Line. Last MTR is around 12:30–1 a.m. — taxis and Uber are readily available after that.

### What to Budget

Hong Kong is expensive. A draught pint at a mid-range sports bar runs HK$60–75. Premium venues and hotel bars charge HK$80–100. Budget accordingly, and hit happy hour (usually until 8–9 p.m.) if you can.

### Champions League After Dark

UCL group stage games start at 3 a.m. HKT — most bars won't open. For knockout rounds and the final, venues like The Globe and Stormies stay open or run special events. Check their social media in the week before a big match. If you're watching from the hotel room anyway, Wan Chai's 7-Elevens are 24 hours and licensed to sell alcohol.

### HK Sevens Planning

Tickets for the stadium sell out well in advance — check official HKRFU resale channels. For bar watching during Sevens week, arrive in Wan Chai early (midday Friday for the first day). Book a table at The Wanch or Delaney's if possible. Costumes are strongly encouraged and universally welcomed.
