São Paulo is not a city that gets mentioned in the same breath as London or Singapore when expats discuss sport-watching destinations — and that's a mistake. Brazil's financial capital has a large international community, a wealthy domestic class with genuine interest in European football, and a GMT-3 timezone that puts the Premier League on at 10:30am and the Champions League at 4pm. Those are some of the best viewing hours you'll find anywhere.

Throw in the fact that São Paulo is hosting World Cup 2026 matches at the Estádio do Morumbi — with the whole city already at fever pitch months before the tournament — and you're looking at one of the most exciting cities in the world to be a sports fan right now.

The key neighbourhoods for sport-watching are Itaim Bibi, São Paulo's upscale international district in the south-west, and Pinheiros, which is younger, more bohemian, and increasingly well-served. Moema is worth knowing for Lord's Pub specifically.

## The Blue Pub, Itaim Bibi

The Blue Pub is São Paulo's most committed sports bar — multiple screens, deliberate sports programming, and a crowd that turns up specifically to watch. The EPL following here is serious: Saturday mornings draw a genuine crowd for the 10:30am kick, which is an impressive display of commitment. Champions League afternoons are excellent. If you're in the city for any length of time and you follow English football, this is your base.

## Harry's Bar

Harry's sits in the more upscale corner of São Paulo's international bar scene. Reliably screens Premier League and Champions League, with a clientele that mixes expats, business travellers, and Paulistano professionals who've acquired a taste for European football. Good food, comfortable setting, consistently good sport coverage.

## William's Bar

A slightly more relaxed atmosphere than the Blue Pub without sacrificing genuine sporting interest. William's has a loyal crowd for both European football and Brazilian domestic sport — the Brasileirão and Copa Libertadores screenings here are as packed as the EPL ones, which tells you something about the breadth of the clientele. Good to know that the bar takes Libertadores seriously — it's the best club competition in the Americas and watching it in Brazil with Brazilians is a very different experience from the sanitised English version.

## Lord's Pub, Moema

Named after the home of cricket, Lord's Pub has cultivated a following among São Paulo's British expats and cricket enthusiasts — a small but dedicated community. It also screens football reliably. If you're looking for cricket coverage in a city where cricket barely registers with the domestic population, Lord's Pub is your answer.

## Lion's Pub

Solid and dependable rather than spectacular. Lion's Pub covers the major fixtures without any particular personality — which is sometimes exactly what you want. Good fallback for weeknight Champions League when you don't want to think too hard about where to go.

## Sub Astor

Sub Astor sits at the cocktail bar end of the spectrum but has a good-sized screen and screens sport for a clientele that's more interested in the atmosphere than the scoreline. Better for a group with mixed sporting interest than for dedicated viewing sessions, but worth knowing for its location in the Vila Madalena area.

## St Paul's Cervejaria

A craft beer bar that leans into the city's name and screens sport with increasing regularity as its international following has grown. Champions League nights here are good — the 4pm local kickoff means you're watching on a weekday afternoon with a beer in hand, which is a very civilised way to spend a Tuesday.

## Practical Tips for Watching Sport in São Paulo

**The timezone works.** GMT-3 puts the Premier League at 10:30am and 1pm on Saturdays, and Champions League at 4pm on midweek evenings. These are genuinely excellent viewing hours. You're not dragging yourself out at 7am or staying up past midnight — São Paulo is one of the easier South American cities for European sport as a result.

**Brasileirão and Libertadores dominate.** Brazilian football is not a casual interest here — it's practically religion. São Paulo FC, Corinthians, Palmeiras, and Santos all have massive supporter bases in the city, and Libertadores nights can reshape the entire city's plans. If you're in town during a big domestic match, factor that into your bar planning.

**World Cup 2026 — plan seriously.** The Estádio do Morumbi is hosting group stage and knockout fixtures in June 2026. The whole city will be watching, and bars in Itaim Bibi and Pinheiros will be absolutely rammed. Book ahead, arrive early, and expect prices to reflect the occasion. Watching those matches in a São Paulo sports bar — in Brazil, in a World Cup year — will be something genuinely special.

**Itaim Bibi is the expat hub.** If you're not sure where to start, make your way to Itaim Bibi. The concentration of international-standard bars is highest here, the clientele is most likely to include other expats and English-speaking Paulistanos, and the Blue Pub is a short walk from most of the area's hotels and apartments.
