Johannesburg is South Africa's largest city and its commercial heart, and it takes sport with the seriousness you'd expect from a city that produces world-class rugby players, cricketers, and footballers. The Springboks are the city's collective obsession — South Africa's back-to-back World Cup victories turned rugby into something close to a national identity marker, and the atmosphere when the Boks play is genuinely extraordinary. But Johannesburg also has a large British expat community that ensures EPL is well-catered for, and cricket enjoys the kind of devotion here that comes from a country that genuinely competes at the highest level.

## Billy the Bum's, Sandton

The flagship Johannesburg sports bar and the city's most famous venue for live sport. Billy the Bum's has been in Sandton for decades and has earned its reputation through consistent delivery: large screens, good sound, a crowd that knows the game, and enough capacity to handle the full city turning out for a Springboks Test. For major rugby, this is where Johannesburg goes.

- The definitive Johannesburg sports bar — long-established, well-run, and serious about sport
- Best in the city for Springboks matches — arrives 45 minutes early for major Tests
- EPL at 4pm Saturday local is one of the best football-watching timings anywhere
- Cricket comprehensively covered — Proteas Tests, ODIs, and T20 internationals
- Price: $$
- Rating: 4.5/5

## Tiger Tiger, Montecasino

Montecasino is one of Johannesburg's entertainment complexes, and Tiger Tiger is its sports bar centrepiece. The scale is impressive — large screens across a multi-room venue, good sound, and the kind of capacity that makes it suitable for Super Rugby finals and Springboks knockout matches. Less intimate than Billy the Bum's but very well-equipped.

- Large-scale venue that handles major events without losing atmosphere
- Multiple screens across different zones — good for watching two fixtures simultaneously
- Strong EPL and Champions League coverage with a regular crowd
- Montecasino location means plenty of parking and other dining/entertainment options nearby
- Price: $$
- Rating: 4.3/5

## The Local Grill, Sandton

Primarily a steakhouse — and one of the best in a city that takes its steak seriously — but The Local Grill screens major sport and has built a loyal following for the combination. Watching the Lions play Super Rugby while eating a properly cooked South African beef cut is a specifically Johannesburg experience worth having.

- Best food of any sports venue in the city — the steak is genuinely excellent
- Major sport covered including Super Rugby, Springboks, and EPL
- More relaxed, restaurant-first atmosphere than a dedicated sports bar
- Good for weekday evening Champions League fixtures where you want a proper dinner
- Price: $$$
- Rating: 4.4/5

## The Knockdown Bar & Grill, Rosebank

Rosebank's most reliable sports bar, with a setup that suits everything from a midweek EPL game to a Saturday Super Rugby fixture. The Knockdown has a younger crowd than Billy the Bum's and a slightly more relaxed atmosphere — less intense on the big match days, but consistently good for regular-season sport. The food program is better than average for a sports bar.

- Rosebank location makes it accessible from a wider area of the northern suburbs
- Reliable EPL coverage — the 4pm Saturday kick-off local time fills it comfortably
- Good atmosphere for Lions Super Rugby fixtures — strong local following
- Younger crowd, more casual vibe than the Sandton heavyweights
- Price: $$
- Rating: 4.2/5

## The Grillhouse, Rosebank

Another Rosebank institution that combines serious food — specifically grilled meat — with reliable sport. The Grillhouse is more upscale than a standard sports bar, but it screens major fixtures and the combination of good food and well-positioned screens makes it a strong choice for afternoon sport when you want more than bar snacks.

- Upscale setting that doesn't sacrifice sport coverage for ambience
- Well-positioned screens visible from most of the restaurant
- Good for afternoon Springboks and EPL fixtures where you want a proper meal
- Rosebank location — safe, well-lit, easy to reach
- Price: $$$
- Rating: 4.3/5

## Melville Bars

Melville is Johannesburg's bohemian neighbourhood — 7th Street is the main strip — and it has a different character from the polished Sandton and Rosebank scene. The bars here are more local, less corporate, and the sport-watching experience is more neighbourhood pub than purpose-built sports venue.

- More authentic, less tourist-facing atmosphere than Sandton
- Multiple bars along 7th Street screen major sport — explore the strip
- Better for local football (PSL, Bafana Bafana) and for rugby with a mixed crowd
- Good for evening Champions League fixtures in a relaxed setting
- Price: $
- Rating: 4.1/5

## Practical Tips

**Timing is very good here.** UTC+2 puts EPL Saturday kick-offs at 4pm local — perfect late afternoon viewing. Champions League at 9pm local on Tuesdays and Wednesdays is a comfortable evening fixture. South African sport (rugby, cricket) runs on local time and is therefore always ideal. Johannesburg has among the best sport-viewing timings of any African city.

**Springboks = city stops.** This is not an exaggeration. During a Springboks Test — particularly against the All Blacks, or in World Cup knockout stages — Johannesburg's sports bars operate at the level of a national event. Arrive 45 minutes before kick-off minimum. Billy the Bum's and Tiger Tiger will be at capacity for major Tests.

**The Lions are worth following.** Johannesburg's Super Rugby franchise, the Lions, have one of the most passionate supporter bases in South African rugby. Home games at Ellis Park are extraordinary. Bar coverage of Lions matches is strong across Sandton and Rosebank throughout the Super Rugby Pacific season (February–June).

**Transport at night:** Use Uber or Bolt for all evening travel. Both services operate reliably throughout Johannesburg. Sandton and Rosebank are among the city's safest areas — well-lit, with security — but rideshare remains the sensible transport option for getting home after a late game.

**Cricket seasons:** The Proteas' home Test summer runs roughly October to February. The SA20 (South Africa's T20 franchise competition) has built a strong following and is widely screened in Johannesburg venues. When South Africa host England or Australia for Tests, the city's sports bars treat it as a major event.
