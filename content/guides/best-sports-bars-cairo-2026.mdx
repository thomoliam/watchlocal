Cairo's relationship with football is unlike anything else on earth. This is a city where Al Ahly and Zamalek — two clubs from the same Egyptian city — are among the most supported football clubs on the planet. Egyptian domestic football draws crowds and passion that put most Premier League towns to shame. When the national team plays in AFCON or World Cup qualifying, the entire city effectively stops.

For the visiting sports fan or expat, navigating Cairo's bar scene requires a little advance knowledge. Egypt is a predominantly Muslim country, and while alcohol is entirely legal at licensed venues, those venues are concentrated in specific districts — primarily Zamalek island, Maadi, and the newer developments of New Cairo. Hotel bars also reliably serve alcohol. Outside these zones, bar-watching sport as a western expat would recognise it is harder to find.

But within those zones, Cairo has some genuinely good options — and the GMT+2 timezone is excellent for European football. Premier League at 2:30pm and 5pm. Champions League at 10pm (late, but Cairo barely starts its evening at 10pm).

## The Bulldog Pub, Zamalek

The Bulldog is the cornerstone of Cairo's expat sports scene. Located on Zamalek island — the neighbourhood that functions as Cairo's international and diplomatic quarter — it has the combination of a reliable licence, multiple screens, and a clientele that actually cares about the football. Premier League weekends here draw British, Irish, and other European expats alongside English-speaking Egyptians who've acquired the EPL habit. Arrive early for the big fixtures; it fills up.

## Deals Bar, Maadi

Maadi is Cairo's other major expat neighbourhood — more residential and relaxed than Zamalek, popular with families and long-term expats rather than the diplomatic crowd. Deals Bar serves this community well. Reliable sport screening, good food, and a comfortable atmosphere that works for an afternoon Premier League session or a late Champions League night. If you're based in Maadi, this is your local.

## The Tap East, New Cairo

New Cairo is the modern, purpose-built district on Cairo's eastern edge, and The Tap East is one of the better sports bars in the region. More recently opened than the Zamalek and Maadi stalwarts, it has a modern setup with good screen coverage. The Tap East draws a slightly younger crowd of Cairo's professional class — Egyptians who've studied abroad and returned with Premier League habits. Good for Champions League; strong on international sport generally.

## The Tap West

The companion venue to The Tap East, The Tap West serves the western side of the city. Similar formula and similar quality. Useful if your Cairo base is west of the Nile rather than in Zamalek or the east.

## Hops Craft Beer Bar

Cairo's craft beer scene is small but growing, and Hops is the best example of it. Sport is screened but is not the primary focus — Hops is better as a venue where you can catch a match in comfortable surroundings with interesting beer rather than as a dedicated sports bar. Good Champions League option on a Tuesday when you don't want the full pub experience.

## African Sports Bars, Dokki

For AFCON and Egyptian domestic football, the viewing culture extends beyond licensed bars into cafes and unlicensed establishments in districts like Dokki. These are not the places to go for a beer, but they are the places to go for the atmosphere. Egyptian football fans watching Al Ahly in an AFCON campaign or a continental final are one of the great football-watching crowds on earth. The passion is extraordinary and the coffee is excellent.

## Practical Tips for Watching Sport in Cairo

**The timezone is generous.** GMT+2 is an excellent position for European football. Premier League Saturday kickoffs land at 2:30pm and 5pm local time — you're watching in comfortable afternoon and early evening hours. The 10:30am early kick hits at 12:30pm, also fine. Champions League at 10pm local is the late one, but Cairo runs late: restaurants fill at 10pm, bars are busy at midnight. Late Champions League nights here work better than they would in Munich or Amsterdam.

**Stick to licensed venues.** This is not complicated — the bars listed in this guide are all properly licensed. But it's worth understanding the landscape: walk into a random café or restaurant in central Cairo and you will not be served alcohol, and a sports bar as a European or American would recognise it won't exist. The concentration of options in Zamalek and Maadi is a feature of the geography, not a bug.

**Be respectful.** Cairo is a predominantly Muslim city and the bars that serve alcohol are in the minority. Behaving with basic consideration — not staggering through conservative neighbourhoods late at night — goes without saying, but it's worth saying anyway. The licensed bar scene exists comfortably within Cairo's culture; don't give anyone a reason to look at it differently.

**Egyptian football is worth watching.** Al Ahly vs Zamalek is one of the great derbies in world football. If you're in Cairo when the Egyptian Premier League is running, make the effort to find a venue screening it — or buy a ticket to Cairo International Stadium. The level of passion from the domestic crowd makes most European football atmospheres look restrained.

**Champions League late nights.** The 10pm start is the main friction point in Cairo for European football. Most venues will be open and buzzing until 1am or later without issue, so the match itself is fine — it's the scheduling around it that requires planning. Dinner at 9pm, bar by 9:45pm, football at 10pm: Cairo's natural rhythms actually suit this rather well.
