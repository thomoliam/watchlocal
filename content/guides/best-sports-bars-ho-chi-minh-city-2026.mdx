If there's one city on earth that was designed for watching live sport, it's Ho Chi Minh City. The timezone is genuinely perfect — EPL Saturday kickoffs land at 9pm local time, which means you're watching Manchester City vs Arsenal with a cold Saigon Beer in hand at exactly the right hour. The bars don't close. The beer is cheaper than a bottle of water back home. And the energy in this city at midnight on a match night is something you'll talk about for years.

Here's where to find the best screens in Saigon, whether you're a broke backpacker on Bui Vien or an expat settled in for the season in Thao Dien.

## The Cock & Bull — Ly Tu Trong, District 1

This is the one everyone mentions first, and for good reason. The Cock & Bull is a proper British pub that's been an institution in Saigon's expat scene for years. Multiple big screens, Sky Sports and beIN, and a crowd that actually cares about the result. They show EPL, Champions League, and will put on the AFL and NRL for the Australian contingent if you ask nicely — and there are enough Aussies in here that you usually don't have to ask.

- Full Sky Sports and beIN Sports package across multiple screens
- Strong EPL and Champions League coverage every matchday
- AFL and NRL shown on request — arrive early to guarantee a screen
- Draft beers from around 60,000 VND
- **Price range:** $$ | **Rating:** 4.8/5

## Bui Vien Walking Street — District 1

Bui Vien isn't one bar — it's an entire street worth of open-fronted venues all showing sport simultaneously. If you want to watch the Premier League for basically nothing, pull up a plastic stool at any bar along here, order a Saigon Green for 25,000 VND, and you're set. The atmosphere during big Champions League nights is genuinely electric.

- EPL, Champions League, NBA, and UFC all shown across the strip
- Bia Hoi available at some spots for 10,000–15,000 VND (about 50 cents)
- Best seats go fast — arrive 30+ minutes before kickoff
- Grab is the easiest way in; taxis struggle with the one-way streets
- **Price range:** $ | **Rating:** 4.3/5

## The Irish House — Pham Ngu Lao, District 1

A step up from the chaos of Bui Vien but still very much backpacker-accessible, The Irish House sits at the slightly calmer end of District 1 and does sport properly. Good screens, solid audio, and a menu that goes beyond beer if you want to eat through a match.

- Strong Champions League and EPL coverage
- UFC PPV events shown — check their Facebook for confirmed fight nights
- Better food options than most dedicated sports bars
- **Price range:** $$ | **Rating:** 4.2/5

## Muddy's Sports Bar — Bui Vien area, District 1

Muddy's sits a block off Bui Vien and pitches itself squarely at the sports crowd. The screens are genuinely big, and they commit to showing the full slate of weekend sport — EPL all Saturday, then NBA overnight if there are people who want it. The Australian expat community uses this as a go-to for AFL and NRL, and they'll open early (read: 4am) for Grand Finals and State of Origin if you've organised a group.

- One of the best dedicated sports bar setups in District 1
- AFL and NRL shown regularly — the Aussie crowd keeps it honest
- UFC events with big crowd turnout
- **Price range:** $$ | **Rating:** 4.4/5

## Pasteur Street Brewing Company — District 1

Not a sports bar in the traditional sense, but Pasteur Street is worth knowing about for Champions League nights and NBA Playoffs. The craft beer is exceptional, the screens are well-positioned, and you're watching sport with a Jasmine IPA instead of the usual lager. Book a table for big knockout matches.

- Craft beer well above average for sports bar drinking
- Champions League and NBA shown on match nights
- Book ahead for knockout rounds — fills up completely
- **Price range:** $$$ | **Rating:** 4.1/5

## The Deck — Thao Dien, District 2

For expats living in Thao Dien, The Deck is the neighbourhood anchor for big sport. It's a more polished setup than anything in District 1 — riverside location, good food, proper imported beers — and the crowd skews toward long-term residents rather than backpackers. Grab from Bui Vien takes about 15 minutes.

- Upscale setting with quality screens and sound
- EPL and Champions League reliably shown
- Riverside location — arrive early for the good outdoor seats
- **Price range:** $$$ | **Rating:** 4.5/5

## Saigon Outcast — An Phu, District 2

A genuine hidden gem for the Thao Dien expat crowd. Saigon Outcast is a creative arts space that doubles as a brilliant sport-watching venue — outdoor screens, hammocks, cold beers, and a vibe that manages to be relaxed and buzzing at the same time. The Aussie scene here for AFL Grand Final is apparently legendary.

- Outdoor screens in a unique creative-space setting
- EPL, Champions League, and AFL shown
- Grab is essential — it's slightly off the main drag
- **Price range:** $$ | **Rating:** 4.6/5

## Practical Tips for Watching Sport in HCMC

**The timezone is perfect.** EPL Saturday 3pm kickoffs are 9pm local — primetime. Champions League Tuesday and Wednesday games start around 2am for late kickoffs, which is late even by Saigon standards, but the city is still going.

**Grab is non-negotiable.** It's cheap, reliable, and gets you from Bui Vien to Thao Dien for around 50,000–70,000 VND. GrabBike is faster in traffic. For Bui Vien, walk the last 200m — the street is pedestrianised at night.

**Try bia hoi.** Fresh-brewed draught beer served daily, 10,000–15,000 VND a glass (about 50 cents). It's a HCMC rite of passage. Start there, move somewhere with screens.
