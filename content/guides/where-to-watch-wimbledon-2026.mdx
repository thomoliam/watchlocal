Wimbledon occupies a particular place in the sports calendar. It is two weeks long, it is played in the afternoon, and it is the one tennis event that people who do not otherwise follow tennis stop to watch. Finding a bar that will actually show it — as opposed to switching back to football the moment anything else is on — requires a little more effort than usual.

**Wimbledon 2026 runs from 29 June to 12 July.** The Ladies' Final is on 11 July, the Men's Final on 12 July. If you are planning ahead, those are the two dates to prioritise.

## Why Wimbledon Works in a Bar

Tennis is underserved in the sports bar world relative to its viewership. But Wimbledon is different. The tournament's two-week run overlaps with summer in the Northern Hemisphere, which means outdoor screens, longer evenings, and a crowd willing to linger over a drink in a way that cold-weather sport does not always attract.

The British cultural context travels well too. Wherever British expats have gathered — Singapore, Hong Kong, Dubai, New York, Sydney — they have usually found a bar that shows Wimbledon. The Pimm's o'clock culture may be harder to replicate (though some bars make the effort), but the viewing habit is firmly established.

The Finals weekend, in particular, draws a genuine crowd. It is appointment television for an audience well beyond dedicated tennis fans.

## Kickoff Times: What You Are Working With

Centre Court play begins at **11am BST** on most days. Key match windows:

## Where to Watch: City by City

### London — The Only Place That Truly Feels It

<WatchPageLink league="Wimbledon" city="London" href="/watch/wimbledon/london" />

You do not need to be at the All England Club to feel Wimbledon in London. The city hums with it during the fortnight. Pub gardens in Wimbledon village itself fill up with fans watching the TV outside. Across central London — Soho, South Kensington, Covent Garden — sports bars carry every match on Sky Sports.

For the Finals weekend, book ahead. London at noon on a Saturday in July, when the Men's Final is on, is one of the best sports-watching atmospheres you will find anywhere. The collective held-breath during a tiebreak in a packed pub is something special.

**What to look for:** Venues with outdoor space have an edge in summer. Confirm they are showing Wimbledon and not switching to football. The British pub networks — particularly those on Sky Sports — are your safest bet.

### Singapore — Prime Time Finals

<WatchPageLink league="Wimbledon" city="Singapore" href="/watch/wimbledon/singapore" />

Singapore is the best city outside Europe for watching Wimbledon. The Finals begin at around 9pm SGT — actual prime time. The city's large British and Australian expat community turns the Finals weekend into a genuine event. Clarke Quay and Boat Quay venues with outdoor terraces work particularly well.

The earlier weekday matches at 6pm-7pm SGT are also very comfortable viewing — arrive after work, order food, and settle in.

**What to look for:** Bars with BeIN Sports or British pub networks. Not every venue in Singapore carries the channel, so verify before you arrive.

### Hong Kong — Similar to Singapore

Hong Kong tracks with Singapore timing — 11am BST is 7pm HKT, and Finals afternoon sessions land at 9pm-10pm. The British pub culture is well established in Wan Chai and LKF, and several venues run Wimbledon specials for the finals fortnight.

### New York — Early but Manageable

The East Coast 6am start for morning play requires commitment. But New York has dedicated tennis bars — particularly during the US Open season — and several venues around Midtown and the West Village open specifically for Wimbledon mornings. The afternoon sessions, landing around 8am-9am ET, are much more accessible.

If you catch the Finals at 9am on a Sunday morning in a New York bar, with the crowd quietly invested over brunch, it is an unexpectedly pleasant experience.

### Paris — Lunchtime Tennis

The 11am BST start is noon Paris time, and the afternoon sessions run from 2pm to 4pm CEST. This is perfectly comfortable viewing. Paris has a strong tennis culture of its own — Roland-Garros finishes only a few weeks before Wimbledon — and sports bars that carry Sky Sports will show it. The challenge is that many French bars are not set up for British broadcasting.

**Look for:** Irish or British pubs in Paris (there are more than you might expect) and venues in areas like Saint-Germain or the Marais that cater to expats.

### Sydney — Late Nights Only

July is Australian winter, so daylight saving is not in effect. The morning start at 8pm AEST is manageable; the afternoon sessions running to 11pm or midnight are late but within reason for a bar night. Sydney has British pubs in Circular Quay and around the CBD that will show it. The Finals on a Sunday night (landing around 11pm) requires a committed crowd, but they exist.

## What to Look for in a Bar

**Broadcasting rights.** Wimbledon is carried on Sky Sports in the UK. Abroad, rights vary by country — BeIN Sports, ESPN, and local broadcasters all carry it in different regions. The safest option is always a bar that describes itself as a British pub with a Sky subscription.

**Quiet enough for commentary.** This is more important for tennis than most sports. A bar blasting music makes it impossible to hear the serve announcer or the crowd atmosphere, which is a significant part of the Wimbledon experience. Look for sports bars rather than nightclubs.

**Will they actually show it?** This is the real question. Some bars pay for sports packages and then default to football regardless. Ring ahead. Tell them when you are coming and which match you want to watch. A bar that has had this question twice already is a bar that is prepared.

**Multiple screens.** Wimbledon runs simultaneous matches across multiple courts. A venue with several screens may be showing different courts at once — useful if you want to follow a specific player.

## Tips for Finals Weekend

Finals weekend (11-12 July 2026) is the peak. Book ahead. Aim to arrive 20-30 minutes before play starts — you want a seat with a clear view of the screen, not a spot at the back straining to see over somebody's shoulder.

The Ladies' Final on Saturday 11 July tends to draw a different crowd to the Men's — more casual fans, slightly less packed in bars, often a better atmosphere as a result. The Men's Final on Sunday 12 July is the bigger event globally, and the bars reflect that.

Wimbledon is one of the rare sports events that rewards watching in a good venue rather than just at home. The shared experience of a tense fifth set, in a room full of people who care, is genuinely worth the effort.
