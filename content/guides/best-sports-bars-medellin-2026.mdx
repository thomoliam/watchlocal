Medellín in 2026 is not the city it was ten years ago, and the transformation is one of the more remarkable urban stories in Latin America. The digital nomad wave that found it in the early 2020s has deepened and diversified — the city now has a genuine international community rather than just a tourist circuit, and the sports bar scene has evolved to serve it.

For British and European expats, the timezone is a pleasant surprise: GMT-5 puts EPL kick-offs at 7:30am and 10am on Saturdays, identical to New York. After the horror of Pacific Time, that feels almost civilised. For Copa Libertadores and Colombian football, the timezone is the least of your concerns — the atmosphere handles everything else.

## Murphy's Irish Pub — El Poblado

Murphy's is the expat community's anchor for international sport in Medellín, and it's earned that position through consistency. El Poblado is the neighbourhood that most international visitors and longer-term nomads gravitate towards, and Murphy's sits in the middle of it — familiar format, reliable screens, English-speaking staff, and a crowd that mixes Irish, British, American, and Colombian regulars.

EPL coverage here is dependable. For Champions League midweek it's worth checking in advance, but on weekends the coverage is standard. It's the place to meet other people who care about European football in a city where Colombian football is the dominant passion.

## Cervecería Chelarte — El Poblado

Chelarte isn't primarily a sports bar — it's one of the anchors of Medellín's excellent craft beer scene, with locally brewed Colombian craft beers that will recalibrate your expectations of what's available in Latin America. But it has screens, it shows sport, and the combination of genuinely good beer and a relaxed atmosphere makes it the kind of place you spend an entire Saturday morning without planning to.

Good for matches where the atmosphere matters as much as the competition for screen space.

## The Pub — Laureles

Laureles is the less tourist-heavy, more residential Medellín that the longer-term expats tend to prefer. The Pub is the sports bar that serves it — a straightforward venue with multiple screens, good coverage of European football, and a slightly quieter, more local feel than the El Poblado options. If you've been in Medellín for more than a few weeks and have figured out the city's geography, Laureles is worth exploring, and The Pub is a reliable place to watch a match.

## Bogotá Beer Company

The BBC — as it's known locally — is a Colombian brewery chain with locations across the country, and the Medellín outpost is a solid all-rounder. It's not specifically a football bar, but the quality of the beer and the consistent coverage of sport make it a dependable option. The craft beer is good; the atmosphere is lively; the screens handle whatever's on.

## Craft & Draft

The craft beer revolution that transformed Medellín's drinking culture over the past decade produced a number of venues that are worth knowing even if they don't prioritise sport. Craft & Draft is the useful example — it shows football, the beer selection is genuinely impressive, and it attracts the kind of international crowd that has opinions about what's happening on the screen.

## Tejo Bars and Local Culture

Something worth knowing before you arrive: Colombia has its own sporting culture that runs parallel to and often overwhelms the international fixture schedule. Tejo — the Colombian sport involving throwing heavy metal discs at gunpowder-stuffed targets — is played in dedicated tejo bars across the city that are among the most enjoyable experiences available. They're not watching EPL, but they're an essential part of understanding what sport means in Medellín.

Similarly, Atlético Nacional's match days change the city. When Nacional are playing a Copa Libertadores knockout fixture, El Poblado bars reconfigure around it, street food vendors appear, and the noise is audible from places you wouldn't expect. This is worth experiencing regardless of whether you follow Colombian football.

## El Poblado Rooftop Bars

El Poblado's rooftop bar scene is genuinely one of the best in Latin America — warm evenings, mountain views, cold beer. Several rooftop venues have set up screens for big matches, particularly for Copa Libertadores and Champions League finals. The experience of watching a European final at altitude, on a rooftop, surrounded by a mix of nationalities, with the Medellín skyline behind you, is difficult to replicate elsewhere.

## Practical Tips

**The timezone is kind.** GMT-5 means EPL at 7:30am and 10am. The Champions League 8pm UK kick-off lands at 3pm local — perfect afternoon sport. You will not be waking up early to watch football in Medellín.

**Copa Libertadores is the main event.** When Colombian clubs are competing, nothing else matters. Build your sports bar itinerary around this reality rather than fighting it. The atmosphere is worth experiencing on its own terms.

**El Poblado vs. Laureles.** El Poblado is easier, more touristy, more English-friendly. Laureles is where longer-term residents end up. Both have the sports bar infrastructure you need.

**Altitude and alcohol.** Medellín sits at around 1,500 metres. Drinks hit slightly harder than at sea level. Budget your session accordingly, particularly for long morning EPL slots.

**Safety in 2026.** Medellín's transformation is real, not just PR. El Poblado and Laureles are both genuinely safe neighbourhoods for walking at night. Use common sense, as you would anywhere — but don't let outdated perceptions stop you from exploring the city.
