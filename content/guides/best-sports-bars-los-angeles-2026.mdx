Los Angeles is a genuinely strange city to watch sport in. The sprawl means your choice of neighbourhood is also your choice of bar — you're not crossing town on a whim. The early kickoffs are brutal for European football fans. And yet, somehow, the expat sports bar scene here is one of the best in the world. When you've got the largest Australian community outside Australia and a British expat scene that's been building since the 1970s, you get venues that take sport seriously.

Here's where to find them.

## Ye Olde King's Head — Santa Monica

The undisputed institution. Ye Olde King's Head has been serving Santa Monica's British expat community since 1974, and it shows — in the best possible way. This is a pub that opens at 5am on EPL Saturdays without complaint, serves proper ale, and fills with people who genuinely care whether Arsenal drop points.

The crowd skews British and Australian, the screens are well-positioned, and there's a fish and chip shop attached if you need ballast before a 5:30am kickoff. If you're new to LA and want to find your sporting tribe, start here.

## The Pikey — Los Feliz

Don't let the gastropub aesthetic fool you — The Pikey is serious about sport. Named with a wink at British slang, it draws a younger, mixed expat crowd and does excellent work on EPL and Champions League mornings. The food is genuinely good, the sound system is properly turned up during matches, and Los Feliz's relative quietness at 6am means you're not fighting for a seat.

## The Cat & Fiddle — Hollywood

A resurrection of the beloved Sunset Strip original, The Cat & Fiddle has rebuilt its reputation as Hollywood's go-to British pub. It opens early for EPL, has a terrace that's actually pleasant in California's permanent sunshine, and attracts a mix of British expats, industry types, and genuine football fans. The Guinness pours well.

## The British Chippy — Santa Monica

Primarily a restaurant rather than a sports bar, but it's worth knowing about for the Santa Monica cluster. If you're making a morning of EPL with Ye Olde King's Head, this is your post-match debrief spot. The fish and chips are legitimately good.

## Brack Shop Tavern — DTLA

If you're based downtown, Brack Shop Tavern is your answer. It's a proper neighbourhood bar that happens to have excellent screens and a crowd that's engaged rather than just background-noise-generating. Good for NFL, NBA, and the occasional European football morning when you can't face the Santa Monica drive.

## Pour Haus — Venice

Venice has a significant Australian expat community, and Pour Haus has become their living room. The AFL screenings here have a genuine atmosphere — these are people who've set an alarm and mean it. Good for NRL too. The beer selection leans heavily craft, which is no bad thing.

## Cock & Bull — Santa Monica

Another strong option in the Santa Monica British cluster. Cock & Bull is a touch more traditional pub than some of the newer venues — darker, louder during matches, less interested in the food side. Which, frankly, is sometimes exactly what you want.

## The Timezone Problem (And How to Survive It)

Pacific Time is genuinely tough for European football fans. The EPL schedule in LA:

- **Early Saturday kickoff (12:30pm UK):** 4:30am PT. Almost no bars open for this.
- **Standard Saturday kickoff (3pm UK):** 7am PT. The main early-morning session.
- **Late Saturday kickoff (5:30pm UK):** 9:30am PT. The civilised option.
- **Champions League midweek (8pm CET):** noon PT. This is the one that feels like a gift.

The trick is picking your battles. For the brutal early kickoffs, only a handful of venues will be open — call ahead, don't assume. For Champions League, you can afford to be picky.

## Practical Tips

**Traffic is non-negotiable.** A 6-mile drive can take 45 minutes in LA. Pick your neighbourhood based on where you're sleeping, not where you think you want to be.

**Call ahead for early openings.** Hours change, staff change. Ye Olde King's Head has earned its early-opening reputation, but every other venue should be confirmed before you're standing outside in the dark at 5:15am.

**The Aussie community is large and organised.** If you want AFL or NRL in LA, search for the relevant expat Facebook groups — they coordinate bar takeovers for major games and the information is more current than any printed guide.

**Santa Monica is the hub.** If you're only in LA for a weekend and want a proper sports pub experience, base yourself in or near Santa Monica. The density of options per block is unmatched elsewhere in the city.
