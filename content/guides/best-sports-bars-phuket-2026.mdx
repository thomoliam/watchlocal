Phuket is different from every other sports bar city in Southeast Asia, and the difference is demographic. This isn't a city of passing backpackers and short-stay tourists flicking between bars. Phuket has a serious, long-stay expat community — heavily Australian and British, a lot of retirees, people who have been here ten or fifteen years and have extremely strong opinions about which screen has the best picture and whether the bar opens early enough for the 4am AFL Grand Final.

That shapes everything. The sports bars here are real, regular, established. People have their seats. The bar knows their drinks. And when the AFL Grand Final or a Premier League relegation decider comes around, Phuket genuinely goes off in a way that most beach destinations can't match.

Zones matter. Patong is the main strip — Bangla Road and the surrounding streets have the highest density of sports bars, noisiest crowds, and the widest range of screens. Kata and Karon are quieter and more residential — where the long-term expats who have moved away from the Patong madness end up. Rawai and Nai Harn, down at the south end of the island, are the most local-feeling and most community-driven.

## Molly Malone's Irish Pub (Patong)

The most reliable sports bar in Phuket, full stop. Molly Malone's is the place that other bars measure themselves against. Multiple screens covering every angle, decent food that doesn't disappoint when you've been there for six hours of back-to-back matches, and a crowd that shows up for everything from Munster vs Leinster to a mid-table Premier League Saturday. Has been here long enough to know what it's doing.

- Best screen coverage in Patong — no bad seats
- Strong food menu; you won't need to leave between kickoffs
- Reliable crowd for rugby union and rugby league as well as football
- Regular Premier League and Champions League events
- **Price:** $$$
- **Rating:** 4.7/5

## The Green Man (Patong)

A proper English pub that feels transplanted from somewhere in the Midlands — and that's a compliment. The Green Man attracts a crowd that's there to watch the football, not to be seen watching the football. It's louder than Molly Malone's, cheaper on the beer, and the banter is better. Go here for a Premier League Saturday night when you want a room full of people who are actually invested in the result.

- Authentic British pub atmosphere
- Cheaper beer prices than the bigger tourist venues
- Great for high-stakes Premier League nights
- Opens early for Champions League midweek games
- **Price:** $$
- **Rating:** 4.5/5

## Aussie Bar (Patong)

The name tells you everything. This is the AFL Grand Final bar in Phuket. Every September, hundreds of Australians pack into Aussie Bar from 4am for the Grand Final, and if you've never experienced that specific kind of unhinged pre-dawn sports energy, Phuket in September is the place to find out. But don't only come for the Grand Final — the bar shows NRL, State of Origin, and Australian cricket with the same commitment, and the crowd holds up year-round.

- The AFL Grand Final venue for Phuket's Australian community
- State of Origin and NRL covered comprehensively
- Brilliant atmosphere for big Australian sport events
- Opens at 4am for early-morning fixtures — no half measures
- **Price:** $$
- **Rating:** 4.6/5

## Kata Corner (Kata Beach)

If Patong is too much — and sometimes it is — Kata Corner is what you're looking for. Smaller, more local, genuinely friendly. The expats who drink here have been coming for years and there's a community feel that the bigger Patong venues can't manufacture. Good screens, decent kitchen, cold beer. For a Sunday EPL afternoon with a proper crowd that's not two hundred tourists, this is the one.

- Quieter, community feel — long-stay expat crowd
- Better for afternoon fixtures when Patong feels overwhelming
- Solid food and cold beer without the Bangla Road chaos
- **Price:** $$
- **Rating:** 4.4/5

## Baan Kata Bar (Kata/Karon area)

A step up in terms of setting — this is the option when you want to watch the match somewhere that doesn't feel like a pub basement. Outdoor seating, reasonable food, big screens positioned so you can see them from the terrace. The Premier League crowd here tends to be slightly older and less frantic than Patong. Champions League nights draw a proper audience. Worth knowing about if you're based down the south end of the island.

- Outdoor terrace with good screen visibility
- More upscale setting than the Patong strip
- Consistent UCL and EPL coverage
- **Price:** $$$
- **Rating:** 4.3/5

## Rawai Sports Bar (Rawai/Nai Harn)

Rawai is as far from Bangla Road as you can get in Phuket while still being in Phuket, and the bars down here reflect that. The crowd is locals and long-stay residents who have zero interest in the tourist circus. Rawai Sports Bar is unpretentious, cheap, and has screens showing everything. The kind of place where you genuinely become a regular within a week if you're staying in the area. AFL and NRL get shown — the Aussie community south of the island is strong.

- Most local and least tourist-facing option on this list
- Very reasonable prices
- Strong Aussie community — AFL and NRL covered
- **Price:** $
- **Rating:** 4.2/5

## Banana Disco Area Sports Bars (Patong)

The cluster of bars around the Patong walking street and the streets behind Bangla Road have a rotating cast of sports bars that come and go, but the better-established ones here are solid for Champions League nights and big EPL fixtures when the main venues are sold out. Less consistent than the named venues, but if Molly Malone's is rammed and you need a screen fast, working this area will find you something.

- Good backup when main venues are at capacity
- Multiple screens spread across several bars
- Best for Champions League and weekend EPL
- **Price:** $$
- **Rating:** 4.1/5

## Practical Tips

**The AFL Grand Final is a proper event here.** We're talking hundreds of people at 4am local time. Aussie Bar is the main venue but other bars run events too. If you're in Phuket in September, check the expat Facebook groups (Phuket Expats is the main one) about two weeks out — venues announce their Grand Final setups with reserved seating and breakfast menus.

**Daytime sport is big.** Phuket's retired expat population means afternoon fixtures are genuinely well-attended in a way they aren't in younger expat cities. Saturday afternoon EPL is a social occasion. Don't assume a 2pm local kickoff will be empty.

**Kata and Rawai are worth the drive.** If you're based in Patong the temptation is to stay in Patong. But for the right kind of watch — quieter, more local, better food — the Kata and Rawai options are twenty minutes away and a completely different experience.

**Screen quality varies.** The bigger Patong venues (Molly Malone's, Green Man) have invested in good screens. Some of the smaller bars are still running older setups. If screen quality matters to you, stick to the named venues or walk in and check before you commit to the match.

**Tuk-tuks vs Grab.** Grab works fine in Phuket but can be patchy late at night in Patong when demand spikes post-match. If you're heading back to a quieter area after a big Champions League night, sort your ride before the final whistle.
