Chiang Mai is one of the best cities in Southeast Asia for watching sport. Not because it has the most bars — Bangkok destroys it on that front — but because the timing is perfect and the crowd is right. EPL Saturday 3pm kicks off at 9pm local time. You finish dinner, grab a Chang, and you're sat in front of a big screen for a full night of football. That's not an accident. That's why so many British and Australian expats stopped moving.

The city splits into a few zones. Nimman (Nimmanhaemin Road) is where the digital nomads and longer-stay expats gravitate — better beer selection, calmer vibe, actual food. Old City inside the moat has the backpacker energy, cheaper Chang buckets, louder crowds. Chang Klan and the Night Bazaar strip is more tourist-facing but has some dependable British-run spots that have been there for years. Pick your crowd.

## The John Bull Pub

The anchor of the British expat scene in Chiang Mai. It's on Chang Klan — not the coolest address in town — but John Bull has been showing football here long enough that regulars know exactly which screen has the best angle for each seat. Multiple big screens, proper full English breakfasts, and a crowd that actually knows what a false nine is. Gets properly packed for North West derbies and Champions League knockouts.

- Multiple large screens with no bad seats
- Full British menu — pies, brekkie, Sunday roast
- Strong turnout for mid-week UCL games
- **Price:** $$
- **Rating:** 4.6/5

## Warm Up Cafe

Technically a music venue — but Warm Up has screens, it's on Nimman, and the crowd skews international and switched-on. For daytime weekend matches it's one of the more comfortable options: proper coffee, decent food, air con that actually works. Don't come here expecting a wall of screens and a sea of replica kits. Come here for a more relaxed watch on a Sunday morning with a flat white and the early kickoff.

- Great daytime atmosphere for weekend morning fixtures
- Strong coffee and brunch menu
- More mixed crowd than a dedicated sports bar
- **Price:** $$
- **Rating:** 4.3/5

## Zoe in Yellow Area

The bars clustered around the Zoe in Yellow complex (just north of the Old City moat near Ratchamanka) are the late-night option when you want noise. These are backpacker bars that have screens, not sports bars that happen to have a good time — understand the difference. But for Champions League nights or big Premier League fixtures when you want energy and cheap beer, this area delivers. Get there early to claim a good spot.

- Cheap beer and high energy atmosphere
- Best for night-game EPL and UCL
- Standing room only for big matches
- **Price:** $
- **Rating:** 4.1/5

## Camp (Nimman)

One of several coffee-with-screens spots on the Nimman strip. Camp (the Maya Mall branch especially) has decent screens and reliable wifi, which matters when you're a nomad who wants to half-work, half-watch a 1pm Sunday fixture. It's not a sports bar and nobody pretends it is. But the early EPL kickoffs on a quiet Sunday? Genuinely one of the more pleasant ways to watch football in Thailand.

- Open early — catches the 6pm local Saturday early kickoff
- Good coffee and light food
- Quieter and more focused than the bar scene
- **Price:** $
- **Rating:** 4.2/5

## The Pub (Nimman Soi 7)

A straightforward British-leaning pub sitting quietly on one of the Nimman sois. Regulars know it, tourists often miss it. Multiple screens, cold beer, reliable kitchen. The crowd is predominantly long-stay expats in their 30s and 40s — less chaotic than the Old City options, less pretentious than some of the newer craft beer places. AFL and NRL get shown here regularly, which tells you everything about who drinks here.

- Regular AFL and NRL coverage — strong Aussie following
- Quieter and more local than the Night Bazaar strip
- **Price:** $$
- **Rating:** 4.4/5

## Boy's Craft Beer Bar

Chiang Mai has quietly become one of the better craft beer cities in Southeast Asia, and Boy's is among the best of the local bottle shops with taps. They have a screen, they show sport, and the beer list embarrasses most of what you'd find back home. This is your move when you want to watch the match but you're done with Chang and Leo. Fills up for big Saturday night EPL fixtures with a crowd that's equally interested in the beer and the game.

- Best beer selection of any sports-watching spot in the city
- Screen is solid; atmosphere is relaxed but engaged
- **Price:** $$$
- **Rating:** 4.5/5

## The North Gate Jazz Co-Op Area (Old City North)

Not a sports bar, but the cluster of bars around the North Gate (Pratu Chang Phueak) side of the old city has enough screens spread across enough venues that you can usually find a spot showing what you're after. Useful to know when the more obvious spots are rammed for a big fixture. The bars here are laid-back, local-leaning, and significantly cheaper than anything on Nimman.

- Good backup option when main spots are full
- Very cheap by Chiang Mai standards
- **Price:** $
- **Rating:** 4.0/5

## Practical Tips

**Timing is your friend.** UTC+7 means EPL Saturday 3pm = 9pm local. You will never struggle to find a crowd for a big Premier League night here. Early kickoffs (12:30pm UK) land at 6:30pm — still a perfectly decent evening slot.

**AFL and NRL are taken seriously.** Chiang Mai has a larger Australian expat community than people expect. The John Bull and The Pub (Nimman) both show AFL regularly. Grand Final events happen — check expat Facebook groups (Chiang Mai Expats is active) a few days out.

**Nimman vs Old City.** If you want comfort, food, and craft beer: stay on Nimman. If you want noise, cheap Chang, and a crowd of backpackers: Old City is fifteen minutes by Grab. Both are valid depending on the fixture.

**Book ahead for derbies.** Man Utd vs Liverpool, El Clásico, UCL finals — the reliable expat bars fill up fast. John Bull in particular gets rammed. Message ahead or show up 30-45 minutes early to get a table near a good screen.

**Grab is cheap.** Don't stress about being in the wrong zone. A Grab across the whole city rarely tops 80 baht. You can be in Nimman, realise your bar is packed, and be in the Old City within ten minutes.
