Zurich has a secret that its reputation as the world's most expensive city tends to obscure: it is an excellent place to watch Premier League football. CET (UTC+1) in winter gives you 1:30pm and 4pm kickoffs on Saturdays. CEST in summer adds an hour. Either way, you're sitting in a well-appointed bar in one of Europe's great cities, watching live EPL at entirely civilised times, surrounded by a large and financially comfortable expat community who actually know their football. Yes, the pint costs CHF 8–12. It costs less than you might think when you factor in what else Zurich offers.

The expat community here is dominated by the banking and finance sector, with a significant addition from tech and pharmaceutical workers. British, Irish, American, and Australian expats form the core sports bar crowd. They're demanding customers — they've watched a lot of football in a lot of cities — and the better venues have risen to meet that.

There's one more thing worth knowing: UEFA is headquartered in Zurich. Champions League isn't just a television broadcast here — it's, in a peculiar way, a local matter.

## Oliver Twist — Niederdorf

Oliver Twist is the best EPL bar in Zurich and most expats will tell you so without hesitation. It sits in the Niederdorf, the old town's bar and restaurant district, and has the combination of committed ownership, good screen placement, and a regular crowd that makes sports bars work. Saturday EPL here is as good as anywhere in Switzerland. The venue is unapologetically oriented around showing football well — not a trendy restaurant that happens to have screens. A proper sports pub.

Come back on Sunday mornings if you follow American football. A significant American expat community has established NFL viewing traditions here — the finance sector brings over enough US nationals that Sunday morning NFL sessions at Oliver Twist have quietly become a fixture.

## Paddy Reilly's Irish Pub — City Centre

Paddy Reilly's is the main Irish pub option, centrally located and drawing the expected Irish and British expat crowd. It's lively, shows all the major sport, and has the lived-in quality that well-used expat pubs accumulate over years. Champions League midweek nights here are reliable — a full Paddy Reilly's at 9pm CET on a big UCL evening is a good experience. Worth having on rotation alongside Oliver Twist.

## The James Joyce Pub — Limmatquai

The James Joyce sits on Limmatquai, the riverside street running along the Limmat near the old town — one of the more scenically placed sports pubs you'll find anywhere. Named after the man who wrote much of Ulysses while living in Zurich, it is a proper institution: genuine Joycean connections, a solid Guinness, and football on the screens. The riverside location makes it a pleasure to visit regardless of the fixture. Strong Champions League evenings, and the terrace in summer is worth the trip alone.

## Murphy's Law Irish Bar

Murphy's Law is a dependable presence on the Zurich sports bar circuit, particularly good when you want a different crowd from the Oliver Twist regulars. Lively on big match days, the kind of place where a stranger at the next table will argue with you about substitutions. Exactly what you want from a pub. Good for Champions League nights and Celtic fixtures, which Murphy's treats as major events.

## Hive Club — For Big Events

Not a traditional sports bar, but Hive Club puts on large-scale Champions League viewing events that are worth knowing about. For a major semi-final or the final itself, this is where the atmosphere gets genuinely large. Check their listings as the rounds progress — it's a different experience from a pub showing football and worth doing once.

## Zeughauskeller — City Centre

Full disclosure: Zeughauskeller is not a sports bar. It doesn't have screens and it doesn't show football. But if you're in Zurich and you haven't eaten in a historic Swiss beer hall — a former medieval armoury, now serving Züri-style food and local beer to a thousand covers — you're missing something. The atmosphere for a big communal occasion is excellent. Go here before or after the match, not during it.

## Practical Tips

**Timing:** CET (UTC+1) in winter means EPL at 1:30pm and 4pm Saturdays. CEST (UTC+2) in summer, add an hour. Champions League 9pm local kickoff is ideal — proper evening viewing. Zurich's timezone is genuinely one of the best in the world for following British sport.

**Cost:** Accept it. A round in Zurich will cost roughly double London prices. The venues are generally clean, well-maintained, and take sport seriously, which is partly what you're paying for. Pre-drinking at home before a session is a common local coping strategy — though the pubs won't thank you for saying so.

**UEFA connection:** When Champions League draws happen, Zurich is genuinely engaged in a way other cities aren't. If a Swiss team is in European competition, the city's sports bars fill in ways that go beyond the usual expat crowd.

**Niederdorf:** This is your base of operations. The old town bar district is compact, walkable, and contains Oliver Twist, Paddy Reilly's, and The James Joyce Pub within a short radius. Pick your venue; know your alternatives.

**Swiss Super League:** FC Zurich and Grasshopper play domestic Swiss football, which isn't the main event for most expats. But if you're here long-term, attending a live match is worth it for the experience rather than the level.
