Lisbon has one of the best sport-watching timezones in the world, and enough people have figured this out that the bar scene has responded accordingly. WET (UTC+0) in winter, WEST (UTC+1) in summer — meaning EPL kicks off at 12:30pm and 3pm on Saturdays, Champions League runs from 8pm, and even South American sport is accessible in the evening. The city has transformed in the last decade: a wave of digital nomads and expats has created enough demand for proper sports bars to sustain a real scene. Add the Portuguese football culture — Benfica and Sporting are not minor obsessions — and Lisbon punches well above its weight. Here's where to watch.

## O'Gilins Irish Pub, Rua dos Remolares

The most famous expat pub in Lisbon. O'Gilins has been doing this for decades and has become the reference point for British and Irish sport in the city. Multiple screens, a reliable atmosphere for Six Nations and EPL, and a crowd that has been coming back year after year because it delivers. It's not the most stylish venue in Lisbon, but that's entirely beside the point.

- The established go-to for British and Irish expats — reputation earned over years
- Six Nations and EPL are where it absolutely excels
- Champions League nights pack out — arrive early for a good seat
- Guinness on tap, obviously, and well-kept
- Price: $$
- Rating: 4.4/5

## The Irish & Co, Cais do Sodré

Cais do Sodré is the right neighbourhood for a night out in Lisbon — it's lively, walkable, and has the Pink Street strip nearby. The Irish & Co sits in this mix and benefits from the foot traffic. The sport coverage is serious: multiple screens, EPL on every Saturday, and a strong Champions League crowd. The mix of expats and locals gives it a better energy than a purely tourist-facing venue.

- Best location of any sports bar — Cais do Sodré is Lisbon's most accessible nightlife area
- Mixed crowd of expats and locals makes for genuine atmosphere
- EPL, Champions League, and Primeira Liga (Portuguese football) all covered
- Late licence means you're not being pushed out after the match
- Price: $$
- Rating: 4.3/5

## Hennessy's Irish Bar, Cais do Sodré

Another Cais do Sodré entry, and worth distinguishing from The Irish & Co. Hennessy's is slightly more focused on the sport-watching experience specifically — the screen placement is better, the sound is turned up when it matters, and the bar has a more dedicated following for big rugby and football matches. Regular Six Nations watch parties here are among the better ones in the city.

- Screen setup is the most deliberately sport-focused in the Cais do Sodré area
- Six Nations is the standout event — proper rugby atmosphere
- EPL follows closely, consistent attendance from regulars
- Doesn't try to be more than it is — that's a compliment
- Price: $$
- Rating: 4.3/5

## Tasca do Chico, Bairro Alto

Not a sports bar — but worth knowing about. Tasca do Chico is a small fado house, and on nights when Portugal plays a major match, the television goes on and the whole room watches. It's a reminder that sport and culture aren't separate things in Lisbon. If you're in Bairro Alto and Portugal is playing, wander in — the reaction of the room to a goal is something you won't forget.

- Fado venue that transforms for major Portugal international matches
- The crowd reaction here is as authentic as Lisbon gets
- Small, intimate, genuinely memorable for big Portugal games
- Not for routine league fixtures — this is for national team nights only
- Price: $$
- Rating: 4.5/5

## Megasport Bar, Intendente/Anjos

The Intendente and Anjos neighbourhoods have become the centre of Lisbon's long-stay expat community, and Megasport has evolved to serve it. This is more of a local sports bar than an expat pub — Primeira Liga is taken seriously here, and you'll find Portuguese football coverage that the Irish bars don't always prioritise. Good for watching Benfica and Sporting alongside EPL.

- Best place to watch Primeira Liga with a genuinely engaged crowd
- Multiple screens, practical setup, no pretension
- Strong for Champions League given Portuguese clubs' regular participation
- Expat community mixed with locals — different energy to the Cais do Sodré spots
- Price: $
- Rating: 4.2/5

## British Bar, Rua Bernardino Costa

One of Lisbon's oldest bars, and a genuine institution. The British Bar has an atmosphere that comes from decades of use — the fittings are old, the ceiling is low, and the screens fit into the space rather than dominating it. It's not a big-screen-and-surround-sound operation. What it is: a proper bar with history, a crowd that knows sport, and EPL on at the right times.

- Genuinely old Lisbon character — some venues claim this, British Bar actually has it
- EPL and rugby union are the primary sport draws
- The atmosphere on a quiet Saturday afternoon is excellent
- Better for afternoon football than late-night or finals occasions
- Price: $$
- Rating: 4.4/5

## Pé Sujo, Intendente

Pé Sujo translates roughly as "dirty foot" — it's that kind of place. An unpretentious neighbourhood bar that takes sport seriously, particularly football. The Champions League and Primeira Liga dominate here, and the enthusiasm is real. This is Lisbon without the tourist sheen, and for watching sport that's often exactly what you want.

- Best atmosphere for Champions League in the Intendente area
- Primeira Liga coverage is consistent and taken seriously
- Cheap beer, no frills, loud when the football's on
- Mixed crowd of locals and long-stay expats
- Price: $
- Rating: 4.2/5

## Practical Tips

**The timezone is the story here.** Lisbon is essentially the only major European city with a UTC/UTC+1 timezone, which means EPL Saturday football lands at 12:30pm and 3pm in winter, 1:30pm and 4pm in summer. Champions League is 8pm (winter) or 9pm (summer). This is the most comfortable European football-watching timezone in Europe — better than Central Europe, far better than anywhere east of there.

**South American sport is genuinely accessible.** Brazilian Serie A, Copa Libertadores, and Argentina's Liga Profesional all kick off in the evening Lisbon time. If you're a South American football fan, Lisbon is one of the best cities in Europe to follow it.

**Cais do Sodré vs Intendente.** For one-off visits, stay around Cais do Sodré — The Irish & Co and Hennessy's are well-run and easy to navigate. If you're in Lisbon longer term, the Intendente/Anjos venues have better long-stay expat communities and more authentic atmosphere for mid-week football.

**Portuguese football culture is worth engaging with.** Benfica vs Sporting is not a casual rivalry — it's an identity marker. If you're in Lisbon when a Clássico is on, find a bar that's showing it and pay attention to the room. Understanding this helps you understand Lisbon.

**Book for big nights.** Champions League knockout stages and major Portugal internationals fill the expat bars completely. Turn up 45 minutes early or call ahead.
