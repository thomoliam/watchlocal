Berlin is not a city that sleeps, and its sports bar scene reflects that. The German capital has two Bundesliga clubs — Hertha BSC and Union Berlin — which between them cover the full spectrum of German football culture, from old-school West Berlin identity to the cult working-class passion of Köpenick. Layered on top of that is a large English-speaking expat community (British, American, Australian, Irish) that has built a parallel ecosystem of dedicated sports bars running everything from Premier League Saturday afternoons to Super Bowl watch parties that stretch into Sunday morning.

If you're visiting Berlin and want to watch live sport, you will not struggle. What you need to know is which neighbourhood to head to and which bars are worth your time.

## The Best Areas for Sports Bars in Berlin

### Mitte

Mitte is Berlin's central district and where most visitors base themselves. It has several dedicated English-speaking sports bars — The Pint Berlin is the standout, with an enormous screen setup, comprehensive sports subscriptions, and a crowd that skews heavily British and Irish on Premier League weekends. Sports Bar Berlin on Friedrichstrasse is the other major venue in this area: multiple screens, extended opening hours, and a strong reputation for American sports coverage.

If you're new to Berlin and want a reliable, English-friendly experience with guaranteed Premier League or Champions League coverage, start in Mitte.

### Prenzlauer Berg

East of Mitte, Prenzlauer Berg is a neighbourhood of renovated Altbau apartments, craft beer bars, and an international crowd that landed in Berlin and never left. Sport watching here is less about dedicated sports bars and more about knowing which regular bars have good screens and a relaxed attitude about changing the channel.

It's a better neighbourhood if you want to watch Bundesliga with actual Berliners — the local connection to Hertha BSC is strong here, and der alte Fritz pub culture means football on the television is the default setting on a Saturday afternoon, not something you need to negotiate for.

### Kreuzberg

Kreuzberg is where the expat scene and the local Berlin scene genuinely overlap. The bars are grittier, the hours are longer, and the atmosphere on big European nights can be extraordinary. Several Kreuzberg bars have built strong reputations for NFL coverage — the 2:00am–5:00am Sunday morning window that American football demands suits Kreuzberg's natural rhythms perfectly. If you want to watch the Super Bowl in Berlin with a crowd that is genuinely into it, Kreuzberg is the place.

It's also the best area for an authentic Union Berlin atmosphere — the club has strong roots south of the city, but the Kreuzberg bar scene is where many fans end up before and after matches.

## Bundesliga in Berlin

Berlin's two-club Bundesliga dynamic is unusual in German football. Hertha BSC — historically the bigger club, currently fighting to reassert themselves in the top flight — draw their identity from West Berlin's cosmopolitan, internationalist culture. Union Berlin — Bundesliga's great recent success story, reaching European competition from a standing start less than a decade ago — are everything the opposite: community-owned in spirit, rooted in a specific corner of the former East, ferociously loyal.

On Bundesliga weekends, the city splits along those lines. Hertha games draw a broader, more casual crowd across the city's bars; Union games create a concentrated intensity in south-east Berlin that is genuinely one of German football's more special atmospheres.

Bundesliga fixtures run on Friday evenings, Saturday afternoons, Saturday evenings, and Sunday afternoons. In Berlin (UTC+2 in summer, UTC+1 in winter), these are all manageable domestic hours — most bars are well set up by the opening whistle.

## Champions League in Berlin

Germany's Champions League representatives vary year to year, but Berlin's sports bars take European nights seriously regardless of who is involved. The 18:45 CET early kickoff lands mid-evening; the 21:00 CET main game is prime time. Most venues in Mitte and Kreuzberg show both matches on separate screens.

When Bundesliga clubs — Bayern, Dortmund, Bayer Leverkusen — are in the knockout rounds, expect sports bars across Berlin to be significantly busier than usual. The UCL Final has become a major event in Berlin bars across recent years. Reserve ahead for any knockout fixture.

## Premier League in Berlin

Berlin's English expat community is large enough to sustain consistent Premier League demand across the weekend. The timezone works in your favour: Berlin is UTC+2 (CEST) in winter — actually UTC+1 (CET) when EPL is in full swing — which means:

- **12:30 UK** → **13:30 Berlin** — early afternoon, easy
- **15:00 UK** → **16:00 Berlin** — main Saturday slot, mid-afternoon
- **17:30 UK** → **18:30 Berlin** — Saturday evening, busy

Sunday EPL runs from 15:00–18:30 Berlin time. These are all comfortable hours for a city that barely gets started before midday on a weekend.

The Pint Berlin and Sports Bar Berlin are the most reliable venues for comprehensive EPL coverage, including the full Sky Sports and TNT Sports packages. For specific club support groups — Arsenal, Liverpool, Manchester United all have organised supporter bases in Berlin — check WatchLocal listings for which bar runs their match-day events.

## NFL and American Sports in Berlin

Germany now hosts two NFL regular season games per year, and Berlin has become one of the country's most NFL-literate cities as a result. The American expat community is concentrated in Mitte and Prenzlauer Berg; the dedicated sports bar scene has adapted accordingly.

NFL Sunday windows from Berlin (UTC+1):
- **1pm ET games** → **19:00 Berlin** — Sunday evening, easy
- **4pm ET games** → **22:00 Berlin** — late evening, still manageable
- **8:20pm ET Sunday Night Football** → **02:20 Berlin** — this is where Kreuzberg takes over
- **Super Bowl kickoff** (approximately 00:30–01:00 Berlin time) — multiple venues run all-night events

Several bars in Berlin are now open 24 hours on NFL weekends during the season. It is genuinely worth checking listings in advance for overnight events — they fill up with a committed crowd and the atmosphere is unlike a standard bar night.

The NBA also draws a consistent weeknight crowd in Berlin — tip-offs land at 19:00–21:00 Berlin time for most games, which is a perfect mid-evening slot.

## Six Nations in Berlin

The Six Nations (February–March) is well-watched in Berlin's English and Irish bars. England, Ireland, Scotland, and Wales games all draw expat crowds; France games pick up a broader European audience. The Saturday triple-headers work perfectly in the Berlin timezone — all three games run across the afternoon and into the early evening.

Ireland games in particular draw large crowds at Mitte's Irish-leaning bars. The Irish expat community in Berlin is active and organised — you'll find no shortage of company watching a Six Nations Ireland fixture.

## Tips for Watching Sport in Berlin

- **Book for NFL overnight events.** The best Super Bowl and Sunday Night Football watch parties fill quickly — particularly in Kreuzberg. Check WatchLocal listings and reserve your spot.
- **Bundesliga on Friday night is underrated.** The Friday evening Bundesliga game is one of Berlin's best sport-watching experiences — bars are lively, the football is quality, and it starts the weekend properly.
- **Mitte for guaranteed screens; Kreuzberg for atmosphere.** If reliability matters — you need to definitely see a specific game — go to Mitte. If you want an evening out built around sport, Kreuzberg's bars offer something more interesting.
- **Berlin bars stay open.** Unlike most European cities, Berlin bars do not have a strict closing time. This matters enormously for overnight American sports.
- **Union Berlin vs Hertha is a derby worth planning around.** Matches between the two Berlin clubs happen twice a season in the Bundesliga. The city gets noticeably more charged around these fixtures — find a bar in advance and settle in.
- **Google Maps hours are unreliable.** Berlin bars frequently open later or earlier depending on the sporting calendar. Follow the venues on social media or use WatchLocal for accurate match-day schedules.
