Bogotá doesn't get the credit it deserves in the expat sports bar conversation. The city has a vibrant, growing international community, a bar scene that has genuinely matured over the past five years, and an EPL viewing time that — while not European comfortable — is entirely manageable. 7:30am for the early game, 10am for the main Saturday fixture. Same as Eastern US time. You've had breakfast, you have coffee or a first beer in hand, and you're watching Premier League football in a city that is increasingly one of South America's genuinely great places to live.

The transformation is real. Bogotá has gone from a city that international visitors avoided to one they actively choose. The Zona Rosa, Parque 93, and Usaquén neighbourhoods are safe, sophisticated, and full of international arrivals — UK, American, European digital nomads and expats who have discovered that Bogotá offers a quality of life that more expensive cities can't match.

The local football context is exceptional. Millonarios vs Independiente Santa Fe — the Clásico Bogotano — is one of the most intensely felt rivalries in South American football. Copa Libertadores nights, when Colombian clubs are involved, generate an atmosphere that imported sport simply can't match. You're not here just to watch the Premier League; you're here to engage with a city that lives and breathes football on its own terms.

The altitude is real. Bogotá at 2,600 metres means your first few days should involve acclimatisation before you commit to a full session. Drink water. Eat well. Your body will thank you.

## Murphy's Irish Pub — Parque 93

Murphy's sits near Parque 93, a smarter and more restaurant-focused neighbourhood than Zona Rosa. It draws the Irish and British expat crowd reliably and shows all major sport. Parque 93 on a Sunday afternoon has a particular Bogotá pleasure to it — coffee shops and brunch spots ring the park, and Murphy's is a natural destination for the 10am EPL fixture followed by a long afternoon. Champions League midweek games at 2:45pm local time are ideal here. Solid Guinness management at altitude, which is harder than it sounds.

## Bogotá Beer Company — Multiple Locations

BBC is Bogotá's best craft beer chain and has become an institution. Multiple locations — Zona Rosa, Parque 93, Usaquén, and beyond — mean there is almost always one near you. The local craft beer is some of the best in Colombia, the food is genuinely good, and large screens showing sport are standard across the estate. For EPL it's a solid rather than specialist option, but the combination of quality beer, food, and screens makes it the best all-round choice for a group where football enthusiasm varies. The craft beer scene in Bogotá has exploded in the last five years and BBC sits at the centre of it.

## William's Bar — Usaquén

Usaquén is a different Bogotá — a colonial neighbourhood in the north of the city that transforms on Sundays into a market and a destination. William's Bar is the expat sports venue here, with a loyal local following and the relaxed Sunday afternoon atmosphere that Usaquén naturally generates. For a long Sunday watching EPL over a cold Águila or a BBC craft lager, it's one of the best venues in the city. Worth the Uber ride up from Zona Rosa.

## Green Hills Irish Bar — Zona Rosa

Green Hills rounds out the Irish pub options in Zona Rosa and provides a reliable alternative on big match days. The expat circuit in Bogotá rotates between venues depending on the match, the company, and the mood — having three solid Irish pubs in the city means you're never without options when Murphy's is heaving or The Irish Pub is showing the wrong game.

## The Irish Pub — Zona Rosa

The Irish Pub anchors the expat sports-watching scene in Zona Rosa and is the first recommendation for anyone newly arrived in the city. It shows EPL comprehensively, opens for morning fixtures, and has a crowd that reflects the international community that has established itself in the area — British, American, Australian expats alongside local professionals who follow European football. The staff are used to international sports enquiries and the atmosphere on a Premier League Saturday morning genuinely works.

## Local Fan Bars — Millonarios and Santa Fe

Don't spend your entire time in Bogotá in venues catering to the British expat community. The Clásico Bogotano — Millonarios (blue) versus Independiente Santa Fe (red) — is one of the most passionately felt derbies in South American football. Copa Libertadores nights when Colombian clubs are in contention generate an atmosphere that EPL crowds in an Irish pub simply cannot replicate. Find a bar where the clientele is split between the two sides and watch a Derby. Ask your hotel, ask at Bogotá Beer Company, ask a local contact. The recommendations you get from a Colombian football fan will be more authentic than anything written here.

## Practical Tips

**Timing:** COT (UTC-5) gives EPL at 7:30am and 10am on Saturdays — same as US Eastern Time. Champions League midweek kickoffs land around 2:45pm and 3pm local time, which is ideal afternoon viewing. No middle-of-the-night suffering required in Bogotá.

**Altitude:** 2,600 metres is serious. Beer hits differently — alcohol is more potent and dehydration accelerates. Drink water alongside every beer, especially in the first few days after arriving from sea level. Give yourself two or three days of lighter activity before committing to a full sports-watching session. The altitude headache on day one is not compatible with enjoying early EPL.

**Zona Rosa vs Parque 93 vs Usaquén:** These three neighbourhoods contain most of the good expat sports bars. Zona Rosa and Parque 93 are adjacent and easily accessible by Uber. Usaquén is worth the ride, especially on Sundays. The city centre and south of the city are not where the international sports bar scene lives.

**Águila:** Colombia's national lager is perfectly drinkable and very affordable. It's what everyone around you is drinking. Don't arrive expecting imported European lager — embrace the local option and save the Bogotá Beer Company craft selections for when you want to treat yourself.

**Copa Libertadores timing:** Libertadores group stages run from February to June; knockouts through October. If you're here during the latter rounds and a Colombian club is involved, clear your diary and find a local fan bar. It is one of the great live sports experiences in South America.

**Safety:** Bogotá's reputation has improved considerably and the areas described here — Zona Rosa, Parque 93, Usaquén — are safe for bar-going. Standard city common sense applies: avoid displaying expensive items unnecessarily, use registered taxis or Uber at night, and don't wander into unfamiliar areas alone after dark.
