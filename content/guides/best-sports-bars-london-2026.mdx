London is one of the best cities in the world to watch live sport. With Premier League clubs based all across the capital, football is embedded into every neighbourhood — and the rest of the sporting calendar isn't far behind. Whether you're visiting, living abroad, or just trying to catch a game you can't watch at home, London's sports bar scene has you covered.

## The Best Areas for Sports Bars in London

### Soho and the West End

The West End has the highest concentration of dedicated sports bars in London, catering to the city's huge tourist trade and lunchtime office crowd. Most open from 11am and show every Premier League and Champions League fixture. Expect large screens, lively atmosphere, and prices that reflect the location. Book ahead for any match involving the top six clubs.

### Shoreditch and East London

East London has become one of the best areas for sport watching over the last decade. Shoreditch combines craft beer pubs with serious screen setups — many venues run multiple screens showing different games simultaneously, which is ideal on busy Premier League weekends. The crowd tends to be younger and the atmosphere louder. Strong coverage of American sports (NFL, NBA) here too.

### Clapham and Brixton

South of the river, Clapham and Brixton are home to a large expat community, particularly Australians and New Zealanders. This means excellent coverage of AFL, NRL, rugby union, and cricket — often at more reasonable viewing hours than you'd find in central London. A Saturday afternoon in Clapham during AFL finals season has a unique energy.

### The City and Canary Wharf

For midweek sport, the City and Canary Wharf are hard to beat. Large bar-restaurants across both areas fill up on Tuesday and Wednesday evenings for Champions League nights. Most venues show multiple games across different screens — essential when the knockout rounds have two matches running simultaneously.

### Paddington and Bayswater

Near the stations, you'll find a strong Australian expat scene with dedicated Aussie sports bars. These venues are reliably open early for overnight fixtures, making them the go-to choice for anyone needing to watch an AFL or NRL game that kicks off in the middle of the London night.

## Premier League in London

The Premier League season runs from August to May. Weekend fixtures in London:

- **12:30 GMT** — Early Saturday game, surprisingly good for the atmosphere
- **15:00 GMT** — Main Saturday slot (UK blackout applies, but sports bars with Sky/TNT are exempt)
- **17:30 GMT** — Late Saturday, often the biggest fixture of the day
- **14:00 / 16:30 GMT** — Sunday Premier League

London clubs (Arsenal, Chelsea, Spurs, West Ham, Crystal Palace, Brentwood, Fulham) have devoted fanbases across the city. Match days involving local derbies — particularly the North London Derby or Chelsea vs Arsenal — fill sports bars hours in advance. Arrive early or book a table.

## Champions League in London

Tuesday and Wednesday evenings belong to the Champions League. The atmosphere in London sports bars is electric, especially in the knockout rounds when English clubs are involved. Most venues show the main game on all screens from around 7:45pm UK time.

Group stage matches can run simultaneously — a good sports bar will have multiple screens showing different games, or let you request which fixture to show. For semi-finals and the final, venues get booked out days in advance.

## Rugby Union and Rugby League

London has strong coverage of Premiership Rugby, Six Nations, and international Test matches. The Six Nations (February–March) is particularly well-watched in London pubs. For rugby league fans, look for bars with Sky Sports that cover Super League and NRL.

The NFL London Games at Wembley and Tottenham Hotspur Stadium have grown the American football fanbase considerably — most sports bars now show all NFL Sunday games.

## Tips for Watching Sport in London

- **Check screen count and size** before committing to a venue. A 42-inch TV in the corner is fine for a quiet watch; for big games you want 6+ large screens.
- **Book ahead for big fixtures.** Most sports bars take reservations for large groups. Walk-ins are harder to accommodate on derby days or international match days.
- **Arrive 30 minutes early.** Venues fill fast for the opening kickoff, even in less-central areas.
- **Midweek is more relaxed.** Tuesday and Wednesday Champions League nights attract a committed crowd rather than a casual one — better atmosphere, easier to find a seat if you arrive at 7pm.
- **Ask about audio.** Some pubs show games muted during quieter periods. If atmosphere matters, confirm the sound will be on before you settle in.
