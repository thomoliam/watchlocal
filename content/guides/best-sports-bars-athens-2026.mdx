Athens is a city of contradictions for the sports-watching traveller. The timezone is brilliant — Premier League at 2:30pm on a Saturday with a cold Mythos in hand is genuinely one of life's pleasures. But Athens is primarily a tourist city rather than an expat hub, and that means the bar scene is uneven. Wander into a place near the Acropolis advertising "football shown here" and you might end up watching a screen the size of a laptop with a Greek commentary nobody can hear. Do your homework and you'll have a great time.

The core expat crowd here is British and Irish, supplemented by a rotating cast of digital nomads and long-stay tourists. The Greeks themselves are fanatical about football — but they're fanatical about *their* football. When Olympiakos and Panathinaikos play, the city has an edge to it. When AEK join the equation, forget it. That passion is contagious if you lean into it.

## James Joyce Irish Pub — Monastiraki

The most reliable sports bar in central Athens. Multiple large screens, a committed Irish-pub atmosphere, and a regular crowd that shows up specifically to watch football rather than to Instagram the Acropolis. EPL Saturdays here feel like a proper British pub transplant. It gets busy, so arrive early for the first fixtures. The food is standard Irish-pub fare, the beer is cold, and nobody will try to seat you in front of a pillar.

## The Highlander Scottish Pub — Psyrri

A short walk from Monastiraki into the slightly grittier Psyrri neighbourhood, The Highlander is the choice for when you want something with a bit more local character. Scottish memorabilia everywhere, a decent whisky selection, and strong Champions League showings. The crowd skews slightly older expat, which suits a midweek CL night perfectly. If there's a Scotland match on, this is where every Scot in Athens will be.

## Shamrock Irish Pub — Syntagma

Conveniently located near Syntagma Square for those staying in the central tourist belt. The Shamrock does what it says on the tin — it's an Irish pub that shows sport. Screens are adequate rather than impressive, but for an early kickoff when you don't want to trek to Monastiraki, it does the job. Can get very touristy on weekend evenings so arrive early or come for the midweek games when the atmosphere is more local.

## Mike's Irish Bar — Monastiraki

Mike's has been around long enough to have earned its reputation. It sits close to James Joyce, which makes Monastiraki a genuine sports bar cluster in the city centre. Mike's tends to be slightly less crowded, which can be an advantage for the big games when James Joyce is rammed. Screens are solid, the Guinness pours properly, and the staff know their football.

## The Old Spice — Kolonaki

Kolonaki is the upmarket neighbourhood where Athens' wealthier expats and well-heeled Athenians live and drink. The Old Spice fits the area — a smarter venue with better food than the Irish pubs and a crowd that mixes international workers with local professionals. It shows all the major sport without being exclusively a sports bar. Good choice if you want to watch a game without sitting in a shrine to the Irish national team.

## Athens Sports Bar — Glyfada

If you're staying on the southern coast — Glyfada, Voula, or the wider Athenian Riviera — Athens Sports Bar is your venue. It's a proper sports-focused operation with multiple screens and a strong local expat following. The beach suburb setting means it's particularly good in summer when you want to watch an evening Champions League fixture with the sea breeze coming through. The commute from central Athens is worthwhile if you're in the area.

## Practical Tips

**Timing:** EET (UTC+2) in winter means EPL at 2:30pm and 5pm Saturdays, CL at 10pm. In summer EEST (UTC+3), add an hour. The CL late kickoffs are standard for Mediterranean life — locals treat 11pm as primetime, not an inconvenience.

**Greek football:** If you're here for any significant Olympiakos, Panathinaikos, or AEK Athens match, go and experience it. The Super League isn't world-class football but the atmosphere and local passion is extraordinary. Ask at your bar about upcoming fixtures.

**Avoid the tourist traps:** Any bar advertising "all sports, all channels" on a chalkboard near the Acropolis or Plaka is almost certainly not worth your time. Stick to the venues above.

**Getting around:** Monastiraki and Psyrri are walkable from most central accommodation. Kolonaki is a short Metro or taxi ride. Glyfada is a tram ride down the coast — Line 3 runs the whole way.

**Beer:** Greek lager (Mythos, Alfa) is perfectly drinkable and cheap. The Irish pubs charge more for Guinness but it's usually kept well.
