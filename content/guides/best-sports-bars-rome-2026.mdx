Rome is not a city built for rushing. But when the Derby della Capitale is on — AS Roma vs Lazio, two clubs separated by politics, neighbourhood and decades of mutual contempt — the city holds its breath. This is one of the most charged fixtures in world football, and watching it in Rome is an experience you don't forget.

Beyond the derby, Rome has a solid sports bar scene for expats and visitors. The main concentrations are in Trastevere, Testaccio, and Campo de' Fiori, with more tourist-oriented options up in Prati near the Vatican. The timezone works in your favour: UTC+2 means Premier League Saturday afternoons kick off at 4pm local, and Champions League nights run from 9pm — both perfectly civilised.

## The Drunken Ship

The most established English-language sports bar in Rome, right on Campo de' Fiori. Multiple screens cover the major leagues, and the expat crowd here knows their football. It can get loud and packed for big fixtures — arrive early for Champions League nights. American sports also feature heavily.

- **Best for:** Premier League, Champions League, American sports
- **Neighbourhood:** Campo de' Fiori
- **Price range:** $$
- **Rating:** 4.3/5
- **Tip:** Book ahead or arrive 45 minutes early for Champions League knockout rounds

## Scholars Lounge Irish Pub

One of Rome's best-known Irish pubs, Scholars Lounge delivers the full match-day experience: multiple screens, decent draught, and a crowd that actually cares what's happening on screen. Six Nations rugby gets proper treatment here — Azzurri games bring in a good Italian contingent alongside the expats.

- **Best for:** Six Nations, Premier League, rugby union
- **Neighbourhood:** Near the Pantheon
- **Price range:** $$
- **Rating:** 4.2/5
- **Tip:** Six Nations Saturdays get extremely busy — this is one of the better rugby venues in the city

## Big Hops

Craft beer bar with an honest sports setup — good screens, rotating taps, and a more relaxed atmosphere than the Campo de' Fiori strip. It draws a younger, international crowd and shows a broad range of leagues. Better for a midweek game than a raucous Saturday afternoon.

- **Best for:** Champions League midweek, Premier League, relaxed viewing
- **Neighbourhood:** Central Rome
- **Price range:** $$
- **Rating:** 4.4/5
- **Tip:** The beer selection is genuinely good — treat it as a proper craft bar that happens to show sport

## Testaccio Neighbourhood Bars

Testaccio is old Rome — working-class, fiercely giallorossi, and not built for tourists. The bars around the Mercato Testaccio area are where Roma supporters actually watch their team. You won't find an English menu, but you will find an authentic match-day atmosphere that the polished expat bars can't replicate.

- **Best for:** Serie A, Roma home fixtures, Derby della Capitale
- **Neighbourhood:** Testaccio
- **Price range:** $
- **Rating:** 4.1/5
- **Tip:** Know that you're in Roma territory — Lazio colours are not welcome

## Prati Sports Bars

Prati, the neighbourhood just north of the Vatican, has the most tourist-friendly concentration of sports bars in Rome. Useful if you're staying near St Peter's, though the atmosphere is thinner than Testaccio or Campo de' Fiori. Several bars here advertise live sport prominently and are set up for an international crowd.

- **Best for:** Casual viewing, visitors unfamiliar with the city
- **Neighbourhood:** Prati
- **Price range:** $$
- **Rating:** 4.0/5
- **Tip:** Good fallback option — works for any match but lacks the edge of the more local venues

## Yellow Bar

Part of the Yellow hostel complex and one of the more reliably lively spots for international sport in Rome. A younger, heavily international crowd, good screen coverage, and it stays open late. Not the most atmospheric but consistently busy for big fixtures.

- **Best for:** All major European leagues, late-night sport
- **Neighbourhood:** Termini area
- **Price range:** $/$$
- **Rating:** 4.1/5
- **Tip:** Better suited to evening fixtures — goes properly loud for knockout Champions League ties

## Practical Tips for Watching Sport in Rome

**Timezone:** Rome is UTC+2 (CET/CEST). Premier League 3pm Saturday kick-offs are 4pm local. Champions League 8pm UK = 9pm Rome. Serie A fixtures are spread across the weekend, with Sunday evening the traditional prime slot.

**The Derby della Capitale:** Roma vs Lazio is the fixture that divides the city. It's played at the Stadio Olimpico, which both clubs share — adding an extra layer of weirdness to the rivalry. On derby weekends, decide in advance which end of the bar you belong on.

**Getting in:** Major fixtures draw genuine crowds at the expat bars. For Champions League knockout stages and Serie A title run-ins, arrive 30-45 minutes early at The Drunken Ship or Scholars Lounge.

**Language:** At expat bars, English is standard. At Testaccio and local neighbourhood spots, Italian is expected. A basic working knowledge helps — and knowing the Roma lineup earns immediate respect.

**Eating:** Most Roman sports bars serve bar snacks at best. Have dinner before the game or plan around it — Rome's restaurants don't care about kick-off times.
