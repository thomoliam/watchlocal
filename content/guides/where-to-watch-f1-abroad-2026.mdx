Formula 1 is the most global sport on the calendar — 24 races across six continents, from the neon streets of Singapore to the mountain backdrop of Montreal. That reach is what makes it brilliant. It's also what makes watching it live a timezone lottery.

A race in Bahrain or Abu Dhabi is perfect Sunday evening viewing in Central Europe. Singapore is the dream for Asian fans — 8pm local, city buzzing, half the bars in town with screens up. The Australian Grand Prix, on the other hand, kicks off at 3pm local on a Sunday afternoon, which sounds ideal until you realise that's 5am in the UK and 1am on the US East Coast.

If you're an expat or a traveller who refuses to miss a race, here's what you need to know by city.

## Singapore

Singapore might be the single best city in the world to be an F1 fan. The Singapore Grand Prix runs under floodlights on a street circuit that wraps around Marina Bay — and even when it's not race weekend, the city takes F1 seriously. Bars along Clarke Quay, Boat Quay and the CBD stay open late for European races (which kick off around midnight to 2am local time) and the Asian race calendar is perfectly timed for after-work viewing.

The home race weekend in September is electric. Book ahead — everywhere fills up.

## Melbourne

Melbourne hosts the season opener every March and the city genuinely embraces it. Albert Park is the venue, and the race itself runs mid-afternoon local time — convenient for anyone in Australia or New Zealand. For Europeans planning a visit, the race is live at 5am UK / 6am Central Europe, which means early alarms but dedicated sports bars will open for it.

Outside the home race, Melbourne is a strong F1 city. The culture of watching sport in pubs is deeply embedded, and the Sunday morning slot for European and Middle Eastern races (broadcast around 9–11am local) is ideal brunch-and-F1 territory.

## Bangkok and Southeast Asia

Bangkok sits at UTC+7, which places it in a sweet spot for the bulk of the F1 calendar. Asian-timezone races — Singapore, Japan, China, Bahrain — all kick off between 7pm and 9pm local time. European races (Silverstone, Monza, Monaco) start around midnight, which for dedicated fans means late-night venues that are worth tracking down.

The Bangkok expat bar scene is large and well-catered. Sukhumvit Road has dozens of venues with sports packages, and UFC and F1 co-exist comfortably on the same screens.

## London and the UK

The UK is the spiritual home of F1 — Silverstone has been on the calendar for over 70 years, and Lewis Hamilton's era turned the sport into mainstream prime-time television. Most races fall in the early-to-mid afternoon in UK time, which means sports pubs do serious F1 trade on Sunday lunchtimes.

Monaco (2pm UK), Spain (2pm UK), Italy (2pm UK), the home race at Silverstone (3pm UK) — the European swing is perfect. The outlier is Australia (5am UK), which the hardcores treat like a Champions League final: alarm set, bar reserved.

## Dubai

Dubai's UTC+4 position makes it a solid F1 timezone. Bahrain and Abu Dhabi — both within the region — run at sensible evening hours. European races kick off around 5–6pm, ideal for after-work viewing. The Abu Dhabi Grand Prix at Yas Marina Circuit is a short hop away and a genuine F1 pilgrimage if you're based in the region.

Sports bars in Dubai Marina and Downtown are well set up for F1 weekends.

## Tokyo

Japan hosts one of the best races on the calendar at Suzuka — fast, technical, and with a fanbase that borders on religious devotion. The home race (October) runs at 2pm local, perfect. European races kick off around 8–10pm JST, meaning Sunday evening in Tokyo works for the bulk of the calendar.

Sports bars in Roppongi and Shibuya carry Sky Sports and motorsport channels. F1 has a strong following among the Tokyo expat crowd.

## New York

The United States Grand Prix in Austin and the Miami and Las Vegas races have supercharged American F1 interest, but the timezone is brutal for anyone based on the East Coast trying to follow European rounds. Monza at 2pm Italian time is 8am in New York — doable, actually. But Bahrain and Abu Dhabi push into the 9–10am Sunday slot, which works fine for brunch viewing.

NYC has a growing F1 bar scene. The sport's US fanbase exploded after Drive to Survive and bars have followed the audience.

## The Golden Rule

Before any race weekend, check two things: the local time of the race start, and whether your city has a dedicated sports bar culture around F1. In Singapore, Tokyo and London, the sport is embedded enough that venues plan around it. In cities where F1 is more niche, a quick search on WatchLocal will show you which bars are reliably showing it — and whether they're opening early or staying late to do so.

The worst thing in F1 is finding a bar that says it shows motorsport, only to discover they've switched to golf halfway through lap 40. Find a proper F1 venue and the timezone problem becomes half the fun.
