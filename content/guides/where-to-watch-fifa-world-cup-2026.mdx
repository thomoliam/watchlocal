The FIFA World Cup 2026 is unlike anything that has come before it. For the first time in the tournament's history, 48 teams will compete across 16 host cities spread across three countries — the United States, Canada, and Mexico. The group stage alone will feature 104 matches. If you are travelling or living abroad between June 11 and July 19, here is everything you need to know about finding a great place to watch.

## Why 2026 is Different

Two things set this tournament apart from every previous World Cup.

The first is scale. With 48 teams instead of 32, every confederation gets more slots. Europe, South America, Africa, Asia, and North America all send more sides, which means the stakes are higher and more fans around the world have a team in the tournament.

The second is timezone. The host cities span three time zones — ET (New York, Miami, Boston), CT (Dallas, Kansas City, Los Angeles is PT), and MT (Mexico City). The majority of group stage games kick off at **3pm ET, 6pm ET, or 9pm ET**. This is excellent news for European viewers, workable for the Middle East, mixed for Southeast Asia, and challenging for Australia.

## How the North American Timezone Affects the World

### Europe (UK, France, Germany, Spain)

North American kickoff times translate well for European audiences. The 3pm ET slot is 8pm BST/9pm CEST — prime time for a midweek or evening match. The 6pm ET slot lands at 11pm BST/midnight CEST, late but manageable. The 9pm ET slot runs until 2am BST — tough on a work night, perfect for weekend games or when your own country is playing.

European fans face no trade-off: if you want to watch every game, you can do so from the sofa or your local sports bar at civilised hours.

### Middle East (Dubai, Riyadh, Doha)

Dubai sits at UTC+4. The 3pm ET kickoff = midnight Dubai, the 6pm ET kickoff = 3am, the 9pm ET kickoff = 6am. The late ET slot is brutal, but the early and mid slots fall at midnight and 3am — which is within the operating hours of Dubai's 24-hour sports bars. Big games draw crowds regardless of the hour.

### Southeast Asia (Bangkok, Singapore, Kuala Lumpur)

Thailand and Vietnam sit at UTC+7; Singapore and Malaysia at UTC+8. The 3pm ET slot = 2am or 3am local. The 6pm ET slot = 5am or 6am. The 9pm ET slot = 8am or 9am — early morning, but viable.

The counter-intuitive truth about SE Asia and this World Cup: the **9pm ET games are the most watchable**, landing in the morning. Sports bars in Bangkok and Singapore have already announced early-opening programmes for the tournament's big fixtures.

### Australia (Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane)

Australia is UTC+10 in June/July. The 3pm ET kickoff = 5am. The 6pm ET kickoff = 8am. The 9pm ET kickoff = 11am — the only slot landing at a genuinely comfortable time. If the Socceroos qualify, expect bars to open early for Australia games regardless of kickoff time. This tournament is the hardest of any recent World Cup for Australian fans in terms of scheduling.

### East Asia (Tokyo, Seoul, Hong Kong)

Japan and Korea sit at UTC+9. The 3pm ET slot = 4am; the 6pm ET slot = 7am; the 9pm ET slot = 10am. The late ET slot is again the most workable. Sports bars in Tokyo and Seoul are well-equipped for all-night events, and the Korea/Japan 2002 nostalgia will drive audiences even for non-Asian games.

## Best Cities for Watching the World Cup

### 1. London

London is the undisputed capital of World Cup viewing outside the host cities. Every single game is shown somewhere. By the quarter-finals, major sports bars will be selling tickets for big screens. Soho, Shoreditch, and the City fill up on match days, and for England games, the atmosphere rivals anything inside a stadium. Even if England go out early, London pubs keep the party going for every game until the final.

### 2. Bangkok

Bangkok's sports bar scene — particularly along Sukhumvit Road — runs 24 hours during major tournaments. The city's huge British, Australian, and European expat population makes it electric for knockout games. The 8am–9am local time kickoffs for the late ET slot are a Bangkok strength: the biggest bars open early and serve full breakfasts alongside the football. This is one of the few places on Earth where watching the World Cup at 8am feels completely normal.

### 3. Barcelona

When Spain play, Barcelona becomes one of the most extraordinary places in the world to watch football. The city's bars are already intense on any given matchday; during a World Cup knockout, every screen in the neighbourhood is on and strangers are hugging in the street. Even if you are not Spanish, the atmosphere is infectious. If Spain make a deep run — as they are expected to — Barcelona in June and July will be unmissable.

### 4. Singapore

Singapore's sports bar scene is professional, well-equipped, and globally connected. The expat population is diverse — British, Australian, South African, and American fans all congregate here — which means the interest spans every game, not just the European fixtures. Clarke Quay and the CBD have the best concentrations of large-screen venues. The 8am–9am kickoff times for late ET games are easy for Singapore's early-rising business crowd.

### 5. Dublin

Ireland may not have qualified yet as of publication, but if they do — Dublin will be one of the most electric cities in the world for a World Cup. Even without Ireland in the tournament, Irish pubs show every game with a level of enthusiasm that most cities reserve for their own national team. The city's pub culture and early kickoff times (the 3pm ET slot is 8pm in Dublin) make it near-perfect for group stage viewing.

### 6. Dubai

Dubai's sports bars are technically excellent — large screens, high-quality feeds, efficient service — and many operate around the clock during tournaments. The late local kickoff times (midnight, 3am) are offset by the city's hospitality culture, which means bars are licenced and staffed for exactly this scenario. The international crowd makes every game feel relevant.

### 7. Sydney and Melbourne

Australia has qualified for every recent World Cup, and its fanbase is passionate. The 11am kickoff for the late ET slot is the sweet spot — and even 5am and 8am starts draw crowds of committed Socceroos fans. Sydney's CBD and Melbourne's Fitzroy have sports bars that have shown willingness to open at any hour for Australia games. For the rest of the tournament, expect a quieter but engaged audience for big games.

### 8. Tokyo

Japan is a consistent World Cup qualifier and the country's following for international football is significant. Tokyo's sports bars in Roppongi, Shibuya, and Shinjuku screen every game. ## Tips for Finding the Right Venue

**Ask directly about coverage.** Not every sports bar holds a broadcast licence for the World Cup. Call ahead and ask: "Do you have the rights to show all World Cup games?" A venue that pauses before answering probably doesn't.

**Book ahead for knockout games.** From the round of 16 onwards, large-screen venues sell out. The semi-finals and final in particular require reservations weeks in advance in cities like London and Singapore.

**Giant screen vs. pub atmosphere.** There are two experiences on offer. A giant outdoor screen or a dedicated sports venue gives you spectacle and scale. A traditional pub gives you community, singing, and the sense that you're watching with people who care deeply. Decide which you prefer before the tournament starts.

**Check the commentary language.** In cities like Bangkok and Singapore, local-language broadcasts run alongside English ones. If commentary matters to you, confirm the venue runs the English feed.

**Use WatchLocal.** Venues listed on WatchLocal are verified for broadcast coverage. Filter by "FIFA World Cup" in your city to see confirmed options — especially useful for less obvious cities where coverage varies wildly.

The World Cup comes around every four years. In 2026, the 48-team format means it lasts five weeks and spans a time zone spread that makes it genuinely global. Wherever you are, there is a venue worth going to — and finding it in advance is the difference between a great memory and a pixelated stream on your phone.
